# Code of Conduct

## Our standard

AIDD Commons welcomes participation across disciplines, institutions, career stages, countries, and lived experience. We expect contributors to be precise, curious, respectful, and willing to revise a claim when evidence changes.

Acceptable conduct includes:

- criticizing methods and evidence without attacking people;
- stating uncertainty and conflicts of interest;
- giving specific, actionable review;
- respecting names, pronouns, accessibility needs, and cultural differences;
- crediting prior work and correcting attribution errors;
- protecting confidential reports and restricted information.

Unacceptable conduct includes harassment, threats, discriminatory language, sexualized attention, doxxing, sustained bad-faith disruption, retaliation, plagiarism, fabrication, deliberate benchmark manipulation, or publication of material that maintainers have asked to hold for a private safety review.

## Scope and enforcement

This code applies in project repositories, discussions, events, and spaces where a person represents AIDD Commons. Report conduct concerns privately to the project stewards through the repository's private security-reporting channel. Do not include unnecessary personal or sensitive information.

Stewards will acknowledge, assess, and document reports proportionately. Possible responses include clarification, a private warning, content removal, temporary participation limits, or a permanent ban. Anyone involved in a report must recuse from deciding it. Retaliation against a reporter or witness is itself a violation.

Public scientific disagreement is not misconduct merely because it is firm. Enforcement focuses on behavior, evidence integrity, safety, and sustained effects on participation.
