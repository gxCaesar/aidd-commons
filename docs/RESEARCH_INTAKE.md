# Research Intake and Landscape Decision

Decision date: 2026-09-04

## Data feasibility: feasible for v0.1

The initial release can be built from public, linkable materials without restricted clinical or proprietary discovery data. The website, registry, curriculum, and report use public metadata and primary-source evidence. The first executable benchmark uses the versioned FreeSolv v0.52 dataset. Model demonstrations that require large weights or GPUs are not part of the local v0.1 execution contract.

This judgment does not mean every AIDD task has adequate open training data. Each later lab must establish that its required inputs, labels, licences, entities, and independent units coexist before implementation.

## Scoop verdict: ADJACENT

Several established projects cover important parts of the proposed system:

- [TeachOpenCADD](https://github.com/volkamerlab/teachopencadd) provides open, notebook-based computer-aided drug design teaching material.
- [DeepChem tutorials](https://deepchem.io/tutorials/) teach machine learning for molecular and biological problems alongside an open software library.
- [Therapeutics Data Commons](https://tdcommons.ai/start/) organizes AI-ready therapeutic tasks, datasets, evaluators, and leaderboards.
- [AI in Drug Design](https://aidd.ochem.eu/) provides a structured European training program.
- [Open 3D Drug Design](https://o3dc.org/) focuses on open education for structure-based drug design.
- [Marin](https://marin.community/) demonstrates an open, community-developed learning-and-research environment for foundation models.
- [OpenAthena's open-development essay](https://openathena.ai/blog/open-development-of-frontier-ai/) articulates why frontier-model development should expose more than an endpoint.

These projects make the general idea of open AIDD education non-novel. The current review did not establish an identical artifact that combines an end-to-end graduate curriculum, a claim-evidenced and multidimensional openness atlas, protocol-frozen executable labs, and a living open-development report under one contribution system. That difference is a working delta, not a proven exclusivity claim. A dated comparison audit must settle it before stronger public language is used.

## Venue fit: community infrastructure first

The current deliverable is an open education and research-infrastructure project with a living technical report. It is not yet framed as a benchmark, SOTA-method paper, or formal systematic review. A later paper requires a separate venue-fit decision based on observed adoption, coverage, reproducibility outcomes, and a defensible scholarly contribution.

## Falsifiers and cheapest settling checks

The project should be reframed if any of the following is established:

1. An actively maintained open project already provides the same four connected artifacts at comparable AIDD scope and evidence depth.
2. The registry cannot sustain claim-level evidence and licence updates at the planned scale.
3. The curriculum breadth produces shallow pages that do not meet the chapter contract.
4. The labs cannot reproduce their frozen protocols on the stated CPU-first environment.

The cheapest settling checks are a versioned feature-by-topic comparison table, schema-validation statistics, chapter-contract coverage reports, and clean-room lab executions. Popularity, repository stars, and raw link counts do not settle these questions.
