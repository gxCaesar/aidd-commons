# Researcher decision task sheet

This session is for readers who already work in AI drug discovery. It checks
whether the teaching material supports a decision, not whether you are right.
Nothing you write here is marked right or wrong. You may skip any part or stop
at any point.

Do not provide names, account details, credentials or private data.

## What you are being asked

Write a short decision memo before you read any worked answer on the lessons.
Use the site as you normally would. Say when you open a worked answer or
receive help, and keep your first explanation before you revise it.

In the memo, mark each statement as something you observed in the material or
something you are assuming.

## What will be recorded

For every task the organizer records what you did, the kind of help you needed
and the time you spent. Three kinds of help are recorded separately.

- A page pointer. Someone told you where to look and you read it yourself.
- A concept explained. Someone explained the reasoning to you.
- The answer. Someone gave you the answer.

The record is not scored, and it is not used to judge you.

## Task 2. Write the decision memo

An invented follow-up reports a relative reporter signal of 63 and a
viable-cell count of 70, each normalized to a vehicle value of 100.

Under the lesson's linear model, calculate the relative per-cell signal. A
second detection method then shows no change, but its ability to detect the
proposed biological effect has not been established.

Does this settle interference versus biology? Write the next useful check and
the result that would change your interpretation.

For the real ChEMBL case, explain why its aggregate metrics alone cannot
identify the cause of transfer failure.

Do not fit a new model and do not rerun an evaluation.
