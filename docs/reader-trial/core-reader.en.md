# Core reader task sheet

This session checks our instructions, not your ability. Nothing you write here
is marked right or wrong. You may skip any task or stop at any point, and you
do not have to finish the sheet.

An organizer will supply a working site address or an agreed local source copy.
Paths below are relative to that site. `127.0.0.1` on your device is not a
shared site.

Do not provide names, email addresses, employer details, credentials, private
datasets or screenshots that contain personal information.

Allow about 30 minutes. If the optional engineering task does not install, the
organizer stops it after 15 minutes. That is a timebox for the session, not a
performance threshold.

## What you are being asked

Use the site as you normally would. For each task, record what you did, whether
you needed help and of what kind, and how long you spent. Say when you open a
worked answer or receive help, and keep your first explanation before you
revise it.

## What will be recorded

For every task the organizer records what you did, the kind of help you needed
and the time you spent. Three kinds of help are recorded separately, because
they mean different things.

- A page pointer. Someone told you where to look and you read it yourself.
- A concept explained. Someone explained the reasoning to you.
- The answer. Someone gave you the answer.

Your first explanation is recorded before any revision. The record is not
scored, and it is not used to judge you.

## Where to start

Open `/learn/foundations/first-session/`.

## Task 1. Find your route

Choose Foundations, Research or Engineering and say why it fits your
background. Find the representation lesson and its resource list. Open an Atlas
entry and its original resource.

Does a visible link establish permission to use every associated asset? Point
to the access or licence information that applies, and name any field the
source leaves unresolved.

## Task 2. Interpret a label-free example

Find the inspector's pyridine, alanine and sodium-acetate examples.

Explain whether different strings must mean different molecules, which output
loses the alanine stereochemical distinction, and whether the salt becomes one
neutral molecule. Name the output fields your answer relies on.

Reading in the browser is enough for this task.

## Task 3. Read a completed comparison

Open the completed ChEMBL lab. Compare Morgan Ridge with descriptor Ridge on
both evaluation sets.

Explain whether the same representation wins in both, and what the earlier T15
label access prevents the authors from claiming. Do not run another evaluation.

## Task 4. Find the next useful lesson

Return to the curriculum map at `/learn/foundations/curriculum-map/` and choose
a topic you would study next.

Identify one concrete explanation, example or prerequisite that you could not
find. Distinguish a missing resource link from a resource that exists but does
not teach the needed concept.

## Optional engineering task

Follow the first-session instructions in a fresh local source copy and run
only the label-free inspector. Find its output and compare it with the
documented relationships.

Record setup and interpretation separately. Do not download ChEMBL or rMD17
data, run pretests, change a protocol or invoke a locked evaluation.
