## The question this lab asks

A genetic perturbation leaves a cellular state behind, and two instruments can record
that state at the same time. The L1000 readout records 978 landmark transcript probe
sets. The Cell Painting readout records 291 morphological features. Both were applied
to the same A549 lung adenocarcinoma cells carrying the same engineered genetic
perturbations, and both were read 96 hours after the perturbation. Joining the two
readouts makes one question measurable. How strongly do they agree for the same
perturbation, and how much of that agreement survives when the plate and batch
structure of the measurement is accounted for?

The lab answers that question and stops there. It nominates no target, predicts no
phenotype and offers no model for use. What it settles is narrower and still useful.
It separates the part of an agreement between two readouts that is a property of the
perturbation from the part that is a property of the experiment the perturbation was
measured in.

The second part is easy to miss, because a design-blind analysis cannot see it. A map
that predicts one perturbation's imaging profile from its transcriptomic profile can be
right for a reason that has nothing to do with biology, namely that the training side
already contained the same plates and the same plate maps as the held-out perturbation.
This lab measures that possibility instead of assuming it away.

## The data and its provenance

Both objects come from one study of the Broad Cell Painting Gallery, `cpg0003-rosetta`,
and one batch of it, `LUAD-BBBC041-Caicedo`. The transcriptomic object is
`L1000/replicate_level_l1k.csv.gz` at 11,448,797 bytes. The imaging object is
`CellPainting/replicate_level_cp_normalized_variable_selected.csv.gz` at 15,858,778
bytes. Together 27,307,675 bytes. Each byte length was read from the bucket's own
object listing on 2026-10-21 and is re-verified on every download and every load.

| Readout | Wells | Treated wells | Control wells | Perturbations | Plates |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Transcriptome | 4,232 | 4,224 | 8 | 528 | 16 |
| Imaging | 6,144 | 5,824 | 320 | 593 | 16 |

The imaging plates are drawn from two plate maps, which are the two batches of this
batch of the study. The transcriptomic readout carries no batch column, so its grouping
unit is the assay plate. Joining the two readouts on the perturbation identifier leaves
528 perturbations with both profiles. 65 imaging perturbations have no transcriptomic
counterpart and are excluded. 509 of the admitted perturbations sit in one batch and 19
span both. Control wells are read, counted and reported, and they are never entities.

The gallery also publishes a full-feature imaging release for the same batch. This
protocol does not use it, because every imaging feature in the variable-selected release
has a finite value in every well, so the feature set needs no further selection rule of
its own, and the smaller object keeps the lab inside a CPU-first budget. The lab makes
no claim that its numbers transfer to the full feature set.

## The reading and the endpoint

An entity is one genetic perturbation with at least one treated well in each readout.
Its profile is one row per readout, the median across that perturbation's replicate
wells in that readout. Every perturbation therefore appears exactly once per readout,
so no replicate of a perturbation can be split across a training and a test side.

The transcriptomic features are the 978 Affymetrix landmark probe sets, found by the
naming rule for a landmark probe, and the imaging features are the 291 Cell Painting
features, found by the `Cells_`, `Nuclei_` and `Cytoplasm_` prefixes. Features are
found by name and never by position, so a release that adds or drops a column is either
read correctly or rejected.

The endpoint is cross-modal agreement, which asks how well the transcriptomic profile of
a perturbation predicts its imaging profile. The primary metric is the mean over
held-out perturbations of the Pearson correlation between the predicted and the
observed imaging profile. The secondary metric is the mean over imaging features of the
correlation between predicted and observed values across the held-out perturbations of a
fold. The model is a multi-output ridge map whose penalty is chosen from a fixed grid by
an inner five-fold cross-validation on the training rows of each fold. The
standardisation and the penalty are fitted on training rows only, so no held-out row
influences its own map.

The unit of inference is one perturbation. The grouping units are the imaging assay
plate, the imaging batch and the L1000 assay plate. Because each perturbation is
measured on 8 of the 16 plates in each readout, a plate cannot be held out at the level
of one perturbation, so a blocked evaluation holds out every perturbation measured on a
plate and trains on every perturbation never measured on it. The seed 20261021 fixes
every partition and every permutation.

Two controls sit underneath the comparisons, and both are cheap. The first replaces
each perturbation's imaging profile with another perturbation's imaging profile from the
same batch, which keeps the plate and batch structure and destroys only the pairing.
The second draws a random split with exactly the same training and test sizes as a
blocked evaluation, from the same population, so that a loss of performance can be read
as a design effect rather than as a smaller training set.

## The numbers

The table reproduces the committed summary at the four decimals its summary reader
prints.

| Evaluation | Perturbation r | Feature r | Folds | Perturbations tested |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Apparent (random five-fold) | 0.8158 | 0.8256 | 5 | 528 |
| Null, pairing destroyed within batch | 0.1317 | 0.0033 | 5 | 528 |
| Batch blocked (one whole batch held out) | 0.6886 | 0.6326 | 2 | 509 |
| Size-matched random, same sizes | 0.7923 | 0.7877 | 2 | 509 |
| Imaging plate blocked | 0.6921 | 0.6516 | 16 | 528 |
| Size-matched random, same sizes | 0.7931 | 0.8014 | 16 | 528 |
| Transcriptome plate blocked | 0.7041 | 0.6694 | 16 | 528 |
| Size-matched random, same sizes | 0.7972 | 0.8089 | 16 | 528 |

First the apparent agreement. With a random split, the imaging profile of a held-out
perturbation is predicted from its transcriptomic profile at a correlation of 0.8158 per
perturbation and 0.8256 per imaging feature. That number is not a design artefact.
Replacing each perturbation's imaging profile with another perturbation's imaging
profile from the same batch drops the agreement to 0.0033 per feature, and the
perturbation-level agreement to 0.1317. A model-free check agrees with the fitted one,
fitting nothing and drawing no split. The median Spearman correlation between
the transcriptomic and the imaging similarity rankings is 0.7449, against 0.0007 for the
same statistic under the permuted pairing.

Then the design. What the plate and the batch do to the measured variance is small. The
imaging plate accounts for 2.71% of the imaging feature variance, the L1000 plate for
1.56% of the transcriptomic feature variance, and the imaging batch for 0.36%. A small
variance share does not mean a small effect on a prediction, which is why the
agreement is measured again with the structure held out. Holding out one whole batch
drops the per-feature agreement from 0.8256 to 0.6326, and a random split of exactly the
same training and test sizes, drawn from the same population, reaches 0.7877. The batch
separation therefore costs 0.1551 of per-feature agreement that a smaller training
set does not explain. Holding out one imaging assay plate costs 0.1497 and holding
out one transcriptomic assay plate 0.1394, by the same comparison. The survival
ratio is the share of the above-null agreement that remains, computed as the blocked
per-feature correlation minus the null, divided by the apparent per-feature correlation
minus the null. It is 0.7653 for a held-out batch, 0.7885 for a held-out imaging plate
and 0.8101 for a held-out transcriptome plate.

That is the teaching point, and it is narrower than a signature being a mechanism. Three
quarters of the agreement between two readouts of the same perturbation survives an
evaluation in which the model never sees the batch the perturbation came from. The other
quarter does not, and a design-blind analysis would have reported all of it.

Named examples at the ends of the apparent agreement distribution:

| Perturbation | Perturbation r |
| --- | ---: |
| `LAP3_WT.o` | 0.9861 |
| `NFE2L2_WT.c` | 0.9846 |
| `NFE2L2_p.T80K` | 0.9838 |
| `MDM2_p.E421Q` | -0.1525 |
| `RBM15B_WT.o` | -0.0802 |
| `NUP88_WT.o` | -0.0117 |

A negative correlation means the transcriptomic profile of that perturbation points the
wrong way for its imaging profile. These six rows are where to start reading if you
want to know which entity a number is about, and they are not evidence about the genes.

## What this does not show

- It does not show that a perturbation signature is a mechanism. It measures agreement
  between two readouts and the survival of that agreement under the measurement design.
- It does not show that the imaging readout can be predicted for a perturbation the
  model has never seen in any real deployment, because the holdouts here are holdouts
  inside one published experiment.
- It does not separate the batch's nuisance effect from the fact that a batch-homogeneous
  training set covers less of the perturbation space. The size-matched columns isolate
  the cost of the structural separation at a fixed training-set size and nothing more.
- It says nothing about any gene, any patient or any clinical outcome. The named
  perturbations at the ends of the agreement distribution are the ends of a
  distribution.
- The protocol fixes one batch of one study, one cell line, one timepoint and one set
  of engineered edits. The study's other four batches, the other gallery studies and
  the JUMP pilot are outside this protocol, and nothing here transfers to them
  without being re-measured.
- The imaging feature set is the gallery's variable-selected release of 291 features.
  The gallery's full-feature release for the same batch was not used, and this lab does
  not claim its numbers transfer to it.
- The model is a ridge map and not a state-of-the-art cross-modal predictor. Its
  penalty is selected inside each training fold, but the architecture is not tuned, so
  the numbers bound this simple map's agreement and not the agreement that is
  achievable.
- The transcriptomic readout carries plate-matched negative controls on only 6 of its 16
  plates, so no control-based baseline can be computed for it without silently covering
  part of the design. The protocol therefore adds no baseline subtraction of its own
  and uses the gallery's published normalisation as published.
- No reader outcomes have been collected for this lab. The committed summary records
  what the pipeline computes, not whether anyone has learned anything from it.

## Independent exercise

A slide states that the transcriptome predicts the Cell Painting profile of a held-out
perturbation at a feature correlation of 0.8256, and that the perturbation signature is
therefore a mechanism.

1. Name the two controls this lab runs that a design-blind analysis would not have, and
   say what each one would show if the plate and batch structure, rather than the
   perturbation, were producing the agreement.
2. Holding out one whole batch drops the per-feature agreement to 0.6326, while a random
   split with exactly the same training and test sizes reaches 0.7877. Compute the
   difference, name it, and state which of the two numbers you would report as the
   agreement of a cross-modal map.
3. The most agreeable perturbation in the distribution is `LAP3_WT.o` at 0.9861 and the
   least is `MDM2_p.E421Q` at -0.1525. What do those two rows establish about the
   perturbation, and what do they not establish?

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

The two controls are the null that keeps the plate and batch structure and destroys only
the pairing, and the size-matched random split. If the design were producing the
agreement, the permuted null would sit near the apparent 0.8256 rather than at 0.0033, and
a blocked fold would reach the value of its size-matched random twin. The model-free
neighbourhood concordance of 0.7449 is a third, independent agreement, because it fits
nothing and draws no split and so cannot inherit a split-specific shortcut. Its
permuted counterpart falls to 0.0007.

The difference is 0.7877 - 0.6326 = 0.1551, and the protocol calls it the design cost of
the structural separation at a fixed training-set size. The blocked value of 0.6326 is
the number to report, because the size-matched random split at 0.7877 is the value the
design would have produced with the same amount of training data.

The two named rows are the ends of an agreement distribution and nothing more. They say
that under one fitted map the transcriptomic profile and the imaging profile of those
two perturbations pointed in the same direction, or in opposite directions for the
second one. They are not evidence about LAP3 or about MDM2, and the negative value says
the map pointed the wrong way for that perturbation rather than that the perturbation
does nothing.
