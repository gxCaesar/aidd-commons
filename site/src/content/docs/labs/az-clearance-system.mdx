## Why this document, and why three named assays

The data here comes from one real, citable ChEMBL dataset deposition,
[document `CHEMBL3301361`](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/explore/document/CHEMBL3301361),
"Experimental in vitro DMPK and physicochemical data on a set of publicly
disclosed compounds" (AstraZeneca, Mark Wenlock and Nicholas Tomkinson,
2016), not a proxy for a paywalled paper. It reports intrinsic clearance in
three named assays: human liver microsomes, human hepatocytes and rat
hepatocytes. `protocol.json` admits only these three, by assay ID, because
they are exactly the systems this document itself distinguishes, and reads
each one's assay description to confirm the species and matrix it names.

## An admission rule adapted to a real difference

This endpoint's real data does not use `standard_relation="="` for an exact
value the way the sibling IC50 labs do. Here, an empty `standard_relation`
field is the exact value, and `"<"` or `">"` marks a reading outside the
assay's own quantifiable range, for example "&lt;3 to &gt;150 microL/min/mg"
as the assay description states. A record is admitted only when its relation
field is empty, `standard_flag=1`, `potential_duplicate=0`, it carries no
data validity comment, its molecule is its own parent, its structure is
RDKit-valid, and its two independently reported value fields agree. Computed
on 2026-09-12:

| Assay | Species | Matrix | Input rows | Admitted parents |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| `CHEMBL3301370` | Human | Microsome | 1,102 | 743 |
| `CHEMBL3301371` | Rat | Hepatocyte | 837 | 592 |
| `CHEMBL3301372` | Human | Hepatocyte | 408 | 288 |

No admitted parent carries more than one activity in any of the three
assays, so no median aggregation happens in this run; `show` still reports
the duplicate-parent count so a future re-fetch that does find repeats does
not go unreported.

The microsome assay reports clearance per gram of microsomal protein
(`mL.min-1.g-1`); both hepatocyte assays report it per million whole cells
(`uL.min-1.(10^6cells)-1`). These are different physical bases, not two
units of the same quantity, and this protocol applies no scaling factor to
convert between them. Multiplying either axis by a fixed positive unit-conversion
factor leaves both Spearman rho and Pearson r unchanged. This mathematical
property does not make the two biological normalization bases interchangeable.
The `log10 ratio` printed for a microsome-involving pair compares the reported
numbers, but its magnitude and sign depend on their numerical units. It cannot
establish a physical fold change or the direction of a clearance difference
between those systems. The two hepatocyte assays share a physical basis and
unit, so their paired values support literal fold-change comparisons.

## Three comparisons, two variables

`show` reports all three pairwise overlaps, each holding one variable fixed
where possible:

| Comparison | What varies | Overlap | Full Spearman rho | Clean-subset Spearman rho |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Human hepatocytes vs. rat hepatocytes | species, matrix fixed | 106 | 0.4732 | 0.4732 |
| Human liver microsomes vs. human hepatocytes | matrix, species fixed | 96 | 0.4864 | 0.4864 |
| Human liver microsomes vs. rat hepatocytes | both differ | 359 | 0.3868 | 0.3868 |

The full overlap and the clean single-pair subset agree exactly here because
no parent in this snapshot carries a repeated admitted activity in any
assay; `check` asserts that the clean subset is always a subset of the full
overlap regardless. All three observed Spearman coefficients are positive and
below 1, indicating partial rank agreement among the shared parents. A
correlation coefficient is not a fraction of predictive value retained. This
descriptive lab fits no predictive model and does not estimate a loss of
predictive performance or a causal effect of species or matrix.

## What the correlation hides

Named divergent examples from `show`, for the cleanest single-variable
comparison (species, matrix fixed at hepatocyte):

| Compound | Human hepatocyte CL | Rat hepatocyte CL | log10 ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `CHEMBL361546` | 135 | 14.8 | +0.96 |
| `CHEMBL1229592` | 3.31 | 79.4 | −1.38 |

Both real compounds sit in the admitted set for both species; one clears
roughly ninefold faster in human hepatocytes than in rat, the other roughly
24-fold faster in rat than in human. A single cross-species clearance ratio
does not exist for this pair of assays; a claim needs to say for which
compound, and cite the actual measured pair.

## What this does not show

This protocol reads only in vitro intrinsic-clearance records from one
document. It says nothing about absorption, volume of distribution or oral
bioavailability, the other PK-exposure measures its taxonomy topic names,
and it makes no causal or human-dose claim: an in vitro clearance
disagreement between two assay systems is not a prospective interaction or
dosing statement. See `protocol.json`'s `excluded_by_design` for the
complete, machine-readable list.

## Independent exercise

A summary table reports "Compound Z: CL = 22" with no further detail.

1. Using only what `show` reports, name the two axes (not one) that this
   number is missing, and give one real compound from the tables above
   whose value would look completely different if either axis changed.
2. The human-hepatocyte-vs-rat-hepatocyte comparison and the
   human-microsome-vs-human-hepatocyte comparison both land close to
   Spearman rho ≈ 0.48. Does that similarity mean species and matrix affect
   clearance by the same mechanism? What would you need, beyond this
   protocol, to tell those two mechanisms apart?

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

The missing axes are species (human vs. rat) and matrix (microsome vs.
whole-cell hepatocyte). The three named assays make those system identities
explicit, while the descriptive comparisons do not isolate their causal effects.
`CHEMBL361546` differs by close to tenfold between human and rat hepatocytes, both reported in the
same unit, so that is a real fold-change with the other axis fixed. The
microsome-vs-hepatocyte pair cannot show an equally clean example: its two
assays report clearance in different physical units (per gram of
microsomal protein versus per million whole cells), so a raw ratio between
them, such as `CHEMBL2181927`'s or `CHEMBL1916271`'s, cannot establish a
physical fold change or its direction. Changing the numerical units can change
the sign of the printed log ratio. A similar correlation coefficient across two
pairs does not identify a shared mechanism. Discriminating the causes would require additional evidence with
controls that separate the proposed species and matrix effects. These
observational records alone do not provide that identification.
