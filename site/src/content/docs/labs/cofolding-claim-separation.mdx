## Before you run

**Mode: runnable inspection of committed files.** The command reads a frozen
extract of the resource registry, committed beside it, holding the 32
registered records that make a structural or affinity claim, selected from 232
source records and checked on 2026-10-25. It makes no network
request, downloads no structure, imports no third-party package and predicts
nothing. It does not evaluate any system's accuracy. It answers a question that
comes before accuracy: what is this system's output a claim about?

```bash
python labs/cofolding_claims/inspect_claims.py show
```

A predicted monomer, a predicted complex, a co-folded protein-ligand pose, a
docked pose and an experimental binding measurement can all reach you as a
coordinate file with a score attached. The file format does not distinguish them
and neither does the vocabulary. The registry does, because each record declares
the task it performs and where its evidence stops.

Membership follows the declared task and nothing else. `export_claim_records.py`
selects every record whose tasks intersect the five class definitions, so no
system can be hand-picked into this family or quietly left out of it, and the
exporter refuses to overwrite the extract unless asked, naming each record that
would change.

## 1. The modality label does not name the claim

The first block groups every registered system in this family by the claim its
task licenses. Read the modality column beside it. The label
`biomolecular-complex` appears under four different claim classes, and
`small-molecule` under three.

That is the first place a reader gets moved from one claim to another without
noticing. Two systems can both be labelled for complexes while one predicts a
structure and the other estimates an affinity, and a sentence that says "it works
on complexes" is true of both while meaning different things.

## 2. One system can make two claims at once

Of the 32 records, exactly one declares both a co-folded complex and an
affinity estimate: `model-boltz-2`. That is not a flaw in the record. It is a
real property of a real model, and it is precisely where the two claims are
easiest to merge.

A pose and a potency are separate outputs of the same run. One being good does
not make the other good. They can disagree on the same compounds, and a reader
who has seen a convincing structure figure has been given no information about
whether the compound is active.

OpenFold3/OpenBind-0 also occupies two extract classes, through
`structure-prediction` and `pose-validation`. It does not add a second
pose-plus-affinity record. The three-record co-folding subset uses the literal
`co-folding` task label; these groups follow declarations, not a fresh assessment
of every model's capabilities.

## 3. None of these is an experimental measurement

The third block prints the record kinds present in this family. They are models,
software, model assets, benchmarks and a paper. Not one is a measurement record.

Experimental binding evidence lives somewhere else entirely, in a record that
describes an assay and its conditions. No entry in this family can be cited for
what a compound actually does in an experiment, however good its structural
output looks.

## 4. Check what the registry can and cannot support

```bash
python labs/cofolding_claims/inspect_claims.py check
```

Ten checks run. They confirm that the extract and the inspector group by the
same five classes, that every extracted record earned its place through a
declared task rather than by name, that every system carries a review date, that
no co-folding system evidences both its training data and the code behind its
accuracy claim, that exactly one system claims both a pose and an affinity, that
no measurement record is hiding in the family, and that at least one modality
label spans more than one claim class.

The literal co-folding subset is not uniform on training-data evidence. Two of
its three records mark training data available: `model-boltz-1` and
`model-boltz-2`. Only the first also has verified training code; Chai-1's training
data remain unverified. All three leave evaluation code unverified. These are
recorded evidence states, not findings from a training or evaluation run.

One check deserves reading twice. **None of the three records declaring the
literal co-folding task evidences evaluation code.** Seven records in the wider
family do: `benchmark-multipardigm-docking`, `benchmark-plabench`,
`benchmark-plinder`, `benchmark-posebench`, `benchmark-protdbench`,
`model-diffdock` and `model-rosettafold-all-atom`. The five benchmark entries
expose scoring assets; the two model entries expose model-evaluation assets.
Those are different objects. This extract supplies no route to recompute the
co-folding scores, and it does not establish that unverified code is absent
outside the recorded review surfaces.

## Independent exercise

A colleague sends you a predicted protein-ligand complex and writes: "this model
reached 0.8 angstroms median ligand RMSD in its paper, so the pose is reliable
enough for us to prioritise this compound for synthesis."

Name the distinct claims being chained together in that sentence, and for each
one say whether anything in this registry can support it.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer — three claims, and what the registry reaches</summary>

**Claim one, the reported accuracy.** A median over a benchmark set is a property
of that set, not of your target. Even taken at face value it says nothing about
the hardest cases, and a median hides the tail where a pose is wrong by enough to
change a decision. The registry can locate the system and its task, and it shows
that evaluation code is not evidenced for the literal co-folding subset, and for
25 of the 32 records in the family. The extract therefore does not establish how
to recheck this number from the published artifacts. The number is not verified
here.

**Claim two, this pose is right.** A benchmark median does not transfer to one
new complex, and the registry offers nothing about your target at all. This is
where the modality label misleads most: the system is labelled for complexes,
your case is a complex, and the sentence slides from one to the other without an
argument.

**Claim three, the compound is worth making.** This is a potency and tractability
claim, and no record in this family is a measurement record. A correct pose is
compatible with an inactive compound. The registry can show that the system also
declares an affinity task if it does, which is a second prediction, still not a
measurement.

A complete answer notices that the three claims need three different kinds of
evidence, and that the sentence supplied only one number for all three. The
useful reply is not "your model is wrong" but "which of these three do you want
to establish, and what would establish it".

</details>

## What this does not do

This route does not run a co-folding model, evaluate one, or rank any system
against another. It cannot tell you whether a prediction is accurate. It reads
declarations and evidence states from a registry maintained in this repository,
so its authority is exactly the authority of those records, and each record's own
caveats and review date travel with it. An `unverified` openness state means the
recorded review has not established the asset's availability. It is not a claim
that the artefact does not exist.

For the systems themselves, see the
[open co-folding frontier inventory](/atlas/open-cofolding-frontier/) and the
[structure and co-folding lesson](/learn/structure-and-pockets/cofolding-frontier/).
