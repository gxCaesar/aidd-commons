## Before you run

**Mode: runnable demonstration on constructed data.** The command reads one
committed JSON file, fits a ridge model on Morgan fingerprints, and traces the
credit back to atoms. **No activity value here is real**, no molecule was
measured, and nothing is a design proposal.

```bash
python labs/sar_attribution/inspect_attribution.py show
```

Model explanations are routinely read as statements about which atoms cause
activity. On real data that reading cannot be checked, because the cause is
exactly what you were trying to learn. **Here it can**, because the labels come
from a rule stated in the dataset.

## 1. The dataset stores a rule, not labels

A molecule is active if and only if it contains a carboxylic acid. That is the
whole generating rule. The file contains **no label field at all** — labels are
computed from the rule every time, so no label can drift away from its cause, and
a test asserts the file has none.

A second substructure, an unsubstituted furan, is **perfectly correlated with the
cause** across the sixteen training molecules and has no causal role whatsoever.
Every active carries both; no inactive carries either.

That correlation is not a flaw in the data. It is the ordinary situation. A
series explored around one scaffold will confound the scaffold with whatever made
the series worth exploring.

## 2. The model fits, which is when an explanation feels safest

Largest training error across all sixteen molecules: **0.0549**. Actives predict
near one, inactives near zero.

This is the case where reading an explanation feels safe. The model has clearly
learned something, so surely the explanation reports what it learned. It does —
and that is the problem.

## 3. The credit lands on atoms that cause nothing

Per-atom credit is traced back through fingerprint bit provenance, then summed
over the acid atoms, the furan atoms, and everything else.

**The confounder outscores the true cause on 7 of 8 active molecules.** Every
active molecule assigns positive credit to furan atoms, which by construction
cause nothing.

It won for a reason worth naming. **The furan appears in an identical environment
every time, so its bits repeat. The acid appears in a different environment each
time, so its credit is split across many distinct bits.** Uniformity, not
importance, is what concentrates weight — and nothing in the explanation
distinguishes the two.

## 4. The analogues are the experiment

An explanation produces a hypothesis: *the furan matters*. A matched pair that
breaks the correlation is what tests it.

| id | molecule | cause | proxy | predicted | called |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| a01 | `CCCc1ccoc1` | 0 | 1 | +0.4908 | inactive |
| a02 | `OC(=O)CCc1ccccc1` | 1 | 0 | +0.4607 | inactive |
| a03 | `OC(=O)CCc1ccoc1` | 1 | 1 | +0.9671 | active |
| a04 | `CCCCc1ccncc1` | 0 | 0 | -0.0086 | inactive |

Read a01 and a02 together. They have **opposite true activity**. The model
separates them by **0.0300** and puts them in the **wrong order**: the molecule
with no cause at all scores higher than the genuinely active one, which is then
called inactive at a threshold declared before fitting.

The two controls behave exactly as the rule dictates, which is what makes the
first pair readable rather than noise.

```bash
python labs/sar_attribution/inspect_attribution.py check
```

Fourteen checks run, including that the correlation is perfect in training, that
the confounder is declared non-causal, that the threshold was declared before
fitting, and that the inverted ranking still holds.

## 5. What this licenses, and what it does not

**It licenses a hypothesis.** An explanation reports which features the model
used. That is a fact about the model and it is worth having. Turning it into a
claim about which atoms cause activity needs an analogue that breaks the
correlation, and that is an experiment rather than a re-reading of the same fit.

**It does not license a causal atom effect.** Here the non-causal substructure
carried the larger share of credit, and that was visible only because the
generating rule was known.

**On real data this check is unavailable.** You cannot compare the explanation to
the cause, because the cause is what you are trying to find. The analogue is the
only route left, and it costs an experiment.

## Independent exercise

A colleague shows you an attribution heat map over an active compound. The
sulfonamide is coloured strongly. They propose: "the sulfonamide is driving
activity, so let's keep it fixed and optimise elsewhere."

Say what the heat map does and does not support, and design the cheapest
experiment that would distinguish the two possibilities.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer — what the colour means, and the pair to make</summary>

**The heat map supports one claim: the model's prediction depends on those
atoms.** That is a fact about the fitted function, and it is checkable by
perturbing the input. It is not a fact about chemistry.

**The competing explanation is a confound, and it is usually the likelier one.**
If the sulfonamide entered the series early and every subsequent analogue kept it,
then every active in the training data carries it and the model cannot separate
its contribution from whatever else those molecules share. The lab above is that
situation made visible: the confounder took the larger share of credit while
causing nothing.

**"Keep it fixed and optimise elsewhere" is the decision the confound damages
most.** It removes the only compounds that could have distinguished the two
explanations, and it does so before the question is asked. After that round, the
correlation is stronger and the heat map looks more convincing, having gained no
new evidence.

**The cheapest experiment is a matched pair.** Make one analogue that removes or
replaces the sulfonamide while holding the rest of the molecule as fixed as
synthesis allows, and one that keeps the sulfonamide but changes a region the
model treats as unimportant. If activity survives removal, the attribution was
reading a confound. If it collapses, the hypothesis survives this test — which is
not the same as being confirmed, and the next series will re-confound it unless
the pair is repeated.

**And check what the model says about the pair before making it.** In the lab
above the model gave two molecules of opposite true activity scores 0.0300 apart
and in the wrong order. A model that cannot separate the pair on paper is telling
you the pair is worth making, and that its own attribution should not be used to
skip it.

</details>

## What this does not do

This route does not evaluate an attribution method, rank explanation techniques,
or establish anything about real structure-activity relationships. It constructs
one situation where the answer is known and shows what an explanation does in it.
The molecules were authored for teaching, and the exact predictions depend on the
fingerprint implementation, observed with RDKit 2025.09.6 and NumPy 2.4.6.
