## Before you run

**Mode: runnable inspection of committed experimental structures.** Three
deposited crystal structures of the human EGFR kinase domain are in the
repository, unmodified apart from decompression. **No structure is prepared,
protonated, minimised or docked, and no pocket-prediction method is run.** Both
commands print to the terminal and write no file, so running them cannot
overwrite anything.

You need Python 3.11 or newer and NumPy, and no network access. From the
repository root:

```bash
python labs/egfr_structures/inspect_state.py show
python labs/egfr_structures/inspect_complex.py show
```

If you downloaded the files from this page instead, keep `cif.py`, the two
commands and `entries.json` in one folder and put the three `.cif` files in a
subfolder named `structures`. Each command runs in about a second.

**What you should be able to do afterwards.**

- Say what a deposited structure file is evidence of, and what its resolution and
  R-free do not tell you.
- Compare residues across entries only after mapping them to one reference
  sequence, and check the mapping.
- Treat a binding site, a docking box, an interaction and a pose verdict as
  outputs of declared choices, and name the choice behind each.

**Prerequisites.** [Protein, complex, cell-state and knowledge-graph
representations](/learn/representations/complex-cell-and-knowledge-graphs/) and
[pocket states, confidence and reusable structural
evidence](/learn/structure-and-pockets/pocket-states-and-confidence/). The
[docking protocol lab](/labs/docking-protocol/) is the companion: it separates the
seven components a docking score comes out of, and this lab supplies the
structures such a protocol would start from.

## The three files

| Entry | Ligand | Resolution | Why it is here |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1M17 | erlotinib | 2.60 A | The reference frame for every superposition |
| 4HJO | erlotinib | 2.75 A | The same ligand in a kinase domain its depositors call inactive |
| 1XKK | lapatinib | 2.40 A | A different quinazoline inhibitor with a larger substituent |

They were downloaded on 2026-09-11 from the RCSB PDB file service. PDB archive
data files are released under CC0 1.0. wwPDB asks that the original authors be
credited, so both commands print each entry's primary citation. The manifest
`entries.json` records each source address, byte size, PDB entry identifier,
deposition date and latest deposited revision. Both checks validate these fields
before reporting a verdict. They do not certify byte-for-byte identity.

## Part A. What the files say, and how a site is defined

### 1. A file is a model of an experiment

```text
1M17  X-RAY DIFFRACTION, 2.60 A, R-work 0.251, R-free 0.295 (gap 0.044)
4HJO  X-RAY DIFFRACTION, 2.75 A, R-work 0.230, R-free 0.253 (gap 0.023)
1XKK  X-RAY DIFFRACTION, 2.40 A, R-work 0.209, R-free 0.255 (gap 0.046)
```

A crystal structure is an atomic model refined against diffraction data.
Resolution is the finest spacing in the data that were used. R-work measures how
well the model reproduces the reflections it was refined against, and R-free does
the same on a small set of reflections withheld from refinement. **R-free is the
crystallographer's validation set**, and a large gap between the two is the same
warning as a large gap between training and validation error.

All three numbers describe the whole model. None of them says how well one atom,
one residue or one ligand is determined.

### 2. What was crystallised, and how it is numbered

```text
1M17  P00533 695-1022 aligned; author number + 24 = reference number
      hinge methionine: author 769, reference 793
4HJO  P00533 696-1022 aligned; author number + 24 = reference number
      hinge methionine: author 769, reference 793
      substitutions: VAL948ARG (engineered mutation)
1XKK  P00533 695-1022 aligned; author number +  0 = reference number
      hinge methionine: author 793, reference 793
```

**The same methionine is residue 769 in two files and 793 in the third.** The
offset of 24 is the length of the signal peptide, which older numbering leaves
out. The best-known resistance mutation in this kinase, T790M, appears as T766M
in that convention. Comparing residues by the number printed in the file would
therefore compare different residues.

The command maps every residue to the UniProt reference sequence P00533 through
the file's own alignment record, and the check confirms that five landmark
positions hold the expected residue after mapping: Lys745, Glu762, Thr790, Met793
and Asp855.

The file also records what was actually crystallised. 4HJO carries one engineered
substitution, V948R. **The file records that the substitution was engineered but
not why**, and the reason belongs to its primary citation, not to this lab. Each
construct also lacks residues the model does not contain: 16 in 1M17, 49 in
4HJO and 39 in 1XKK, including the whole C-terminal stretch 985 to 1022 in 4HJO.
A missing loop is a region the data did not determine, not a region that is
absent from the protein.

### 3. One state descriptor, and who assigned the label

```text
1M17  Lys745 NZ to Glu762 CD: 3.71 A
4HJO  Lys745 NZ to Glu762 CD: 14.41 A
1XKK  Lys745 NZ to Glu762 CD: 13.73 A
```

In an active kinase the catalytic lysine pairs with a glutamate on the C-helix.
When that helix swings out, the two separate. The glutamate's CD atom stands in
for its carboxylate so the number does not depend on which oxygen happens to be
closer.

The distance is computed here. **The word "inactive" in the 4HJO title is the
depositors' label.** One distance agrees with that label, and it is not a state
assignment on its own: a full assignment also reads the activation loop and the
conformation of the DFG motif, which this lab does not.

### 4. How well the ligand is determined, relative to its protein

```text
1M17  erlotinib (AQ4), 29 heavy atoms: mean B 72.8 against protein 63.0; waters modelled 20
4HJO  erlotinib (AQ4), 29 heavy atoms: mean B 47.5 against protein 47.2; waters modelled 34
1XKK  lapatinib (FMM), 40 heavy atoms: mean B 41.8 against protein 42.5; waters modelled 77
```

A B-factor describes how smeared an atom's position is in the model. In 1M17 the
ligand is less well defined than the protein around it, in the other two it is
not. No file models a hydrogen atom, so **protonation is not in the evidence at
all**. Section 7 shows what that costs.

### 5. The site is a definition

| Entry | Residues within 4 A | within 5 A | within 6 A |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1M17 | 15 | 20 | 25 |
| 4HJO | 17 | 20 | 26 |
| 1XKK | 23 | 28 | 37 |

"The binding site" is the set of residues within a chosen distance of a chosen
ligand. **Every site grows at every step of the cutoff**, so its size is a
property of the cutoff as much as of the protein.

At 4 A the two erlotinib sites share a Jaccard index of 0.78, and Glu762 is in
the 1M17 site but not in the 4HJO site: the same ligand, a different receptor
state, a different site. The lapatinib site contains eight residues that neither
erlotinib site reaches, 766, 775, 776, 777, 799, 803, 856 and 858.

All three ligands sit in the ATP site. No file carries a ligand anywhere else, so
**these entries cannot define an allosteric or transient pocket**, and no
pocket-prediction method was run to suggest one.

### 6. A docking box inherits its ligand and its frame

```text
4HJO onto 1M17: site C-alpha RMSD 57.78 A before, 1.45 A after superposition (20 residues)
1XKK onto 1M17: site C-alpha RMSD 40.60 A before, 1.21 A after superposition (20 residues)
4HJO  box volume at 0 A padding: 883 A^3 in its own frame, 393 A^3 in the 1M17 frame
1XKK ligand atoms inside the 1M17 erlotinib box: 21/40 at 0 A, 32/40 at 2 A, 36/40 at 4 A, 37/40 at 5 A
```

Each deposited file sits in its own coordinate frame, which is why the site
C-alpha atoms are tens of angstroms apart before superposition. Most docking
programs take an axis-aligned search box in the frame of the receptor file. **The
same pose gives a box of a different volume in another frame**, because the box
edges follow the axes rather than the molecule.

A box drawn around erlotinib and padded by 5 A still leaves 3 of the 40
lapatinib atoms outside. A search box is a hypothesis about where a ligand can go,
and it inherits the ligand it was drawn around.

## Part B. Contacts, interactions and poses

### 7. A contact is geometry; an interaction adds an assumption

```bash
python labs/egfr_structures/inspect_complex.py show
```

```text
1M17  4 polar pairs, 2 donor-acceptor compatible
      N2   acceptor   MET793 N    donor     2.70 A  could be a hydrogen bond
      O4   acceptor   PRO794 O    acceptor  3.21 A  not a hydrogen bond under these roles
      O4   acceptor   GLY796 N    donor     3.24 A  could be a hydrogen bond
      O2   acceptor   LEU718 O    acceptor  3.27 A  not a hydrogen bond under these roles
```

A **contact** is two atoms closer than a cutoff, here 3.5 A between nitrogen and
oxygen atoms. A **hydrogen bond** additionally needs one partner to carry a
hydrogen and the other to accept it. No file models a hydrogen, so the roles are
declared in the manifest: quinazoline ring nitrogens as acceptors, the anilino
nitrogen as a donor, ether oxygens as acceptors, and the protein under a neutral
pH assumption. The check verifies every declared ligand role against the ligand's
own bonding before it reports any verdict.

Two of the four 1M17 pairs pair two acceptors and cannot be hydrogen bonds under
these roles, however short they are. The one pair every complex shares is the
ring nitrogen facing the backbone NH of Met793, the hinge contact. **If that ring
nitrogen were protonated it could no longer accept, and the same 2.70 A pair
would stop being a possible hydrogen bond without any atom moving.** The test
suite contains exactly that edit.

### 8. Water-mediated contacts depend on which waters were modelled

```text
1M17  20 waters modelled; bridges: N3 - water - THR790 OG1
4HJO  34 waters modelled; bridges: N3 - water - THR790 OG1; N3 - water - THR854 OG1
1XKK  77 waters modelled; bridges: N20 - water - THR854 OG1
```

Every complex has at least one bridge through a water, including one to the
gatekeeper Thr790. The number of modelled waters follows resolution and refinement
choices, so **a representation that keeps waters inherits those choices, and one
that drops them loses a real contact.**

### 9. A fingerprint belongs to a complex, not to a ligand

```text
1M17 vs 4HJO (same ligand): Tanimoto 0.650, 0.667 without water bits
1M17 vs 1XKK (different ligands): Tanimoto 0.458, 0.500 without water bits
4HJO vs 1XKK (different ligands): Tanimoto 0.583, 0.591 without water bits
```

Each complex becomes a set of residue-interaction bits: polar, carbon-carbon
within 4 A, and water-mediated. The same ligand in two receptor states shares only
65 percent of its bits. Two different ligands in the two C-helix-out states, 4HJO
and 1XKK, share more bits (0.583) than erlotinib in the reference state shares
with lapatinib (0.458). **An interaction fingerprint encodes the receptor state as
much as the ligand.**

### 10. Two experimental poses of one ligand

```text
erlotinib, 29 atoms matched by name: RMSD 1.65 A; quinazoline core 0.80 A; everything else 1.96 A
largest single displacement 3.55 A at C14
1M17 mean B: core 53.6, rest 82.9
```

After superposing the two receptors on 20 site C-alpha atoms, the two erlotinib
poses differ by 1.65 A. Under the common 2 A convention they are one pose. The
core agrees to 0.80 A while one atom of a side chain moves 3.55 A, and in 1M17 the
side chains are also the least determined part of the ligand. **A single RMSD
averages a well-placed core with a loosely placed periphery.**

### 11. One pose, three receptor states

```text
ligand from \ receptor             1M17            4HJO            1XKK
1M17 erlotinib       0 (2.70 A)      0 (2.86 A)      0 (2.44 A)
4HJO erlotinib       1 (1.97 A)      0 (2.89 A)      3 (0.71 A)
1XKK lapatinib       6 (1.21 A)      0 (2.60 A)      0 (2.96 A)
```

Each cell places one ligand rigidly into one receptor after superposition and
counts heavy atoms closer than 2.2 A to a protein heavy atom, with the closest
distance in brackets. Every ligand fits its own receptor. **Lapatinib clashes with
six atoms in the reference receptor and with none in the 4HJO state.** With side
chains held fixed, one receptor conformation rejects a ligand that binds another
conformation of the same protein. A clash says the pose does not fit that
receptor state. It does not say the ligand cannot bind the protein.

### 12. Three ways to judge a pose, and where they disagree

```text
crystal pose
    RMSD 0.00 A (passes), hinge 2.70 A (kept), clashing atoms 0
constructed: translated 1.5 A away from the hinge
    RMSD 1.50 A (passes), hinge 4.20 A (lost), clashing atoms 0
constructed: rotated 20 degrees about the hinge contact
    RMSD 1.61 A (passes), hinge 2.70 A (kept), clashing atoms 4
```

The two constructed poses are **not predictions**. They are declared rigid motions
of the 1M17 crystal pose, made to show that three common criteria answer three
different questions. RMSD asks how far the atoms moved, the hinge criterion asks
whether one key contact survived, and the clash count asks whether the pose fits
the receptor. **Both constructed poses pass a 2 A RMSD convention**, one having
lost the hinge contact and the other clashing with four atoms. A benchmark that
reports only an RMSD success rate would count both as correct.

```bash
python labs/egfr_structures/inspect_state.py check
python labs/egfr_structures/inspect_complex.py check
```

Sixteen state checks and fifteen complex checks recompute every relationship
above. A missing or nonregular file, byte-size mismatch, wrong entry identifier
or mismatched deposition or revision stops both commands before any verdict.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer, with the output this change produces</summary>

Every cell becomes nonzero, including the three diagonal cells where each ligand
sits in its own receptor:

```text
ligand from \ receptor             1M17            4HJO            1XKK
1M17 erlotinib       1 (2.70 A)      2 (2.86 A)      4 (2.44 A)
4HJO erlotinib       5 (1.97 A)      2 (2.89 A)      8 (0.71 A)
1XKK lapatinib      12 (1.21 A)      7 (2.60 A)      1 (2.96 A)
```

Two checks fail: "every ligand fits its own receptor without a clash" and "the
crystal pose passes all three criteria in its own receptor".

The reason is in the 1M17 diagonal cell. Its closest contact is 2.70 A, and that
contact is the hinge hydrogen bond from section 7. **A 3.0 A cutoff counts the
crystal structure's own hydrogen bond as a clash.** Donor and acceptor atoms in a
hydrogen bond sit closer than the sum of their van der Waals radii, so a clash
cutoff has to sit below the closest legitimate contacts. The check that the
crystal pose fits its own receptor exists to catch this choice, and it did.

The largest cell is also instructive. It is still the lapatinib pose in 1M17, so
the most visible result looks the same while every count underneath it changed.
A wrong cutoff can leave the headline looking plausible.

## Independent exercise 2. A screening plan built on one structure

A colleague plans to dock a library of 4-anilinoquinazolines into 1M17, using an
axis-aligned box drawn around erlotinib and padded by 4 A, and to rank the library
by score. Using only what the two commands print, give three reasons the ranking
could reflect the setup rather than the compounds, and one change that addresses
each.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer — receptor state, box, and the pose check</summary>

**The receptor state.** Section 11 shows the lapatinib pose clashing with six
atoms in 1M17 and with none in 4HJO. A rigid 1M17 receptor will penalise any
compound that binds the C-helix-out state, whatever its affinity. Dock into more
than one receptor state, for example 1M17 and 4HJO, and report scores per state
rather than one pooled ranking.

**The box.** Section 6 shows 4 of the 40 lapatinib atoms outside the erlotinib
box at 4 A of padding, and the box volume depends on the frame. Larger
substituents will be truncated or forced into odd poses. Define the box from the
site residues or from the union of known ligands, report its frame, and check how
much of a reference ligand it encloses.

**The pose check.** Section 12 shows two poses that pass a 2 A RMSD convention
while losing the hinge contact or clashing. If the protocol is validated only by
redocking erlotinib and reporting RMSD, it can pass validation with poses that
are chemically wrong. Validate with RMSD, the hinge contact and a clash count
together.

A fourth reason is also defensible. Protonation of the ring nitrogen and the
handling of waters change which interactions a scoring function can reward
(sections 7 and 8), so both should be declared in the protocol, as in the
[docking protocol lab](/labs/docking-protocol/).

</details>

## What this does not do

It prepares no structure, protonates nothing, minimises nothing and docks nothing.
Three structures of one protein cannot establish an allosteric or transient
pocket, because every ligand here is in the ATP site. The distance in section 3
agrees with a label and does not assign a conformational state. A rigid placement
cannot say whether a ligand binds, only whether it fits one receptor conformation
with side chains fixed. **None of the numbers on this page is a binding affinity.**
