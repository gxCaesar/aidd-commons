## Before you run

**Mode: runnable inspection of committed files.** The command reads the completed
pool-budget replay and the ChEMBL development summary this repository already
ships. Nothing is fitted, no compound is acquired, and no experiment is run.

```bash
python labs/screening_transport/inspect_transport.py show
```

A policy that loses in two assays looks twice as convincingly beaten as one that
loses in a single assay. **That inference needs two things the headline never
carries**, and this lab checks both.

## 1. Enrichment without its denominator is unreadable

Recovery is counted in targets. The two contexts hold **64** and **13** of them.

One recovered target moves the score by 0.015625 in the first context and
0.076923 in the second, so the coarser one measures recovery in steps **4.92
times larger**. The same reported number therefore means a different amount of
evidence in each, and neither is readable without the target count printed beside
it.

Everything below is expressed in that unit, because it is the only one that makes
the two contexts comparable.

## 2. The baseline range is about one target wide, in both

Five fixed random instances span 0.096154 in one context and 0.025391 in the
other. Converted, those are **1.25** and **1.62 recovered targets**.

A range that narrow is describing the arithmetic of a small target set, not the
variability of a population. It cannot carry an error bar — which is the same
conclusion the [pool-budget contract inspection](/labs/pool-budget-contract-inspection/)
reaches from the protocol's own wording, arrived at here from the measurement
resolution instead.

## 3. The most decisive-sounding outcome carries the least evidence

In `CHEMBL3705421` the designed diversity policy recovered **nothing at all**. Its
score is 0.0. That sounds total.

It lost to the worst random instance by **0.12 of a single target**.

In the other context, where it did not score zero, it lost by 1.62 targets — more
than thirteen times the margin. **The outcome that reads as a rout is the one
supported by almost nothing**, because a context with few targets produces
large-looking scores from small counts. A reader who saw only the zero would have
drawn the opposite conclusion about which result was stronger.

## 4. Did it transport, or only repeat?

```bash
python labs/screening_transport/inspect_transport.py check
```

**What makes the repeat worth something:** the two assays share **zero** parent
compounds. The chemistry is disjoint rather than the same molecules measured
twice, and a check requires that.

**What holds it back**, taken from the record's own disclosures rather than
inferred: both replays are retrospective on the same target, under one frozen
protocol, with one feature set, and with a penalty informed by an earlier lab. A
repeat under a shared protocol tests the policy in a second chemistry, not in a
second world.

Eleven checks run, covering the derived resolution, the spread in target units,
the losing margins, the disjoint chemistry, the five fixed instances, and the
floored batch sizes that keep both realised budgets below their nominal fifth.

## 5. What this cannot address

**Off-targets.** Both replays are assays on the same target, so nothing here
speaks to selectivity or to activity somewhere unintended. The topic definition
names off-targets and this evidence does not reach them.

**Prospective follow-up.** Both replays are retrospective on completed
measurements.

The command prints both limits rather than leaving them to be noticed, which is
the same discipline as the
[property decision lab](/labs/property-decision-threshold/) stating that it
cannot populate its abstention band.

## Independent exercise

A colleague reports: "We tested our diversity selector against random on two
independent assays. It lost on both. We're dropping diversity selection from the
platform."

Say what "independent" is doing in that sentence, and what you would need before
agreeing to a platform-level decision.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer — two senses of independent, and the decision scope</summary>

**"Independent" is carrying two claims at once.** In one sense the assays are
independent because they share no compounds, which is true here and is what makes
a repeat informative at all. In the other sense they would be independent
evidence about the selector, which they are not: one target, one frozen protocol,
one feature set, one penalty chosen with knowledge of an earlier lab. The
sentence borrows the credibility of the first sense to support the second.

**"Lost on both" hides how much it lost by.** Converted into recovered targets,
one loss is 1.62 and the other is 0.12. The second is an eighth of a single
target, in a context where the entire baseline spread is 1.25 targets. Two losses
of very different evidential weight are being counted as two.

**The measurement may not resolve the effect.** With 13 targets, the smallest
observable difference is 0.077 in score units. A selector could be meaningfully
better or worse and this context would not see it. That is a statement about the
assay, not about the selector.

**"Platform" is the wrong scope.** The evidence covers one target family, one
protocol and two retrospective replays. A platform decision applies to every
future target, every future protocol and prospective use, none of which is
represented.

**What you would need.** A target where the selector is expected to help and the
target count is large enough to resolve the claimed effect; a protocol not
inherited from the arm being tested; and a prospective round, because both of
these are replays of measurements that already existed. Short of that, the
defensible decision is about this protocol on this target, which is what the
completed record actually supports and what its own interpretation says.

</details>

## What this does not do

This route does not run a screen, acquire a compound, or establish that any
policy is better. It reads two completed retrospective replays and reports what
they can and cannot support. Its authority is exactly the authority of those
artifacts, which carry their own protocol and review date.

The replay itself is the
[pool-budget campaign](/labs/pool-budget-campaign/), and its protocol is read by
the [pool-budget contract inspection](/labs/pool-budget-contract-inspection/).
For the surrounding argument see
[acquisition, delays and stopping](/learn/active-learning/acquisition-delays-and-stopping/)
and
[screening prevalence and transfer](/learn/dti-screening/screening-prevalence-and-transfer/).
