This lab turns a representation choice into an auditable scientific comparison.
The same molecules, endpoint, split, linear model family, regularisation grid,
and metrics are held fixed. Only the input representation changes. The public
evidence now includes completed primary and replication evaluations. The
development snapshot is retained separately from these outcomes.

## Learning objectives

After completing the lab, you should be able to:

1. translate an assay API record into an explicit endpoint and entity contract;
2. explain why rows, parent molecules, and scaffolds are different units;
3. fit descriptor preprocessing without leaking validation or held-out data;
4. compare fixed descriptors and fingerprints under a matched Ridge protocol;
5. separate development evidence from primary evaluation and assay-level
   replication;
6. recognize duplicate-label noise and a hyperparameter selected at a frozen
   grid boundary.

Recommended prerequisites are the lessons on [data contracts](/learn/data-governance/data-contracts/),
[scientific machine learning](/learn/machine-learning/scientific-computing/), and
[small-molecule representations](/learn/representations/small-molecule-representations/).

## Source and endpoint contract

The source is the official ChEMBL 37 REST API. The release status, assay
metadata, activity filters, and expected counts are checked before any feature
is built. This live endpoint is a time-bounded convenience: the downloader
fails closed after the service advances beyond ChEMBL 37.

| Field             | Frozen value                                                                |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Database          | ChEMBL 37, released 2026-05-01                                              |
| Primary assay     | `CHEMBL5736995`, human EGFR binding, single protein, confidence 9           |
| Replication assay | `CHEMBL3705421`, human wild-type EGFR binding, single protein, confidence 9 |
| Target            | `CHEMBL203`                                                                 |
| Endpoint          | exact IC50, `standard_flag=1`, `potential_duplicate=0`                      |
| Unit              | nM, cross-checked against `pchembl_value`                                   |
| Validity rule     | no `data_validity_comment`; non-null structure and label                    |

The check `pChEMBL = 9 - log10(IC50 in nM)` is applied with the frozen
0.011 pChEMBL tolerance recorded in `protocol.json`. This catches unit or field
inconsistencies before modelling. The two assays share a target, but they remain
different experimental sources. The second is therefore a transport check, not
extra training data.

## Entity and label contract

The API query returned 975 rows for the primary assay. Nine lacked a usable
structure, leaving 966 exact, valid rows. These collapse to 902 parent molecules.
All retained rows already identify the assayed molecule as its parent, so the
record's structure can be used without a second parent-structure lookup.

When a parent has repeated eligible measurements, the frozen label is their
median `pchembl_value`. This rule was declared before development. It is not a
claim that repeated values are interchangeable:

- 57 primary parents have more than one retained measurement;
- their median within-parent pChEMBL range is 0.48;
- the maximum observed range is 3.60.

These disagreements expose a substantial assay-noise and curation limit. They
are retained and summarized rather than deleted after seeing model residuals.

The replication query contributes 173 rows and 170 parent molecules. There are
three duplicated parents and no parent-molecule overlap with the primary assay.

## Frozen representations

Three baselines share the same training and validation molecules.

### Train mean

The mean training label is repeated for every prediction. It checks whether a
representation model beats a target-only constant on the development slice.
Its Spearman correlation is undefined by construction and is stored as `null`
with status `undefined_constant_prediction`.

### Descriptor Ridge

The descriptor vector contains 214 named RDKit 2D descriptors from RDKit
2025.09.6. `FpDensityMorgan1`, `FpDensityMorgan2`, and `FpDensityMorgan3` are
excluded so the descriptor arm does not contain fingerprint-density summaries.

Missing values are imputed with the training-column median. A column missing
for every training molecule receives zero. Imputed columns are centered by the
training mean and divided by the training population standard deviation; a
zero scale is replaced by one. No validation, primary-held-out, or replication
molecule contributes to these statistics.

### Morgan Ridge

The fingerprint is an RDKit binary `ExplicitBitVect` with radius 2 and 2,048
bits. Chirality, bond types, and ring membership are enabled. Count simulation
is disabled. The bit vector has no data-fitted preprocessing.

Both representation models use Ridge with an intercept, `solver="lsqr"`,
`tol=1e-8`, and `max_iter=10000`. The frozen alpha grid is:

```text
0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100, 1000
```

Minimum validation MAE selects alpha; exact ties choose the smaller value. The
selected training-only models are not refit on train plus validation.
The execution v1.1 clarification (2026-09-08) permits one deterministic replay
of the original train-only development fit. The complete reconstructed pretest
must exactly match the saved evidence, including its selected alphas and
validation predictions. Those same model objects are then reused for both
locked evaluations; no new choice is made from locked outcomes. The original
protocol and development summary are preserved, with the clarification in
`labs/chembl_egfr/execution-amendment.json`.

## Scaffold split

The independent grouping unit is a chirality-aware RDKit Bemis-Murcko
scaffold. Ring-free molecules receive the explicit key
`acyclic:<canonical isomeric SMILES>` instead of sharing one empty scaffold.

Scaffold groups are sorted by descending molecule count and then scaffold
string. Whole groups fill train, then validation, then the primary held-out
partition. The nominal targets and realised counts are:

| Partition      | Target count | Realised count | Used for                         |
| -------------- | -----------: | -------------: | -------------------------------- |
| Train          |          631 |            634 | fit preprocessing and all models |
| Validation     |          135 |            136 | select two Ridge alphas          |
| Primary locked |          136 |            132 | completed one-time evaluation    |

The mismatch is expected because a scaffold group is never split to hit an
exact percentage. All 902 parent molecules appear exactly once, and no scaffold
key crosses partitions. The primary assay contains 238 chirality-aware
scaffold groups; the replication assay contains 23.

This construction is a stringent structural grouping rule, not proof that the
held-out molecules represent every real prospective distribution shift.

## Development-only observations

The development pretest ran once against the frozen grid. Values below are for
the validation scaffolds and are retained only to document model selection.

| Baseline         | Selected alpha | Validation MAE | Validation RMSE | Validation Spearman |
| ---------------- | -------------: | -------------: | --------------: | ------------------: |
| Train mean       |              — |       0.508710 |        0.642329 |              `null` |
| Descriptor Ridge |           1000 |       0.446681 |        0.608036 |            0.363916 |
| Morgan Ridge     |              1 |       0.372864 |        0.520720 |            0.649509 |

Morgan Ridge has the lower validation errors in this one frozen development
split. That observation selects the development configuration; it does not
establish superiority on either locked evaluation. The descriptor alpha lies
at the upper edge of the declared grid. Expanding the grid now would be outcome-informed
retuning, so the grid remains unchanged and the boundary choice is a stated
limitation.

## Run the lab

For reproduction in a fresh workspace, synchronize the pinned environment and
download the version-checked source snapshot from the repository root:

```sh
uv sync --group dev
env UV_CACHE_DIR=.cache/uv uv run python labs/chembl_egfr/run_lab.py download
```

Run the development path in that fresh workspace:

```sh
env UV_CACHE_DIR=.cache/uv uv run python labs/chembl_egfr/run_lab.py pretest
```

Do not repeat these commands in the completed author workspace. Its one-time
state prevents another pretest or evaluation. The delivered development and
locked summaries can be inspected without fitting or downloading anything.

For long-term source recovery, use the official versioned
[ChEMBL 37 release directory](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/chembl/ChEMBLdb/releases/chembl_37/).
It retains the `chembl_37_sqlite.tar.gz` database archive alongside the release
README, notes, schema documentation, and licence. Reconstruct the two assay
queries from `protocol.json` and require every frozen count to match before
fitting. The current CLI does not yet automate archive import; do not bypass a
live-release mismatch by silently using a newer ChEMBL version.

The public `development-summary.json` is aggregate-only. Local execution may
create richer artifacts under `labs/chembl_egfr/results/` and cached ChEMBL
responses under `.cache/chembl_egfr/`; neither directory is part of the curated
public artifact.

Read `labs/chembl_egfr/protocol.json` before interpreting output. The explicit
descriptor registry matters because “RDKit descriptors” can change when a
software release adds, removes, or reorders functions.

## Completed evaluation and remaining boundaries

Both the primary scaffold test and assay-level replication are `completed`.
The public `labs/chembl_egfr/locked-summary.json` contains every baseline and
metric from the paired run. It does not replace `development-summary.json`.

The guarded `evaluate-locked` action is intentionally separate from `pretest`.
It evaluated both surfaces in one confirmed invocation without retuning. The
one-time local marker remains in place; do not remove it or repeat the run.

This is a procedural lock, not a claim that public ChEMBL labels are secret.
The development path parses all records to enforce source, entity, count, and
scaffold contracts, but only training and validation records enter fitted-model
or development-metric paths. T15 accessed shared labels before the T05
evaluation. That exposure is disclosed: this result is not an entirely untouched
independent confirmatory test. The split, grid and selected alphas were not
retuned after the exposure.

Keep three facts separate: validation-based selection, primary scaffold
performance, and transport to the second assay. Morgan improves on the primary
mean baseline but fails badly in the replication. Descriptor Ridge is best on
replication MAE. This does not establish a universally superior representation
or a causal explanation for the transfer failure.

To regenerate the aggregate from already completed local results, use
`uv run python labs/chembl_egfr/export_locked_summary.py`. This exports existing
scores only; it does not run an evaluation or reconstruct a model.

## Exercises

1. **Contract trace.** Pick five fields from `protocol.json` and locate the
   exact assertion that enforces each one in the Python implementation.
2. **Grouping audit.** Inspect the saved development split and explain why 70/15/15
   targets become 634/136/132 without calling the difference random error.
3. **Noise audit.** Compare the median and maximum duplicate-parent label
   ranges. State which performance claims this variation weakens.
4. **Representation counterexample.** Construct a pair of molecules for which
   chirality-aware and chirality-blind fingerprints differ in scientific
   meaning. Do not assume the assay must resolve that difference.
5. **Boundary diagnosis.** Explain three possible causes of descriptor Ridge
   selecting alpha 1000. Propose a future preregistered sensitivity analysis
   without modifying this frozen run.
6. **Evaluation memo.** Use the completed scores to compare primary and
   replication MAE. Keep every baseline and both outcomes visible, and state
   how the earlier T15 label access limits the interpretation.

1. One valid five-field trace uses `data.validate_chembl_status` for the release,
   `data._exclusion_reason` for `standard_flag`, and
   `data.normalise_activity_rows` for the nM unit, the 0.011 pChEMBL consistency

   realised counts sum to 902 and preserve group disjointness. A correct answer
   checks both properties and distinguishes nominal targets from actual counts;
   changing membership to obtain exact percentages is not an acceptable repair.
3. The median duplicate-parent range is 0.48 pChEMBL and the maximum is 3.60.
   These are within-parent replicate ranges, not a measured lower bound on MAE

   evaluation data before observing outcomes. It does not expand this run's grid.
6. Morgan has the lowest primary MAE but the highest replication MAE, while
   descriptor Ridge has the lowest replication MAE. A passing memo includes all

## Limitations and failure gates

- One primary assay and one replication assay do not cover EGFR chemistry,
  experimental formats, mutation states, or laboratories comprehensively.
- A pChEMBL consistency check does not remove all assay, curation, or chemical
  identity error.
- Median aggregation hides the distribution of repeated measurements from the
  model while the summary retains their disagreement.
- Chirality-aware scaffolds are an implementation-defined grouping heuristic,
  not a guarantee of prospective novelty.
- The fixed Ridge baselines measure a representation under one linear
  objective. They do not exhaust the attainable value of either representation.
- The selected descriptor hyperparameter is on a grid boundary.
- Validation differences are not uncertainty estimates and do not support a
  general representation ranking.
- Public labels make the one-time lock auditable workflow discipline rather
  than cryptographic blinding.

Stop and investigate if source metadata or frozen counts change, a parent maps
to inconsistent structures, split groups overlap, any learned transform sees a
non-training ID, a prediction is non-finite, or the locked action appears to
have started previously. Do not repair a scientific contract mismatch by
quietly changing expected counts.
