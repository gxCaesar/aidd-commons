## What question does the notebook answer?

The learning question is: _how do simple representations compare when predicting
measured lipophilicity for molecules with scaffolds absent from training?_
The target is the supplied `exp` column, measured logD at pH 7.4.
It is not binding affinity, pChEMBL, logP, solubility, or a clinical endpoint.

The table is a public teaching CSV with `CMPD_CHEMBLID`, `exp`, and `smiles`
columns. It is retrieved rather than redistributed. The teaching-table loader is
documented in [DeepChem 2.8.0](https://github.com/deepchem/deepchem/blob/2.8.0/deepchem/molnet/load_function/lipo_datasets.py).
The [original deposit](https://doi.org/10.6019/CHEMBL3301361) and
[ChEMBL data licence](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/chembl/ChEMBLdb/latest/LICENSE)
are linked in the notebook. Retain the download date with your own report. The notebook is deliberately
an exercise, not a new benchmark or method claim.

## Follow the teaching sequence

1. **Question and target.** State the endpoint, unit (logD), grouping unit
   (scaffold), and the generalization claim before fitting a model.
2. **Data and molecules.** Download the CSV, check required fields and invalid
   structures, inspect the label distribution and selected structures, and keep
   supplied rows visible rather than silently filtering them after seeing errors.
3. **Scaffold split.** Build Bemis–Murcko scaffold keys and allocate complete
   groups to train, validation, and test partitions. Inspect counts and assert
   that an ID, exact structure, or scaffold key does not cross a partition.
4. **Representations.** Contrast a train mean with compact RDKit descriptors and
   a radius-2, 2,048-bit Morgan fingerprint. `MolLogP` is a computed feature in
   the descriptor baseline, not the answer to the logD task.
5. **Train and select.** Fit preprocessing on training molecules only. Select
   the stated Ridge regularisation from validation RMSE only; do not use the
   test labels to choose a representation or hyperparameter.
6. **Plots and errors.** After selection, evaluate once on the held-out split.
   Read predicted-versus-observed and residual plots as diagnostics. Check
   scaffold membership, endpoint values, and near-neighbour similarity without
   retuning against the same test set.
7. **Exercise and report.** Use a separate exploratory copy for changed choices;
   a new folder alone does not make the already observed test independent.
   Report the question, data source/date, split,
   representation, validation selection rule, test metrics, error examples, and
   limitations. Keep a worse-than-constant or negative result.

## How to read a result honestly

Your numbers are local exercise outputs. They are not the repository's frozen
FreeSolv, ChEMBL EGFR, rMD17, or pool-budget results, and they do not establish
general performance beyond this dataset and split. The rows are not known to be
independent assay replicates; the table does not supply batch, replicate, or
experimental-uncertainty fields. A scaffold split is a declared grouping rule,
not proof of prospective chemical novelty or assay transfer.

Do not improve the reported test result by dropping difficult structures,
changing the split after viewing test labels, or selecting a model on the test
plot. If the notebook fails because a public download or a Colab runtime is
unavailable, record that failure and use local Jupyter when appropriate; do not
substitute another dataset while keeping the same claim.
