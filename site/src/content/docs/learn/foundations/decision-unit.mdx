## Learning goals and prerequisites

After this lesson you should be able to write the decision unit of an AIDD claim
as four fields, say which sentence a reported metric is evidence for, and name the
field that a proposed comparison has quietly changed. Read [Start with the AIDD
pipeline](/learn/foundations/getting-started/) and [Read a source and identify its
biological object](/learn/foundations/source-and-entity-literacy/) first.

**Mode: reading existing results.** The worked case reads numbers already
committed in this repository from a completed, frozen evaluation. Nothing is run,
retrained or re-sampled, and no command is needed. The optional inspection route
at the end runs one offline command over the same committed files.

## Four fields before any model

A claim in AI-driven drug discovery is always about something, for some purpose,
under some conditions, and it generalises over some unit. Writing those four
things down is the decision unit.

| Field                | The question it answers                                                                                                                                 | What goes wrong when it stays implicit                                                                                  |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Scientific object    | What does one row denote, and what is the prediction about? A parent molecule, a salt form, an assay well, a protein construct or a patient sample.     | Two rows of one object count as two pieces of evidence, or a result about salts is read as a result about parents.      |
| Intended action      | Which decision will the output change, among which candidates, and at what budget?                                                                      | A metric is optimised that the decision never uses, such as average error when only the top ten compounds will be made. |
| Experimental context | Under which assay, endpoint, units, admission rules, target state and data version does a label mean what it means?                                     | Labels from different contexts are pooled as if they measured one quantity.                                             |
| Unit of inference    | Over which independent unit is the claim meant to generalise? This fixes what must never be shared between fitting and evaluation, and what `n` counts. | A random row split lets near-copies of one unit sit on both sides, and interpolation is reported as generalisation.     |

The selection population belongs with the intended action. It is the set of
candidates in which the action will actually be taken, and it can differ from
every set the model has seen.

The fields are not independent. The unit of inference follows from the intended
action and the context, not from whatever is easiest to split. If the action is to
rank compounds from chemical series nobody has made yet, the unit is the series,
and an evaluation that shares series between fitting and testing answers a
different question.

## Worked case: one model, two sentences

This repository holds a completed and frozen evaluation on two ChEMBL 37 assays of
the human EGFR kinase. It ran on 2026-09-08 on a local Mac CPU with Python 3.11.15,
NumPy 2.4.6, scikit-learn 1.9.0 and RDKit 2025.09.6. Its locked summary is committed
as `labs/chembl_egfr/locked-summary.json` and was read, not rerun, for this lesson.

Six terms first. **pChEMBL** is the negative base-10 logarithm of the molar
potency value, so an IC50 of 1 nM gives 9 and one of 100 nM gives 7, and a higher
value means a more potent compound. A **Morgan fingerprint** here is a 2,048-bit
binary vector marking which circular substructures, out to radius 2, a molecule
contains. The **descriptor** representation is 214 computed whole-molecule
properties from RDKit, with missing values filled by training-set medians and
every property scaled on training molecules only. **Ridge regression** fits a
linear model with a penalty on coefficient size. Each model was fitted on training
molecules only, and its penalty strength was the value in a fixed grid with the
lowest mean absolute error on the validation molecules. A **Bemis–Murcko
scaffold** keeps a molecule's ring systems and the linkers between them and drops
the side chains, so close analogues usually share one. **Spearman rank
correlation** compares the order of predicted and measured values, from −1 to 1,
where 0 means no monotonic association.

**Sentence A.** "For compounds from scaffolds not seen in fitting, measured in
assay CHEMBL5736995, a Morgan-fingerprint ridge model predicts pChEMBL with a lower
mean absolute error than a descriptor ridge model."

| Field                | Value in the frozen contract                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Scientific object    | The standardised ChEMBL parent molecule. The 966 admitted rows describe 902 parents. Repeated measurements on one parent are reduced to their median. 57 parents had repeats, with a median within-parent range of 0.48 and a maximum of 3.60 pChEMBL units, so for some parents the median hides real disagreement. |
| Intended action      | Choose a representation for predicting the pChEMBL values of compounds from unseen scaffolds in this assay.                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| Experimental context | Assay CHEMBL5736995 on EGFR (ChEMBL target CHEMBL203), which ChEMBL classifies as a binding assay and whose description reports a TR-FRET biochemical kinase assay. Exact nanomolar IC50 records only, expressed as pChEMBL.                                                                                         |
| Unit of inference    | The chirality-aware Bemis–Murcko scaffold. The 902 parents fall into 238 scaffolds, assigned whole and largest first to 634 training, 136 validation and 132 locked evaluation parents.                                                                                                                              |

The unit of inference also tells you which compounds the numbers describe. Because
whole scaffolds were filled largest first, the 634 training parents sit in 53
scaffolds, while the 132 locked parents come from 124 scaffolds, 116 of them
represented by a single parent. These counts were taken on 2026-09-11 from the
lab's split-membership record, which stays local because it lists the members of
the locked evaluation. Only the counts are reported here. The locked numbers are
therefore about compounds from small scaffolds nobody fitted on, not about a typical
new analogue of a large series.

On the 132 locked parents:

| Model                        | Mean absolute error (pChEMBL) | Spearman rank correlation |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------: | ------------------------: |
| Training-set mean (constant) |                         0.479 |                 undefined |
| Descriptor ridge             |                         0.469 |                     0.129 |
| Morgan ridge                 |                         0.418 |                     0.505 |

A constant prediction has no ordering, so its rank correlation is undefined rather
than zero. Work the error comparison through from the stored values. Morgan ridge is
0.061 pChEMBL units below the constant baseline, about 13% of the baseline error,
and 0.050 units below descriptor ridge, about 11% of that model's error. Subtracting
the rounded table entries would give `0.469 − 0.418 = 0.051` instead, which is why
a difference is computed before it is rounded.

These numbers are evidence for sentence A, under the caveats the locked summary
records about itself. A separate teaching lab had already accessed labels shared
with this evaluation before it ran, so it is not an untouched confirmation. No
confidence interval or p value was computed. The selected descriptor penalty sits
at the upper edge of its frozen grid.

**Sentence B.** "A Morgan-fingerprint ridge model predicts EGFR potency."

Read the way such a sentence is usually heard, B is a claim about EGFR potency in
general. It keeps the object and the model and widens two fields without saying so.
The context becomes any EGFR assay, and the unit of inference becomes any EGFR
compound. The primary result is one instance consistent with it, and one instance
cannot establish a general claim. The frozen evaluation measured exactly one other
context, assay CHEMBL3705421. It is also an EGFR enzyme assay with the same
assay-format annotation, but it comes from a different document and uses a
different commercial kit. Its 170 parents share no parent with the primary assay
and fall into 23 scaffolds. The fitted models were applied to it unchanged.

| Model                        | Mean absolute error on CHEMBL3705421 | Spearman rank correlation |
| ---------------------------- | -----------------------------------: | ------------------------: |
| Training-set mean (constant) |                                0.898 |                 undefined |
| Descriptor ridge             |                                0.579 |                     0.113 |
| Morgan ridge                 |                                3.132 |                    −0.041 |

The representation that won under sentence A has an error `3.132 / 0.898 ≈ 3.5`
times that of predicting a constant in the one other context tested, and its rank
correlation there is −0.041, essentially no association. Sentence A is supported. Sentence B, as a claim
about EGFR potency in general, is contradicted by the one other context tested, and
nothing in this repository supports anything broader than sentence A. The model
did not change between the two sentences. The decision unit did.

## Change the action, and the unit follows

Keep the object and the context of sentence A and change only the intended action.
In a **constructed** scenario, a team has one large scaffold that is well
represented in its training data and must choose which 15 proposed analogues of it
to make next. The action now ranks new compounds inside one known scaffold. The unit
of inference becomes the new analogue within a represented series, and the evidence
the decision needs is ranking quality within that scaffold, ideally tested forward
in time.

None of the frozen numbers measures that. They pool all 132 locked parents, which
come from scaffolds unseen in fitting, and 116 of those scaffolds hold a single
parent. Only eight locked scaffolds hold two parents each, so ranking within a
scaffold was not reported and could be checked on at most eight pairs here. The
correct reading is not that the model fails this decision. It is that this
evaluation says nothing about it. Writing the decision unit is how you notice that
before the analogues are made.

## Independent exercise

A **constructed** report reads: "Our hERG classifier is accurate across the
portfolio, with AUROC 0.86 on a random 80/20 split of 4,000 records pooled from
three hERG assays." hERG is a cardiac potassium channel, and compounds that block
it are flagged as a safety risk. AUROC is the probability that a randomly chosen
positive is scored above a randomly chosen negative, so 0.5 is chance. The
classifier was trained on those records, and the team's five active chemical series
are all represented in them. The team intends to use it to deprioritise proposed,
not yet synthesised, analogues in these five series.

1. Write the four fields as the report implies them and as the intended action
   requires them.
2. Which field is mismatched, and which is missing?
3. Name one evaluation that would bear on the intended action.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

| Field                | As the report implies                                                                                                                | As the action requires                                                           |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Scientific object    | Records pooled from three assays. How many compounds they cover, and whether one compound appears more than once, is not stated      | One record per parent compound in the decision assay                             |
| Intended action      | Not stated                                                                                                                           | Deprioritise proposed analogues in five named series before synthesis            |
| Experimental context | Three hERG assays pooled. Whether they share an endpoint, and which one the decision relies on, is not stated. No threshold is given | The assay or assays, endpoint and activity threshold the decision will use       |
| Unit of inference    | A random record                                                                                                                      | A new analogue within one of the five represented series, tested forward in time |

The unit of inference is mismatched. The decision scores future analogues of series
the model already knows, so the matching evaluation holds out the most recently
made compounds within those series, a time split, and reports results per series. A
random split over records answers an easier question. Unless records were grouped
by compound and by series before splitting, close analogues and any repeat records
of one compound can sit on both sides, and 0.86 may then largely reflect
interpolation among known compounds. Check the membership before trusting the
number. A
series-held-out split would answer a harder question, which matters only if new
series will be scored.

The experimental context is missing. The report does not say whether the three
assays measure the same endpoint or which of them the decision relies on, and pooled
labels mean one thing only if they do. Without a threshold, nobody can say what a
positive call means. Even a well-matched time split is retrospective evidence about compounds
like the proposed ones, not a measurement of them.

</details>

## What this lesson does not show

The four fields are necessary, not sufficient. Writing them does not make a
scaffold the right unit for every task, does not repair a poorly chosen endpoint,
and does not turn a retrospective evaluation into a prospective one. The worked
case is one frozen evaluation on two assays of one target. Its second assay is a
single context, not a survey of EGFR assays.
