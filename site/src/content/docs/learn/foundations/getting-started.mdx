This canonical English entry introduces the route from biological evidence to
candidate nomination. It does not treat an in-silico score as experimental
validation. The 18-track graph is a navigation aid, not a claim that every
subject already has a full laboratory exercise.

## Foundations

Choose Foundations to establish vocabulary and evidence habits, Research to
inspect scientific claims, or Engineering to work through a reproducible
protocol. Every route returns to the same graph. The [DeepChem
repository](https://github.com/deepchem/deepchem) and the [O3DC benchmark
index](https://o3dc.org/) are starting points, not blanket endorsements of their
linked assets.

## Practical workflow

For every new source, record its producer, canonical URL, version or release
where available, decision unit, and what its evidence actually supports. Use
the [Open Source AI Definition](https://opensource.org/ai/open-source-ai-definition)
only as a framework for questions; it does not certify a model by itself. [One
claim, one decision unit](/learn/foundations/decision-unit/) works one real result
through the four fields of a decision unit.

## Evaluation

Ask whether the proposed metric and split match the intended generalization
unit. Repeated training seeds do not replace independent patients, assays, or
targets. Keep lifecycle, access, licences, and all seven openness dimensions
`unverified` until an adjacent primary source supports them.

## Frontier and open questions

The frontier is not a single model leaderboard. A useful open-development
review separates code, weights, data, recipes, and evaluation artifacts, then
asks what can actually be reproduced under a matched protocol.

## Current limitations

The current release candidate does not provide an installation guide for every package, a
clinical-development curriculum, or a universal definition of a reproducible
AIDD result.

## Core-source path

Read the [FAIR Guiding Principles](https://www.nature.com/articles/sdata201618)
alongside the [SPDX project](https://spdx.dev/), then inspect the claim-level
records in the [Scientific Atlas](/atlas/).
