## Learning goals and prerequisites

You will define developability as a multi-field evidence problem, design a
functional validation contrast, and select candidates without collapsing missing
fields into a favourable score. Read [Antibody and peptide design with negative
selection](/learn/biologics-design/antibody-peptide-design/) first. This lesson
is conceptual and handworked, not a manufacturing process, expression method, or
clinical-development protocol.

## Developability is not a synonym for potency

Developability asks whether a specified construct can be made, handled, and
studied with properties compatible with its intended use. Depending on modality,
fields can include expression yield, identity and integrity, aggregation or
self-association, solubility, stability in a stated condition, non-specific
interaction, and formulation-relevant behavior. A good binding or functional
signal does not fill these fields. Conversely, good physical behavior does not
prove target-specific function.

The relevant evidence unit includes the construct identity, production context,
measurement condition, output unit, and aggregation rule. If a metric reports a
distribution rather than a scalar, preserve the distribution and predeclare the
summary. Complementary developability assays can disagree because they interrogate
different interactions; studies of engineered antibodies explicitly compare
self-interaction, light scattering, and solubility rather than treating one as a
universal oracle [Shan et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30395473/).

## Functional validation asks a causal comparison

Write a functional claim as: “Construct K produces endpoint Y through the
proposed target-dependent route in context C.” The validation panel needs a
positive condition, a matched negative context or construct, and a readout
close enough to the proposed route to make alternatives visible. A target-negative
cell context addresses dependence on target presence; a binding-deficient or
non-targeting construct addresses a construct-specific alternative; an orthogonal
readout addresses detector dependence. They are not interchangeable.

Let `Y_K,C` be an aggregate over declared independent preparations. The primary
contrast might be `Δ_func = mean(Y_K,C+) − mean(Y_control,C+)`, accompanied by
`Δ_neg = mean(Y_K,C−) − mean(Y_control,C−)`. An intended pattern could be a
large `Δ_func` and small `Δ_neg`, but exact thresholds must be registered for a
real decision. Neither contrast proves direct molecular mechanism if an
unmeasured pathway can produce the same endpoint.

## Worked case: a constructed advance table

Everything below is **constructed for teaching**. Before evaluation, a team
declares three gates: apparent soluble fraction at least 0.80 under a stated
screen; negative-panel signal no more than 20 units; and an intended-context
functional contrast at least 30 units with a target-negative contrast no more
than 10 units. These values are not platform standards.

J is the only conditional survivor. It is not “manufacturable” and not
“validated”: soluble fraction comes from one stated screen, and the functional
contrast can still reflect an untested alternative. The next step is a
predeclared independent production or context comparison coupled to an
orthogonal functional measurement. K cannot be advanced by its larger `Δ_func`
without changing the declared rule. L cannot be rescued by averaging 27 with its
other attractive columns.

## Alternatives, limitations, and independent exercise

A Pareto front can retain several candidates for further evidence when gates do
not decide; a weighted score is defensible only after units, directions, weights,
and missingness policy are frozen. Design-model confidence can prioritize a
small panel but must not substitute for observed expression or functional data.
Published physicochemical rules identify relative risk patterns in particular
antibody datasets, not a universal admission rule [Zhang et
al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32453957/).

**Handworked exercise (not runnable or empirical):** With the same constructed
gates, M has soluble fraction `0.83`, negative signal `18`, `Δ_func = 29`, and
`Δ_neg = 6`; N has `0.81`, `16`, `34`, and `13`. Which gate does each fail?
For J, state one independent unit and one orthogonal readout; why are those not
two more technical wells?

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

M fails the intended-context functional gate because `29 < 30`. N fails the
target-negative contrast gate because `13 > 10`. For J, a separately prepared
construct batch or a separately derived relevant cell preparation could be an
independent unit; a second detection principle for the functional endpoint is
an orthogonal readout. Technical wells repeat the same preparation and do not
create either new biological or production variation.

</details>
