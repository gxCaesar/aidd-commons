## Learning goals and prerequisites

After this lesson, you should be able to name the record needed for an
endpoint measurement, distinguish it from a concentration response record,
and explain why neither establishes a clinical outcome. Read
[developability and validation](/learn/biologics-design/developability-and-validation/)
and [the independent unit lesson](/learn/foundations/decision-unit-scope/)
first. The [functional validation audit](/labs/functional-validation-audit/)
is the runnable companion. Its frozen protocol and two cached public tables
provide the counts below.

## Three claims and their records

A binding measurement is a statement about a reported endpoint under a named
assay. The audit requires a ChEMBL binding assay, a Kd or Ki type, a unit and
an explicit relation before it licenses that statement. An equality permits a
point value. A bound permits only a statement about which side of a threshold
the value lies. Other ChEMBL endpoint types may be valid measurements without
being direct binding records.

A functional response statement needs a response record. In this audit, that
means numeric readings at several concentrations together with an assay
description that states the response definition. An endpoint number alone is
not a response series. Conversely, a response series does not supply a direct
binding measurement merely because its row also has an endpoint number.

A clinical outcome statement would need an outcome endpoint, the population
and observation procedure that produced it, and a record linking it to the
claim being made. Neither of the two tables holds a clinical endpoint. The
missing route is a property of these tables, not proof that no such record
exists elsewhere.

## The record rule

The [ChEMBL 37 source lab](/labs/decision-unit-scope/) first applies its
frozen admission rule for nanomolar endpoint rows. The
[PubChem source lab](/labs/assay-endpoint-controls/) retains compound rows
from the declared assays. The audit then assigns a record kind from stated
fields. It does not choose records because they support a preferred conclusion.
The reading was computed on 2026-10-23 from snapshots read on 2026-10-21
with Python 3.11.15 and RDKit 2025.09.6.

## Worked case

Assay 519 has 272 retained compound rows. Each has an IC50 field and a Ki
field, giving 544 endpoint values. It has 272 response-table records because
one compound row remains one record. The 544 values must not be described as
544 independent responses. The assay's numeric concentration series supports
an observation about its stated response. Its Ki column alone does not make
that series a direct binding measurement under this audit's rule, which
requires a ChEMBL binding-assay record. Nothing in either count supplies a
clinical outcome.

The less complete edge case matters too. Assay 691 has 53 retained rows with
MIC values but no numeric concentration series. Of these, 38 state a bound and
15 state no relation. The 38 support only bound endpoint statements. The 15
license no point or bound statement under the rule, despite carrying numbers.
A recorded number and a supported claim are different things.

## Independent exercise

A report finds a ChEMBL row with assay type B, endpoint type Ki, unit nM and
a greater-than relation. It also finds an unrelated PubChem row with a
numeric response series. The report calls the ChEMBL number an exact binding
value, treats the two rows as a matched functional confirmation, and states
that a clinical outcome follows. Identify each unsupported step and name the
record that would be needed to test it.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

The ChEMBL relation licenses a binding bound, not an exact point. A point
claim would require an equality measurement under the same declared type and
unit. The PubChem row licenses only the response observed in its own assay.
Testing a link from binding to response would require paired records for the
same entity under a matched experiment, which these snapshots do not supply.
Testing the clinical statement would require a clinical endpoint record and
its observation procedure. Neither table contains one. The audit therefore
marks binding-to-response transfer, response-to-clinical transfer and clinical
outcome as unmeasurable routes.

</details>

## What this material does not show

This is a count over declared public snapshots, not a random sample of all
assays or patients. It does not test whether a measurement transfers between
assays, whether a response transfers to a clinical setting, or whether any
particular entity has a clinical effect. It fits no predictive model and
reports no reader outcome. Measurement and response are different claims,
and response is not outcome.
