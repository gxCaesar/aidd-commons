## Learning goals and prerequisites

After [Open models for nucleic-acid and peptide design](/learn/biologics-design/nucleic-acid-and-peptide-models/), use these dated catalogue records to distinguish sequence editing, an iterative design protocol, its sequence network and joint sequence–structure generation. Identify what each record establishes about training material, published assets, and permission to use them. This is a reading lesson with a written exercise. None of the methods or assets was run here.

## Comparison contract

Raygun edits an existing protein sequence with insertions, deletions, and substitutions. NISE iterates between sequence proposals and structure and binding predictions for a placed ligand. LASErMPNN supplies ligand-conditioned sequence design and side-chain packing within NISE, although NISE can instead use LigandMPNN for sequence design. RFD4-Proteina jointly generates sequence identities and all-atom structures in an autoencoder's latent space. Their inputs and editable variables differ. The records provide no shared input collection, split, metric, or baseline from which to rank them. Published code or weights establish a route for inspection, not a reproduced result.

## Raygun

The [Raygun repository](https://github.com/rohitsinghlab/raygun) presents template-guided editing of an existing protein. Its fixed-length block representation of ESM-2 embeddings allows simultaneous insertions, deletions, and substitutions, including a change in sequence length without retraining. The [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rohitsinghlab/raygun/main/README.md) associates its 4 named checkpoints with UniRef50 subsets of 8.8 million, 4.4 million, 2.2 million, and 100,000 sequences. It does not explain how those subsets were filtered beyond their counts.

The record establishes inference and training code and documents pretrained model access through `raygun.pretrained` imports. It names no separate checkpoint download URL. The linked [Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/19546626) is for training and benchmark sequences, not a stated checkpoint download. A reader can inspect the published procedures and listed assets, but the record does not establish an evaluation-code entry point or a reproduced comparison. The [LICENSE file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rohitsinghlab/raygun/main/LICENSE) states CC BY-NC 4.0 and prohibits commercial use. The README instead says CC-BY-NC-SA 4.0 and describes a 60-day trial licence for internal evaluation and a separate agreement for continued commercial use. The two first-party statements disagree, so the applicable terms cannot be settled from this record alone.

## NISE

The [NISE repository](https://github.com/polizzilab/NISE) describes an iterative selection-expansion protocol built to optimize a protein sequence and structure around a protein-ligand pose. It pairs LASErMPNN or LigandMPNN for sequence design with Boltz-1x or Boltz-2x for structure and binding evaluation. The [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/polizzilab/NISE/main/README.md) documents inference use and the protocol architecture. It points to a model-access script and a prebuilt execution container, but enumerates no individual weight file or checkpoint download. A container is an execution environment, not evidence of a published checkpoint.

The record does not verify training code, weights, a data recipe, training data, or evaluation code for NISE. Its README names no training corpus or split. It also has no licence statement, and a LICENSE file was not inspected. The record therefore establishes neither the applicable licence nor a reproducible training or evaluation procedure.

## LASErMPNN

The [LASErMPNN repository](https://github.com/polizzilab/LASErMPNN) describes the ligand-conditioned sequence network used by NISE. It proposes sequences for a fixed backbone and placed ligand and also supports side-chain packing. Its [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/polizzilab/LASErMPNN/main/README.md) says the network was trained on protonated structures. It names separate download scripts for a ligand-encoder training dataset and a protonated PDB training dataset, without naming either corpus or giving a record count, filtering procedure, or split size.

The record establishes published inference and training code. The README names `laser_weights_0p1A_nothing_heldout.pt` as the default checkpoint and places other weights in the repository's `model_weights` directory. It supplies no separate checkpoint download URL. These materials let a reader inspect the documented model and training route, but this lesson has not executed them or reproduced an evaluation. The README makes no licence statement, and a LICENSE file was not inspected. Asset availability therefore does not establish usage rights. The unnamed corpora also prevent exact attribution of the published weights to a reconstructable training set.

## RFD4-Proteina: joint generation and the measurement unit

The [model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/RFD4-Proteina) and [repository](https://github.com/RosettaCommons/RFD4-Proteina) describe a latent flow model paired with an autoencoder. The autoencoder connects sequence identities and all-atom coordinates to a continuous representation; the flow model generates in that representation before decoding. This differs from assigning a sequence to LASErMPNN's fixed backbone. A design specification and its fixed context constrain the generated candidate. The candidate still needs computational checks and independent experimental measurement.

The current pair is `rfd4-proteina-flow-release-2026-10-19` with `rfd4-proteina-ae-release-2026-10-19`. Its components must remain compatible. The card's efficiency benchmark used a previous pair on 2026-10-09 and was not repeated on the current pair. Even for that older pair, computational filter passers per GPU-hour measure a pipeline stage, not experimentally confirmed binding or a target-independent success probability. The [data card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/RFD4-Proteina) releases a PDB- and AlphaFold DB-derived subset rather than the full training mixture. Source, model and weights use BSD 3-Clause; added data annotations and packaging use CC BY 4.0, with upstream terms retained. See the [asset audit](/learn/molecular-generation/generative-binder-design/) for the release boundaries.

### Constructed mechanism exercise

A team has a fixed backbone and a placed ligand for task A. For task B it wants sequence identities and all-atom structure to be generated together within a design specification. Which interface matches each request? It then substitutes an incompatible autoencoder into the current RFD4 flow and quotes the older pair's efficiency as a result for the modified system. Identify both failures before any run.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

LASErMPNN's fixed-backbone, ligand-conditioned sequence-design interface matches A; RFD4-Proteina's joint-generation interface matches B. This assignment follows editable variables, not measured superiority. The substituted autoencoder breaks the flow's latent-representation contract. The efficiency citation also refers to a different released pair. A valid evaluation would identify the matched flow and autoencoder, design inputs, search and filter settings, computational denominator and experimental endpoint separately. No result for task B has been measured in this exercise.

</details>

## Worked case

A team wants to use Raygun in a commercial setting and reads the repository's two first-party licence statements. The [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rohitsinghlab/raygun/main/README.md) states CC-BY-NC-SA 4.0 and describes a 60-day trial licence for internal evaluation. The [LICENSE file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rohitsinghlab/raygun/main/LICENSE) states CC BY-NC 4.0 and says the material may not be used commercially. Neither file establishes which terms govern the team's proposed use. The team would need the authors to clarify the applicable licence, the trial's scope, and the terms of any separate commercial agreement before treating the repository as licensed for that use. A catalogue access label cannot resolve a conflict between first-party statements.

## Independent exercise

A colleague sees that NISE can call LASErMPNN and assumes that NISE therefore publishes the same training assets and weights. Use the openness table to identify which NISE fields remain unverified, and explain why the relationship does not change them.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

NISE's training code, weights, data recipe, training data, and evaluation code are unverified in its own record. LASErMPNN has available entries for those fields, but its assets do not become NISE assets merely because NISE can use that network. The NISE README's container and model-access script do not identify an individual NISE checkpoint. LASErMPNN's dataset scripts likewise do not name its training corpora. Each conclusion follows the resource-specific openness fields and README caveats rather than the relationship alone.

## Scope

The original three papers' article texts were not opened, so reported hit rates and affinities are not carried here. The NISE and LASErMPNN READMEs state no licence, and their LICENSE files were not inspected, so neither record has a licence. Neither names its training corpora. Raygun's subset-filtering procedure and the resolution of its licence conflict remain unknown. The RFD4-Proteina addition uses its opened model card, repository and data card, with no paper-only result imported. Its released subset does not establish complete training provenance. Reading an openness cell does not execute an asset, reproduce a model, or establish a common benchmark or measured reader learning.
