## Learning goals and prerequisites

After this lesson, you should be able to complete a biologic record with its
modality, construct, expression system, and intended function. You should also
be able to assign evidence to each of the six objectives named below and reject
a conclusion that substitutes one objective for another.

Read [Biologics and multimodal design](/learn/biologics-design/multimodal-design/),
[Biological representations](/learn/representations/biological-representations/),
and [Pocket states, confidence, and reusable structural evidence](/learn/structure-and-pockets/pocket-states-and-confidence/)
first. They introduce the biological objects and the distinction between a
structural model and an observation.

**Mode: a reading-and-reasoning lesson, with no command to run.** The objects
are approved medicines described in public labels. The exercises produce written
records and interpretations. Expression systems appear only as source-reported
identity fields.

## Part 1: Modality, construct, expression system, and function

**Modality** names a broad kind of therapeutic object. **Construct** specifies
which molecular form that record actually describes. A target name or a disease
name cannot identify a construct. An **intended function** names the action
sought from that object in a stated context. It is separate from evidence that
the action occurs.

The distinctions apply across modalities. A protein record retains the named
protein, domain coverage, and assembly. A peptide record retains its chemical
form. An antibody record retains its format and chain pairing, so a full
antibody and an isolated domain are distinguishable. A nucleic-acid record
retains the oligonucleotide class, chemical form, and source-reported molecular
target. A multimodal record retains each component, their association, and their respective
roles. These categories can overlap. An antibody is also a protein, and a
peptide can carry a non-peptide component. A combined object cannot be identified
by naming only its recognition component.

For the antisense example below, record the RNA target. Other oligonucleotides
can target a protein. The [2011 FDA MACUGEN label, section 12.1](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/Drugsatfda_docs/Label/2011/021756s018lbl.Pdf)
reports that pegaptanib, a pegylated modified oligonucleotide aptamer, binds
extracellular VEGF165 under in vitro conditions. VEGF165 is an isoform of the
protein vascular endothelial growth factor.

Use the following fields to describe an existing public record. They are a
representation of the object, not a molecular design specification.

| Field                         | What belongs in it                                                                                  | Error it prevents                                                           |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Record identity               | Active ingredient, product name, source URL, source section, and date opened                        | Treating a product name or a target name as a complete molecular identity   |
| Modality and construct        | Molecular class, named components, format or assembly, and relevant chemical form                   | Merging a whole medicine with one domain, precursor, or carrier             |
| Expression system and scope   | The source-reported host, with the component to which that statement applies                        | Confusing the source of a component with the tissue where the medicine acts |
| Intended function and context | Source-reported molecular target, partner or substrate, intended action, and the biological setting | Equating recognition with the consequence sought from it                    |
| Evidence status               | Source-reported fact, constructed value, unverified field, or explicitly inapplicable objective     | Filling a missing field from a favourable result elsewhere                  |

An expression host does not establish the final object's folding or function.
If the selected source does not name a host, record `unverified in this source`.
Use `not applicable` only when the field genuinely does not apply and that
judgment has a stated basis. Missing information alone does not establish
inapplicability.

### Worked case: repair a TNF-blocker record

A **constructed** catalogue row reads: “Object: TNF blocker. Modality: antibody.
Construct: human protein. Expression system: human immune cells. Function: TNF
binding.” The row has merged ENBREL and HUMIRA. TNF means tumour necrosis factor.
The task is to recover the objects from their labels.

**IgG1** means immunoglobulin G subclass 1, an antibody subclass. **Fc** means
fragment crystallizable, the tail region formed from paired constant domains
of the antibody's heavy chains. It is distinct from the variable regions that
recognize antigen. These terms follow the [antibody-structure reference](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK27144/).

**Public record facts.** The table reads sections 11 and 12.1 of the
[ENBREL label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a002b40c-097d-47a5-957f-7a7b1807af7f)
and the
[HUMIRA label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a06a9aec-fd5b-4c3e-a81f-eb46bb384100).
These entries are source-reported descriptions, not constructed measurements.

| Field                                             | ENBREL                                                                                                                      | HUMIRA                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Active ingredient                                 | Etanercept                                                                                                                  | Adalimumab                                                                                                                   |
| Modality and construct                            | Protein fusion assembled as a dimer, with the extracellular ligand-binding part of human p75 TNF receptor and human IgG1 Fc | Human IgG1 monoclonal antibody, with human-derived heavy- and light-chain variable regions                                   |
| Expression system                                 | Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) mammalian cells                                                                                 | CHO mammalian cells                                                                                                          |
| Intended action recorded in the mechanism section | Prevent TNF-α and TNF-β from binding cell-surface TNF receptors                                                             | Bind TNF-α and prevent its interaction with cell-surface TNF receptors. The label states no binding or inactivation of TNF-β |

The repair requires separate identities. Etanercept's receptor component and Fc
component must remain visible. Calling it an antibody because it contains Fc
loses the recognition component. Adalimumab requires an antibody-format record.
“Human” in these descriptions does not identify the expression host. The shared
CHO field also does not make the constructs identical.

Finally, replace the vague function “TNF binding” with the action and partner
scope in each row. This is an interpretation of the documented constructs. It
does not compare clinical performance or establish interchangeability. The
label descriptions also do not supply experimental coordinates for either
object.

### Independent exercise: a peptide and an oligonucleotide

Use these **public record facts** to complete the same identity, construct,
expression-system, and intended-function fields for two different medicines.

In the RNA record below, **splicing** is cellular RNA processing that removes
segments called introns and joins retained segments called exons into mature
messenger RNA (mRNA). **Exon inclusion** records whether a named exon remains in
that mature RNA. This terminology follows the [NHGRI intron glossary](https://www.genome.gov/genetics-glossary/Intron).

- The [VICTOZA label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=5a9ef4ea-c76a-4d34-a604-27c5b505f5a4)
  expands **GLP-1** as glucagon-like peptide-1. Sections 11 and 12.1 identify
  liraglutide as an acylated analogue of this natural signalling peptide. Its
  peptide precursor has an expression-system statement naming _Saccharomyces cerevisiae_. The final
  molecule includes a fatty-acid component. Receptor activation promotes insulin
  release when glucose is elevated, and that secretion subsides as glucose
  approaches normal levels.
- The [SPINRAZA label, sections 11 and 12.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=dd70cd5f-b0fc-4ba4-a5ea-89a34778bd94)
  identifies nusinersen as a modified antisense oligonucleotide acting on the
  transcript of **SMN2** (survival of motor neuron 2). SMN2 is a gene that can
  encode **SMN** (survival motor neuron) protein, as described in the
  [MedlinePlus gene summary](https://medlineplus.gov/download/genetics/gene/smn2.pdf).
  The label reports that in vitro assays and transgenic animal models of spinal
  muscular atrophy showed increased exon 7 inclusion and full-length SMN protein
  production. These sections do not state an expression host for nusinersen.

A **constructed** annotation calls the first medicine “unmodified GLP-1 made
by pancreatic cells” and the second “SMN protein expressed in human neurons.”
Write the corrected records. Identify which component the yeast statement
describes. Explain why the increased SMN protein does not name the administered
construct or its expression system. Retain the scope of each functional claim.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

| Field                         | Corrected VICTOZA record                                               | Corrected SPINRAZA record                                                                                                                       |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Identity                      | Liraglutide, VICTOZA, with the linked source sections and opening date | Nusinersen, SPINRAZA, with the linked source sections and opening date                                                                          |
| Modality and construct        | Acylated peptide analogue with its fatty-acid component retained       | Modified antisense oligonucleotide. SMN protein is a downstream product                                                                         |
| Expression system and scope   | _Saccharomyces cerevisiae_ applies to the peptide precursor            | `unverified in this source` for nusinersen                                                                                                      |
| Intended function and context | GLP-1 receptor activation with glucose-dependent insulin secretion     | SMN2 splicing change and increased full-length SMN protein, with the supplied evidence limited to in vitro assays and transgenic disease models |

The first record distinguishes the precursor from the final chemical form.
Its insulin response is not described as independent of glucose. The second
distinguishes the oligonucleotide from the protein whose abundance changes.
Neither the affected tissue nor a downstream protein identifies a host that
expresses the medicine. The missing host statement remains missing. These
source summaries do not establish a clinical effect size.

</details>

## Part 2: Separate the objectives

An **objective** names the property an evaluation is meant to assess. Its
representation and output must match that property. Here the input is an
identified medicine record and explicitly labelled evidence summaries. The
output is a justified claim for each objective. No model is fitted and no
biological candidate is selected.

| Objective               | Question and appropriate output                                                                                                                             | Boundary                                                                                                            |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sequence recovery       | How many evaluated positions exactly match a named reference? Output a match fraction with the reference and evaluated region retained                      | Reference agreement does not establish folding or activity. Unscored positions remain outside the result            |
| Folding                 | Does the identified object adopt the specified conformation in a stated context? Retain experimental conformation evidence or a clearly labelled prediction | A prediction is not an observation of folding. Agreement with one observed conformation is limited to that state    |
| Structural plausibility | Does a coordinate representation satisfy named geometric checks? Retain the checked model, coverage, and geometric errors                                   | Acceptable geometry can belong to the wrong fold. A plausible model need not be the conformation the object adopts  |
| Binding                 | Does the object interact with the named partner under the reported conditions? Retain the interaction evidence or an affinity quantity with its units       | Association alone does not establish substrate conversion or a downstream response                                  |
| Catalysis               | Does the object accelerate the named substrate-to-product transformation? Retain conversion or rate evidence, its normalization, and context                | A binding result supplies no catalytic rate. Catalysis is not an intended objective for every therapeutic mechanism |
| Biological function     | Does the stated endpoint change in the relevant biological context? Retain the endpoint, comparison, direction, and observation unit                        | Molecular, sample-level, and patient outcomes are different endpoints                                               |

For binding, an equilibrium dissociation constant `K_D` has concentration units.
A smaller value represents tighter binding under a matched equilibrium model.
For catalysis, a reported rate needs a unit such as amount per time and a stated
enzyme normalization before comparison. Neither quantity is a probability of
biological function. The existing [recognition lesson](/learn/biologics-design/antibody-peptide-design/)
explains affinity and specificity in more detail.

The folding and plausibility questions are particularly easy to merge. A
geometrically acceptable drawing addresses internal consistency. Evidence that
the molecule occupies that conformation addresses the state of the molecule.
Neither result supplies a binding or functional measurement by itself.

### Worked case: what the dornase alfa record supports

**Public record facts.** The [PULMOZYME label, sections 11 and 12.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=d8c78a7e-ff99-48f3-8952-643ec2ea0f86)
identifies dornase alfa as a human **DNase I** (deoxyribonuclease I), an enzyme
that selectively cleaves DNA, with a CHO expression-system record. It reports
DNA hydrolysis in sputum from cystic fibrosis patients and
reduced sputum viscoelasticity in **preclinical in vitro studies**.
Viscoelasticity describes both viscous and elastic behaviour. The substrate
transformation and the change in sputum properties are distinct observations.

Now consider an entirely **constructed** computational summary attached to that
reference record. It contains `M = 216` exact position matches among `N = 240`
evaluated positions, and `A = 198` acceptable coordinate checks among `T = 200`
checks. The summary, all its counts, and its evaluated subsets are
**constructed**. They are not measurements of dornase alfa or a newly proposed
molecule.

Let `R = M / N` denote sequence recovery and `G = A / T` denote the fraction of
accepted geometric checks. Both are dimensionless and range from 0 to 1.
Higher is better only for their respective reference or check set. `G` is a
teaching definition, not a recognised universal structural score.

Work the **constructed** arithmetic separately:

1. Recovery has `240 − 216 = 24` unmatched evaluated positions.
   `R = 216 / 240 = 0.90`, and `0.90 × 100 = 90%`.
2. Geometry has `200 − 198 = 2` failed checks.
   `G = 198 / 200 = 0.99`, and `0.99 × 100 = 99%`.

Neither denominator counts independent patients or medicines. Recovery concerns
only the declared evaluated positions. Checks on one coordinate representation
do not create independent molecular observations.

The completed objective record combines these distinct evidence types without
averaging them. The judgments about what remains unverified are this lesson's
interpretation of the supplied fields.

| Objective               | Entry for this worked case                                                | Supported conclusion                                                                    |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sequence recovery       | **Constructed:** `R = 0.90`                                               | Reference matches on the evaluated subset only                                          |
| Folding                 | Unverified by the supplied summaries                                      | No comparison with an observed conformation was supplied                                |
| Structural plausibility | **Constructed:** `G = 0.99`                                               | Most of these geometric checks passed. The fold was not established                     |
| Binding                 | No separate affinity estimate in the selected label sections              | Catalytic substrate interaction does not supply a measured `K_D`                        |
| Catalysis               | Source-reported DNA hydrolysis in the stated preclinical in vitro setting | Evidence of the transformation, without a numerical catalytic rate in this summary      |
| Biological function     | Source-reported reduction in sputum viscoelasticity in that setting       | A sample-level physical consequence, not a patient outcome measured by this observation |

For a claim about DNA conversion, the relevant objective is catalysis. For a
claim about sputum properties, it is biological function at the sample level.
The constructed recovery and geometry fractions answer neither claim. Conversely,
the reported enzyme activity does not validate the constructed coordinate
representation. Marking its folding field unverified says what this evidence
package lacks, not that the approved medicine fails to fold.

### Independent exercise: interpret an antibody record

Return to the public HUMIRA record in Part 1. An entirely **constructed**
computational summary of its reference record contains `M = 81`, `N = 90`,
`A = 171`, and `T = 180`, with the meanings defined above. Every count and
evaluated subset in this summary is **constructed**. It supplies no comparison
with an observed conformation, no affinity estimate, and no catalytic rate.
The label remains the source for the recorded binding and receptor-blocking
action.

1. Calculate `R` and `G`, including the unmatched positions and failed checks.
2. Complete all six objective fields. Distinguish a missing measurement from
   an objective that is not part of the documented mechanism.
3. A **constructed** conclusion reads: “Good geometry proves folding and
   catalytic destruction of TNF.” Correct it. Which objective corresponds to
   blocking TNF's receptor interaction, and why would a binding score alone
   not establish that action?

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

The **constructed** recovery calculation is `90 − 81 = 9` unmatched positions,
then `R = 81 / 90 = 0.90`, and `0.90 × 100 = 90%`. The **constructed** geometry
calculation is `180 − 171 = 9` failed checks, then `G = 171 / 180 = 0.95`, and
`0.95 × 100 = 95%`.

| Objective               | Worked answer                                                                                                                   |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sequence recovery       | **Constructed:** `0.90` on this evaluated subset. No functional conclusion follows                                              |
| Folding                 | Unverified by the supplied summary. No observed-conformation comparison was given                                               |
| Structural plausibility | **Constructed:** `0.95` of the declared checks pass. This is not proof of folding                                               |
| Binding                 | Source-reported interaction with TNF-α. No numerical affinity is supplied here                                                  |
| Catalysis               | Not an objective of the documented receptor-blocking mechanism. Record no catalytic-destruction claim and no inferred zero rate |
| Biological function     | The label describes inhibition of the TNF–receptor interaction. That molecular functional action is distinct from binding alone |

The corrected conclusion is that the constructed representation passes most
of its geometric checks. The binding and inhibitory action have separate
support in the label. Receptor blockade belongs to the biological-function
objective here. Binding alone would establish association without showing that
the receptor interaction is prevented. Neither the geometry score nor that
association supplies evidence of catalytic destruction or a clinical effect
size.

</details>

For the additional evidence needed beyond these objective fields, continue to
[Developability and functional validation](/learn/biologics-design/developability-and-validation/).
Those properties require their own records.

The [biologic construct record audit](/labs/biologic-construct-audit/) applies
these four fields to the records of six named medicines, and shows that three
of them give a construct without a production host and one gives a host
without a construct.
