## Foundations

Protein, peptide, antibody, and nucleic-acid design operates on biological
objects whose sequence, structure, expression, stability, specificity, and
function can disagree. A plausible design is not evidence that it expresses,
folds, binds, or acts as intended.

## Practical workflow

Specify the construct, desired function, negative selection conditions,
expression system, and assay panel. Generate a small auditable candidate set,
then use experimental feedback to update the design rule. Sources such as
[ProteinMPNN](https://github.com/dauparas/ProteinMPNN) and
[RFdiffusion](https://github.com/RosettaCommons/RFdiffusion) should be read
alongside their stated input and output assumptions.

## Evaluation

Separate computational plausibility from sequence recovery, expression,
biophysical binding, specificity, cellular function, and in vivo relevance.
Hold out related families or structural contexts when assessing generalization,
and record failures rather than retaining only successful designs.

## Frontier and open questions

[LigandMPNN](https://github.com/dauparas/LigandMPNN) and
[Chroma](https://github.com/generatebio/chroma) broaden design inputs, but do
not erase uncertainty in complexes, antigen state, or downstream function.
The appropriate model depends on the decision and available assay feedback.

## What v0.1 does not yet teach

v0.1 does not prescribe an antibody or therapeutic protein development
program, claim functional validation from structure alone, or rank design
systems by a synthetic aggregate score.

## Core-source path

Begin with [ProteinMPNN](https://github.com/dauparas/ProteinMPNN) and
[RFdiffusion](https://github.com/RosettaCommons/RFdiffusion), then revisit the
[co-folding Atlas](/atlas/open-cofolding-frontier/) when a design decision
depends on a predicted complex.
