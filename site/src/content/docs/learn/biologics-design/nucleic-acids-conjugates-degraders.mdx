## Learning goals and prerequisites

You will compare sequence-directed nucleic-acid interventions with ADC and
degrader modalities, state their productively required steps, and detect the
error of equating uptake or binding with function. Read [Biologics and
multimodal design](/learn/biologics-design/multimodal-design/) first. This
reading and hand-calculation lesson provides no construct, delivery, or
experimental protocol.

## The object differs by modality

An antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) is a sequence and chemistry intended to
recognize an RNA in a biological compartment. Depending on its architecture,
functional consequence can involve RNase H activity, steric blockade, or
splicing modulation. An siRNA similarly requires guide-strand loading and a
productive RNA-induced silencing complex. Sequence complementarity is necessary
but cannot by itself show cell entry, endosomal escape, correct compartment,
on-target RNA change, or phenotype. These mechanistic distinctions are reviewed
with their explicit limits by [Kuijper et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32391605/).

An antibody–drug conjugate (ADC) is not merely an antibody plus payload: target
recognition, cellular uptake, intracellular processing, release or activity of
the payload, and a context-appropriate consequence all form a chain. A
heterobifunctional degrader analogously requires simultaneous productive
engagement of target and ligase, a compatible ternary complex, ubiquitination,
and degradation. A binding measurement to either component alone does not prove
degradation; observed abundance loss does not identify the intended ligase route
without discriminating controls.

For a deliberately simple chain model, if `p1, p2, …, pk` are conditional
success probabilities for successive required steps, then productive fraction is
`P = Π p_j`. This equation assumes the steps are ordered and probabilities are
defined conditionally; it is not a fitted cellular model. Its value is
pedagogical: a high first-step uptake probability cannot compensate for a near
zero escape or release probability. The lowest step can dominate the final
signal.

The required negative comparison must align with the inferred weak link. For
sequence-directed intervention, a matched non-targeting sequence and a
target-rescue or orthogonal RNA readout distinguish broad stress from a
sequence-linked outcome. For an ADC, a target-negative but otherwise matched
context distinguishes target-associated delivery from payload-only effects. For
a degrader, a nonbinding analogue or route-disrupting comparison may test
whether the observed loss requires the proposed induced-proximity path. No one
control establishes all steps.

## Worked case: a constructed delivery bottleneck

All quantities here are **constructed for teaching**. Consider a hypothetical
conjugated RNA-directed candidate in a named cell context. The team records
conditional probabilities: target-associated uptake `p1 = 0.80`, productive
endosomal escape `p2 = 0.25`, and correct-compartment action `p3 = 0.60`.
The simple model gives `P = 0.80 × 0.25 × 0.60 = 0.12`, or 12% productive
fraction under its artificial assumptions.

Candidate B changes only the constructed escape estimate to `0.50`; its
`P = 0.80 × 0.50 × 0.60 = 0.24`. B doubles this model output, even though
uptake was unchanged. The lesson is not that a researcher should report 12% or
24% as empirical truth. It is that “better uptake” would be a poor selection
objective when the evidence indicates escape is limiting. A target-negative
context with the same payload and a matched non-targeting sequence still matter:
the first probes target-associated delivery; the second probes sequence-linked
function. A favourable result in both does not yet establish human benefit.

For a constructed degrader, let a target-binding signal remain constant across
two cell contexts while protein abundance falls only in C1. That pattern is
consistent with context-dependent productive degradation, but also with C1-only
stress or protein turnover. It warrants a route-discriminating control, not a
claim that target binding transported to C2.

## Limitations and independent exercise

The multiplicative toy model hides saturation, parallel routes, variable
stoichiometry, and feedback. A traditional pharmacology or biochemical model may
be more appropriate when its parameters are observed. The practical limitation
is more basic: each modality's requested evidence must follow its mechanism.
Neither target binding, fluorescent uptake, nor a sequence score is a surrogate
for the whole chain.

**Handworked exercise (not runnable or empirical):** Constructed candidate C
has `p1 = 0.70`, `p2 = 0.40`, `p3 = 0.50`; D has `0.90`, `0.20`, `0.50`.
Calculate both `P` values. Which one should focus on escape under this toy
model? Name a discriminating negative comparison for an observed RNA change,
and one conclusion neither calculation supports.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

`P_C = 0.70 × 0.40 × 0.50 = 0.14` and `P_D = 0.90 × 0.20 × 0.50 = 0.09`.
D has the lower escape term and lower constructed output, so escape is its
obvious focus in this toy model. A matched non-targeting sequence or a
target-negative context, chosen to test the named alternative, can help
distinguish a sequence-linked outcome from broad stress. Neither calculation
supports in-vivo delivery, safety, clinical efficacy, or a measured rate.

</details>
