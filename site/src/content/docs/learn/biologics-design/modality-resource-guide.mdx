## Start with the construct, not the database name

Read [antibody and peptide design with negative selection](/learn/biologics-design/antibody-peptide-design/)
first. A biologic is not interchangeable “sequence.” An antibody
question can concern a variable domain, paired chains, format, antigen state,
and discovery history. A peptide question can concern a sequence, an epitope,
presentation, modification, or a functional assay. An oligonucleotide or
degrader question adds delivery, linkage, target, and cellular context. Resource
choice must therefore begin with a statement of the object and the evidence
needed for the next decision.

Write five fields before retrieving anything: modality and construct; input
entity; desired output entity; inclusion and exclusion conditions; and the
claim that the output cannot establish. For example, “find experimentally
determined antibody–antigen structures near this paired variable-region query”
is a structure retrieval task. “Find natural repertoire sequences from human
IgG studies” is a distributional sequence task. Neither establishes binding,
developability, patent freedom, clinical effect, or an optimal design.

SAbDab2 is the structural choice. It collects publicly available experimentally
determined antibody structures and complexes, preserving experimental metadata,
pairings, construct information, IMGT numbering, and antigen annotations. Its
own download documentation warns that broad, unprocessed downloads can contain
low-resolution structures, residue anomalies, and pathogenic antibodies. Define
a resolution, construct, antigen, and leakage policy before calling the
resulting files an ML dataset. Separately, the
[SAbDab2 homepage](https://sabdab.opig.stats.ox.ac.uk/) explicitly provides a
preprocessed AI/ML training release with standard train/test splits through
[Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20083994). Inspect its release and split
definition before reuse: a supplied split does not establish independence for
every new antigen or sequence-generalization question.

Observed Antibody Space (OAS) is the repertoire-distribution choice. It organizes
sequences into study-level data units and exposes donor and experimental context
such as species, chain, isotype, tissue source, disease, and vaccination. OAS
documentation says its filters are non-exclusive and that bulk downloads can
exceed 700 GB. A metadata filter is a scientific cohort definition, not merely
a storage convenience.

Thera-SAbDab is the therapeutic-reference choice. Its searchable object is a
WHO-recognised antibody or single-domain-antibody-derived therapeutic with a
released sequence. It can link a name, format, target, development attribute,
sequence-similarity query, and available structural coverage. It is valuable for
checking a candidate against disclosed therapeutic space, but it cannot establish
that an undisclosed programme does not exist or that a similar sequence is an
infringement. Its scope is explicitly limited to released sequences.

IEDB is an epitope-evidence choice rather than an antibody-structure repository.
It exports curated experimental and submitted data across epitope, antigen,
B-cell, T-cell, MHC ligand, and receptor entities, and states CC BY 4.0 for
database exports. For a peptide or vaccine-related hypothesis, retain the assay
type, organism, response context, experimental versus submitted provenance, and
the exact epitope or antigen relationship. An epitope record does not alone
prove immunogenicity of a new construct in a new population.

This set does not present a primary registry for
oligonucleotide or degrader design. Those modalities need their own construct,
delivery, target, and cellular-context contracts. Do not silently substitute an
antibody sequence or epitope resource and claim coverage of an oligonucleotide
or induced-proximity design problem.

## Constructed case: map a candidate to the right evidence

This example is **constructed for teaching**. A team has paired heavy- and
light-chain variable-region sequences for an antibody intended to recognize
receptor R. They want one reference set for structural plausibility, one for
therapeutic neighbourhood inspection, and one for natural-sequence distribution.
They also wonder whether a peptide epitope reported for R creates an immune-risk
claim.

First use [SAbDab2](/atlas/db-sabdab2/) to find experimentally determined
structures similar to the paired sequence, recording query region, identity
threshold, antibody format, antigen state, and structure quality. This returns
templates and annotations, not a structure of the candidate. Next use
[Thera-SAbDab](/atlas/db-thera-sabdab/) to inspect released therapeutic sequences
near the variable region, keeping its WHO-recognised and released-sequence scope
visible. Use [OAS](/atlas/db-oas/) only for a separately specified natural-
repertoire comparison, such as human heavy-chain sequences from a named tissue
and isotype; never merge it with therapeutic sequences without cohort labels.
Finally, use [IEDB](/atlas/db-iedb/) to inspect the reported peptide epitope,
assay, organism, and response context. That record motivates a bounded
immunological question; it does not establish that this antibody is
immunogenic, binds that epitope, or has a clinical risk.

## Independent resource-selection question

You receive two requests: (A) make a nonredundant training collection of
experimentally determined antibody–antigen complexes; (B) compare CDR-H3
length distributions between vaccinated and unvaccinated human repertoire
studies. Which resource serves each request, which metadata must be retained,
and what would be a category error?

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

For A, start with [SAbDab2](/atlas/db-sabdab2/) and retain the PDB or SAbDab2
identifier, experimental structure provenance, antibody and antigen chains,
construct and antigen type, quality filters, paired-chain status, and the rule
used to remove related structures across the final split. For B, use
[OAS](/atlas/db-oas/) and retain study and data-unit identity, donor linkage
where present, species, chain, isotype, tissue, vaccine field, sequence
processing state, and the definition of CDR-H3 length. A category error is to
use SAbDab2 structures as a representative human repertoire sample, or to call
an OAS distribution evidence that a sequence binds a specified antigen.

</details>

## Source and access notes

The four Atlas entries link official pages opened on 2026-09-09. They distinguish
retrieval and specific asset licences. SAbDab2 states CC BY 4.0 for its data;
[OAS](https://opig.stats.ox.ac.uk/webapps/oas/) also states CC BY 4.0, as does
IEDB for its database exports. These statements do not establish a source-code
licence or rights over every linked third-party asset. Unknown fields remain
unknown. They organize evidence,
not protocols, recommendations, or confidential development maps.
