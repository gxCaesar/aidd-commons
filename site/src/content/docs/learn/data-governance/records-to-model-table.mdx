## What you will learn

After [data contracts](/learn/data-governance/data-contracts/), work through one
small table before choosing a model. You should be able to identify the object
being predicted, distinguish a measurement from an entity, transform units without
changing the endpoint, and explain every row that does not enter training.
Basic logarithms are enough; no installation is needed.

A modelling table is not simply a spreadsheet with empty cells removed. Its row
definition decides what one prediction means. An assay record, a standardized
parent molecule and a molecule–assay pair are different units. Combining them
carelessly changes the question even if the code still runs.

## A complete constructed example

The following records are invented for arithmetic, not exported ChEMBL data.
The exercise stipulates an already reviewed mapping from A-salt to parent A;
it does **not** recommend universal salt stripping or neutralization. The task
is to predict exact IC50 on assay E, aggregated per parent. Assay F has a different
experimental context and is not a technical replicate of E.

| Record  | Parent     | Assay | Relation | IC50 | Unit | Note                        |
| ------- | ---------- | ----- | -------- | ---- | ---- | --------------------------- |
| r1      | A          | E     | =        | 100  | nM   | accepted structure          |
| r2      | A          | E     | =        | 1000 | nM   | independent recorded repeat |
| r3      | B          | E     | =        | 0.2  | µM   | accepted structure          |
| r4      | C          | E     | >        | 10   | µM   | censored readout            |
| r5      | D          | F     | =        | 10   | nM   | different assay             |
| r6      | unresolved | E     | =        | 50   | nM   | invalid structure           |
| r1-copy | A          | E     | =        | 100  | nM   | duplicated export of r1     |

An export duplicate is removed once; it is not an extra experiment. An actual
repeat stays until the declared aggregation step. Invalid structure is a failed
entity mapping, not evidence of no biological activity. Censoring is information
about a bound, not a missing value to replace with the bound itself.

## Units, transformations and aggregation

For a positive exact IC50 measured in mol/L, define
`pIC50 = −log10(IC50 in mol/L)`. For nM values this is `9 − log10(IC50 in nM)`.
A larger pIC50 represents a smaller IC50. This does not make IC50 a universal
binding free energy or a context-free affinity constant. The
[ChEMBL data FAQ](https://chembl.gitbook.io/chembl-interface-documentation/frequently-asked-questions/chembl-data-questions)
explains that pChEMBL combines eligible standardized activity types; our example
deliberately uses only one endpoint and one assay.

Here r1 and r2 become 7 and 6. Record r3 becomes 200 nM, then approximately
6.69897. Aggregate A by the median **after** the log transform: its target is
6.5. This equals the negative log of the geometric midpoint for these two
values, not the negative log of the raw arithmetic median, 550 nM. Those two
operations answer different aggregation questions; neither ordering should be
chosen after inspecting model performance.

Record r4 implies pIC50 **less than 5**, because the negative logarithm reverses
the inequality. Our exact-value regression excludes it and retains the reason.
A censored-likelihood model could use the bound, but that would be a separately
defined task rather than a quiet substitution. Record r5 remains available for
a possible context-shift question, not pooled with assay E by default.

The audit trail is short: 7 raw rows → 1 export duplicate, 1 unresolved structure,
1 other-assay row and 1 censored row excluded → 3 exact records → 2 parent rows.
The final table is A: 6.5 and B: 6.69897. The raw records and exclusion reasons
remain separate from this model-ready view.

## What enters X and y?

`X` contains information available when a future prediction would be made, such
as descriptors computed from a chemical structure. `y` contains the endpoint
to be predicted. The measured IC50, its logarithm, an activity-class annotation
derived from that measurement, and a future assay result cannot also be input
features for this prospective prediction task.

Keep entity ID, assay ID, source record ID and partition membership as metadata,
not blindly as numeric predictors. Fit learned preprocessing on training data
only. A deterministic structure representation is different from a scaler or
feature selector fitted across all rows. The
[scikit-learn leakage examples](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/common_pitfalls.html)
show why even preprocessing can cross the training boundary.

This two-parent table is too small for a meaningful predictive benchmark. The
point is to account for meaning and counts before expanding the dataset. A real
study also needs assay context, permissible use, an entity/group split and enough
independent units. Counting more repeated measurements does not supply them.

## Independent exercise

Another export contains parent Q twice in assay E: exact IC50 of 10 nM and
0.1 µM. It also contains `IC50 > 1 µM` for parent R. Use the same task rules.
What target enters for Q? What inequality follows for R? May the Q records go
into different train and test partitions?

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer — derive values and preserve the unit</summary>

Q becomes pIC50 8 and 7, then median 7.5. R implies pIC50 less than 6 and is
excluded from this exact-value regression, with its bound preserved separately.
Q's records belong to the same parent and cannot straddle an entity-disjoint
split. In a scaffold-disjoint task, Q must also stay with its scaffold group.
A complete answer includes the inequality direction and the partitioning unit,
not only the two transformed numbers.
