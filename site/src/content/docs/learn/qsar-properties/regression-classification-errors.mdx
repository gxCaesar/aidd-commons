## Start from the endpoint, not the estimator

After [baseline training](/learn/machine-learning/baseline-training-validation/),
you can fit a continuous response. Here you will formulate a binary task, explain
how logistic regression learns probabilities, distinguish probability quality
from a thresholded decision, and write an error analysis that does not tune on
the test set. All numerical examples below are constructed.

QSAR connects a specified chemical representation to a specified activity or
property endpoint. Regression might predict hydration free energy or an exact
logarithmic activity readout. Classification might predict a prespecified assay
hit call. Those targets are not interchangeable. Turning a continuous endpoint
into a class requires a scientifically justified rule, treatment of censoring
and ambiguous records, and a threshold chosen before evaluation.

Our student sandbox's `response` and `hit` are separately authored synthetic
targets, not a recoding of the frozen ChEMBL assay. A hydration-energy value
also cannot automatically become an activity class.

## Regression: size and direction of error

Let residual `e = prediction − target`. MAE is the mean of `|e|`; RMSE is the
square root of the mean of `e²`. Both have the response's units. The
[metric definitions](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/model_evaluation.html)
make these different aggregations explicit.

For targets `[1, 5, 9]` and predictions `[2, 4, 12]`, residuals are `[1, −1, 3]`,
MAE is `5/3 ≈ 1.6667`, and RMSE is `sqrt(11/3) ≈ 1.9149`. Their mean signed
error is 1, but that does not mean all errors have size 1. A low mean signed
error can hide cancellation. A high rank correlation can likewise coexist with
a systematic offset.

In the real [FreeSolv lab](/labs/freesolv-honest-baselines/), the endpoint is
hydration free energy in kcal/mol. In the [ChEMBL lab](/labs/chembl-egfr-representation-baselines/),
the endpoint is parent-aggregated pChEMBL from the specified IC50 records. Their
MAE values cannot be compared as if they measured the same scientific task.

## Classification: from a score to a probability

For a standardized feature `z`, logistic regression predicts
`p = sigmoid(b + w z)`, where `sigmoid(a) = 1 / (1 + exp(−a))`.
The model's score can be any real number; the sigmoid maps it between 0 and 1.
That bounded range alone does not establish calibration on new data.

For binary target `h`, the per-record loss is
`−[h log(p) + (1−h) log(1−p)]`. It is small when the recorded class receives
high probability and large when the model is confidently wrong. In our sandbox,
the objective is mean loss plus `alpha * w² / 2`, and the intercept is not
penalized. The derivatives of the mean loss are mean `(p−h)` for `b` and mean
`(p−h)z` for `w`; add `alpha*w` to the slope derivative. Gradient descent
subtracts a learning rate times each derivative. The
[logistic-model documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/linear_model.html)
gives the broader multiclass formulation and regularization conventions.

For labels `[0, 1]` with probabilities `[0.2, 0.8]`, both records assign 0.8 to
the recorded class. Their mean log loss is `−log(0.8) ≈ 0.22314355`.
Predicting `[0.5, 0.5]` gives approximately 0.69314718. These are natural
logarithms, not percentages.

## A probability is not a decision rule

Thresholding at 0.5 means calling positive when `p ≥ 0.5`. The threshold is a
decision setting, not a parameter learned by the formula above. It may be
chosen using validation under a stated cost or sensitivity requirement, but not
retuned on test labels. Different uses can legitimately need different rules.

| Recorded class | Predicted negative  | Predicted positive  |
| -------------- | ------------------- | ------------------- |
| Negative       | true negative (TN)  | false positive (FP) |
| Positive       | false negative (FN) | true positive (TP)  |

Precision is `TP/(TP+FP)` and recall is `TP/(TP+FN)`. A zero denominator means
the quantity is undefined here, represented as `null`, not silently zero.
Accuracy alone is easily misleading: with one positive among 100 records, an
always-negative classifier is 99% accurate and has zero recall. Compare against
the training prevalence, state class counts, and inspect the relevant errors.
ROC AUC, precision–recall summaries and calibration answer additional questions;
they should not be added or selected just because they produce a favorable value.

## Error analysis that informs the next study

Save predictions with entity identity, target, residual or probability, and the
context needed to interpret them. Ask whether large errors cluster by assay,
scaffold, measurement range or distance from the training domain. A subgroup
noticed after test evaluation is exploratory, not a newly confirmed primary
result. Do not erase inconvenient records or change the evaluation metric.

Check a small number of concrete explanations: wrong units, inconsistent assay
meaning, duplicated entities, extrapolation, or representation information loss.
A performance difference does not itself tell you which explanation is true.
For example, an activity cliff is a sharp activity change between structurally
similar compounds under a meaningful comparison; it is not a synonym for any
large prediction residual. Investigate the measurement and representation before
assigning the label.

## Independent exercise

Four recorded classes are `[0, 1, 1, 0]`, and predicted probabilities are
`[0.2, 0.8, 0.4, 0.7]`. At threshold 0.5, compute TP, TN, FP, FN, precision and
recall. A colleague then changes the threshold to 0.3 because it catches both
positives in these test records. What can be reported, and what cannot?

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer — distinguish scoring from retrospective tuning</summary>

There is one of each confusion-matrix entry. Accuracy, precision and recall are
all 0.5 at the declared threshold. At 0.3, TP becomes 2, FP 1, TN 1 and FN 0,
so precision is 2/3 and recall 1. That is a retrospective diagnostic on the same
records, not an independent confirmation of a better decision rule. Retain the
original result. A future rule needs a separately specified selection procedure
and fresh evaluation; these four records do not establish clinical or assay utility.
