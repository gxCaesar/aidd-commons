## Predict a defined property for a defined chemical object

Read [small-molecule representations](/learn/representations/small-molecule-representations/)
and [regression and classification errors](/learn/qsar-properties/regression-classification-errors/).
This lesson connects molecular identity to a QSAR model's intended use. By the
end, you should be able to choose a split for a scientific question, calculate
a similarity diagnostic, and distinguish predictive SAR from a causal explanation.
The examples are constructed; they do not introduce a new empirical result.

Chemical standardization is not cosmetic cleaning. Removing counterions, selecting
a tautomer, assigning a charge state or retaining stereochemistry can change which
objects a model regards as identical. Define a policy in relation to the endpoint.
A neutral parent representation may be useful for a particular comparison but
does not preserve every pH-dependent state or salt-specific measurement.
The [RDKit Cookbook](https://www.rdkit.org/docs/Cookbook.html) gives concrete
operations for manipulating molecular objects. The availability of an operation
does not establish that it preserves your assay's meaning.

Keep the submitted structure, standardized structure, grouping identity and
transformation reason distinguishable. Deduplicate at the correct level before
splitting. Two records that become the same parent should not cross the train/test
boundary in a claim about new parent compounds. Conversely, two stereoisomers
with different measurements should not receive an averaged label merely because
a chirality-blind fingerprint maps them alike.

A mixture without a defined assayed component is not resolved by choosing its
largest fragment. State whether mixtures are in scope, how component proportions
are represented, or why they are excluded. Conflicting repeated endpoints should
retain their context and the prespecified aggregation or exclusion rule; selecting
the most convenient value changes the label. Record an unresolved conflict when
the available source cannot justify a single value.

## Choose a representation that can carry the relevant variation

Descriptors expose chosen summaries such as size and polarity. Fingerprints encode
substructures or environments. A molecular graph retains connectivity and chosen
atom/bond features. A 3D representation adds a specified conformation, not the
entire equilibrium ensemble. Each has a failure mode: a descriptor can collapse
distinct structures, a hashed fingerprint can collide, a graph can omit stereochemical
or charge features, and a single conformer can misrepresent an environment-dependent
property. See the [representation inspector](/labs/molecule-representation-inspector/)
for an existing editable, label-free exercise.

Select candidate representations with training and validation data under the
same endpoint, eligible population, split and selection budget. A larger model
trained on additional labels is not a representation-only ablation. A pretrained
encoder also adds data exposure and a fitting history; disclose that difference.
Begin with a train-mean or relevant identity baseline and a competent descriptor
or fingerprint model. The [existing ChEMBL comparison](/labs/chembl-egfr-representation-baselines/)
is a worked empirical reference, not evidence that one representation wins every
assay. Preserve its actual outcome rather than deriving an expected hierarchy
from model complexity.

## Three splits answer different questions

Construct a table with two compounds per series, measured at two times:

| Compound | Series | Measurement time |
| -------- | ------ | ---------------- |
| A1       | A      | early            |
| A2       | A      | late             |
| B1       | B      | early            |
| B2       | B      | late             |
| C1       | C      | early            |
| C2       | C      | late             |

A row-random split can place close analogues on both sides. That can match a
within-series interpolation question, but it cannot establish transfer to a new
series. A group split holding out C1 and C2 probes the designated unseen series.
A time split training on A1/B1/C1 and testing on A2/B2/C2 probes later measurements,
while retaining series overlap. A scaffold grouping is one operational definition
of related chemistry; its grouping algorithm is not identical to every medicinal
chemist's series definition. The [scikit-learn cross-validation guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/cross_validation.html)
describes group and temporal separation tools, not which scientific question to choose.

For a future new-series question, neither a plain time split nor a plain series
split alone necessarily suffices. Specify the joint eligibility rule and inspect
how many groups remain. Do not advertise an impossible combination of strong
holdout claims when the available data support only one. Repeated measurements
of the same compound or shared assay sources also require their stated grouping.
All learned preprocessing belongs inside the relevant training fold.

## Worked similarity diagnostic

For binary fingerprint bit sets, Tanimoto similarity is the intersection size
divided by the union size. Let query Q have bits `[1,2,3]`. Training compound A
has `[2,3,4]`, and B has `[1,2,3,5]`. Then

```text
T(Q,A) = 2/4 = 0.50
T(Q,B) = 3/4 = 0.75
nearest training similarity = 0.75
```

This is a property of the selected fingerprint, not a universal distance between
chemical mechanisms. A maximum similarity of 0.75 cannot be converted directly
into “75% prediction confidence.” A one-bit change can coincide with a large
activity change, and a similar structure measured in a different assay can have
a different target interpretation.

An applicability-domain diagnostic asks whether the intended prediction is
supported by the training population. Inspect nearest neighbors, descriptor
ranges, series and assay/context support together. Calibrate any warning threshold
on a declared development procedure. For example, a predeclared warning at
similarity below 0.40 would not flag Q above, but passing this warning does not
certify correctness. A query can be structurally familiar and biologically
out of context. Report subgroup error and sample counts where measured; do not
manufacture confidence intervals for a handful of selected favorable neighbors.

## SAR explanations and counterfactuals

In a constructed matched assay, compound M has pActivity 6.0 and its analogue N
has 7.0. If pActivity is `−log10(activity in molar units)` for the same endpoint,
the corresponding activities are 1 µM and 0.1 µM. The numerical difference is
one log unit, or a tenfold concentration ratio in the stated direction. That is
an observed pairwise SAR contrast **within this invented example**, not proof
that the modified group alone causes the difference in every scaffold.

A matched molecular pair narrows structural differences, but assay variation,
solubility, aggregation, binding mode and other context can still affect the
interpretation. An attribution map explains a fitted model's response under a
chosen method; it does not identify a biological mechanism by itself. A model
counterfactual additionally needs a valid structure, feasible intervention and
relevant measurement context. Optimizing an attribution score can produce a
model-favored object outside the domain in which its prediction was useful.

Turn an explanation into a testable hypothesis: specify the local chemical change,
which endpoint should move, what alternative explanation remains, and what
independent measurement would distinguish it. Keep the claim local until new
series and contexts actually support transfer.

## Watch an explanation miss the cause

The [SAR attribution lab](/labs/sar-attribution/) fits a model to data whose
generating rule is stated in the file, so the cause is known rather than
inferred. A confounder correlated with that cause takes the larger share of atom
credit on seven of eight actives, and the matched pair that breaks the
correlation is ranked backwards. It is the check that real activity data never
allows.

## Independent exercises

1. Query bits are `[1,3,5]` and neighbor bits are `[1,2,3,4]`. Calculate Tanimoto
   similarity. Does it equal a probability of prediction success?
2. Which split in the six-compound table directly holds out series C? Which
   directly separates later measurements? Explain why these are different.
3. A chirality-blind representation assigns identical features to two measured
   stereoisomers with different endpoints. Can a deterministic predictor using
   only those features output both labels correctly?
4. A model highlights a substituent and predicts a one-log-unit improvement.
   What is missing before calling this a validated tenfold biological improvement?

<summary>Worked answers — keep representation, task and claim aligned</summary>

The intersection has 2 bits and union 5, so similarity is 0.40, not a success
probability. Holding out C1/C2 separates series C; training on the early rows
and testing on the late rows separates time but leaves all three series shared.
Identical input features force an ordinary deterministic predictor to return
the same output, so it cannot recover both different labels from those features
alone. A predicted change needs compatible endpoint units, a valid realizable
analogue, matched measurement and suitable independent confirmation. A highlighted
substituent is model evidence, not that confirmation.
