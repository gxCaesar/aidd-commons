## Foundations

Design-make-test-analyze loops allocate finite experimental capacity. The
decision unit is a batch of feasible candidates under a stated budget, assay,
and selection rule. Uncertainty is useful only if it changes an experimental
choice and is later checked against outcomes.

## Practical workflow

Predeclare the candidate pool, objective, constraints, batch size, diversity
rule, acquisition function, and stopping condition. Start with random,
diversity-based, or simple property baselines. [BoTorch](https://botorch.org/)
and [Ax](https://ax.dev/) document tools for optimization; they do not choose
the scientific objective for a project.

## Evaluation

Compare policies on identical budgeted rounds and preserve the time order of
experimental feedback. Report calibration, failed syntheses, assay failures,
and the actual number of independent experimental units rather than only the
best observed molecule.

## Frontier and open questions

Multi-objective selection exposes trade-offs between potency, exposure,
synthesis, and novelty. [MolScore](https://github.com/MorganCThomas/MolScore)
can make a scoring specification executable, but the scoring specification
must remain traceable to a decision and assay plan.

## What v0.1 does not yet teach

v0.1 does not supply a live robotic loop, a universal acquisition function, or
a claim that model uncertainty is calibrated without experimental checks.

## Core-source path

Continue with [Experiment pools, budgets, and feasibility](/learn/active-learning/experiment-pools-and-budgets/)
for a runnable synthetic campaign, exact budget arithmetic, and worked exercises.

Then read [Diversity, uncertainty, and experimental feedback](/learn/active-learning/diversity-uncertainty-feedback/)
to distinguish a refitted predictor from a model-guided selection rule.

Begin with the [BoTorch documentation](https://botorch.org/) and
[Ax documentation](https://ax.dev/), then write a small selection contract
before connecting a model to a laboratory queue.
