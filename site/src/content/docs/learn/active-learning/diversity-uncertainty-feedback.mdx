## Follow the information, not the method name

The current MaxMin policy uses Morgan fingerprints and Tanimoto distance. It
chooses the remaining candidate whose nearest reference molecule is farthest
away, where references include the initial molecules and every earlier pick.
References update after each pick, including within a batch. Labels and fitted
predictions are not inputs. The lab uses an explicit greedy loop with its own
declared ties; the [RDKit MaxMin API](https://www.rdkit.org/docs/source/rdkit.SimDivFilters.rdSimDivPickers.html)
is a related diversity-picker reference, not a promise of identical defaults.

The real replay has a predictor, but its information goes to evaluation:

1. Build the complete MaxMin and fixed random acquisition orders from structures
   and the declared partitions, without providing labels to either selector.
2. At checkpoint zero, fit Ridge on initial labels. At each later checkpoint,
   fit on initial labels plus that policy's acquisitions through that checkpoint.
   Predict the fixed evaluation set each time, keeping Ridge `alpha=1` unchanged.
3. Freeze every policy's checkpoint predictions before scoring. Only then may
   the scorer read evaluation labels for MAE and original-pool labels for
   top-decile recovery. Neither score changes future choices.

Labels therefore update the predictor, but never the acquisition order. The
artificial-score simulator is simpler still: it fits no predictor. Do not
transfer a feature of the real replay to that simulator.

## Case 1: a refitted predictor is not a feedback selector

> “Ridge is fitted after every batch, so this is uncertainty-driven active
> learning. The next batch adapts to the activity just observed.”

Name the missing connection. Could the current acquisition order be calculated
before seeing any activity values? What information changes within a MaxMin
batch, and what does not?

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Case 1 — worked answer</summary>

The missing connection is from observed labels, through the fitted predictor,
to the next acquisition decision. The complete current order can be calculated
without activity values. Adding acquired structures changes the geometric
reference set; it does not make MaxMin sensitive to their measured activity.

The real replay's predictor learns from initial and acquired labels and is
assessed separately. Its changing MAE does not demonstrate model-guided
selection. A defensible description is “label-blind diversity acquisition with
checkpoint-wise predictive assessment.” This is not evidence of a calibrated
uncertainty policy or Bayesian optimization.

</details>

## Case 2: distance, uncertainty, and utility differ

> “The most distant molecule is the most uncertain. The most uncertain molecule
> is the most potent, so MaxMin optimizes the assay objective.”

Separate structural distance, predictive uncertainty and expected experimental
value. Would the same acquisition objective serve both improving predictions
over a pool and finding its highest-activity entries?

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Case 2 — worked answer</summary>

A fingerprint distance measures dissimilarity under a representation. It is
neither a calibrated predictive probability nor a potency estimate. This lab
does not estimate uncertainty for its Ridge predictions. Even a model-supplied
uncertainty estimate would need an explicit interpretation and assessment;
high uncertainty alone does not imply high activity or a feasible experiment.

Reducing predictive error and recovering high-activity entries are different
objectives. An acquisition rule must express which decision matters. For
contrast, the [BoTorch getting-started example](https://botorch.org/#get-started)
fits a Gaussian process and passes it, with the best observed value, to an
expected-improvement acquisition function. Its suggested candidate depends on
the fitted model. That example does not establish calibration, feasibility or
superiority on this molecular pool, and expected improvement is not a rule
to pick the most uncertain candidate alone.

The completed MaxMin recovery AUC is **0.05078125** in the primary assay and
**0** in the separate assay, below every fixed random instance in each. Optimizing
fingerprint diversity did not deliver better target recovery in these comparisons.
The separate pool has only one scaffold group, so its scaffold fraction cannot
distinguish policies. These results do not prove that random selection is
universally best. Keep all five random instances; they are not independent assays.

</details>

## Case 3: which label change may change which output?

> “If the selector is label-blind, changing any labels must leave all its
> reported scores and predictions unchanged.”

Hold structures, identities, partitions, seeds, ties and budget fixed. Consider
two thought experiments: change only evaluation labels, or permute labels only
within the original acquisition pool. Which outputs must remain unchanged?
Which may change? Do not rerun the completed campaign to answer.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Case 3 — worked answer</summary>

Changing only evaluation labels must leave acquisition orders and all frozen
predictions unchanged: those labels are never fitted. Recovery also stays
unchanged because its targets are defined in the pool, not the evaluation set.
Evaluation MAE may change because its comparison targets change.

Permuting pool labels must also leave both label-blind policies' acquisition
orders unchanged. But the top-decile target identities can change, and therefore
recovery may change. The checkpoint-zero predictor remains unchanged because
initial labels are held fixed. Later predictions and MAE may change when the
permutation changes labels of acquired parents that enter fitting.

“May change” is not “must change”: a permutation or numerical coincidence can
leave an output equal. A changed acquisition order under these fixed inputs
would contradict the label-blind contract. Unchanged picks alone do not prove
that fitting or scoring respects its own access boundary. The existing engine
tests check evaluation-label isolation using a controlled fixture; this lesson
does not claim a new mutation experiment on the real assays.

</details>

## Case 4: replay feedback is not laboratory feedback

> “Replace MaxMin with an uncertainty score, read the current batch's final
> activities while filling it, and use evaluation MAE to pick the next rule.
> The retrospective winner then proves prospective discovery.”

Identify the timing violation and the evaluation violation. What would a future
batch study need to specify before any new outcomes were observed?

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Case 4 — worked answer</summary>

For a batch whose outcomes arrive together, none of those actual outcomes is
available while filling that batch. A model may account for pending candidates
under a declared method, but model-generated assumptions are not measurements.
Using final batch labels early changes the information available to the policy.
Using evaluation MAE to select the next rule turns evaluation into development.

A future study must declare its target and objective, eligible pool, initial
labels, representation, surrogate and uncertainty interpretation, acquisition
rule, batch size, outcome availability, refitting schedule and stopping rule.
It also needs a separate development and evaluation plan, matched random and
diversity baselines, and a budget accounting for failures and delays. Historical
successful measurements do not estimate future synthesis or assay failure rates.

T15 accessed labels shared with T05 before T05 evaluation, and its fixed Ridge
setting reuses T05 development selection. The replay is neither untouched T05 corroboration nor
prospective discovery. Designing a future protocol does not authorize tuning
on these exposed outcomes and then calling them a new independent evaluation.

</details>

## Write an information-access card

For the current replay, write one sentence each for the selector's inputs, the
predictor's training labels, the scorer's labels and the time each becomes
available. Then describe the missing model-to-selection connection for a
hypothetical model-based policy. Mark that policy **not run** and leave unknown
costs, failure rates and uncertainty performance unknown.

A complete answer distinguishes structure updates from outcome feedback,
refitting from acquisition, prediction assessment from target recovery, and
retrospective evidence from prospective validation. It names an objective
before suggesting an acquisition rule and keeps evaluation outcomes outside
selection. It must not promise that uncertainty or a new model would reverse
the observed negative result.
