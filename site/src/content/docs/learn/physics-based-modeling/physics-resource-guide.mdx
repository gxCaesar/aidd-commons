## Learning goals and prerequisites

You will choose a physical-modelling tool by the quantity it can produce,
distinguish parameter assignment from simulation and analysis, and identify
when an apparently finished calculation is missing the requested endpoint.
Read [Force fields and sampling](/learn/physics-based-modeling/force-fields-and-sampling/)
and [Free-energy cycles and convergence](/learn/physics-based-modeling/free-energy-cycles-and-convergence/)
first. This guide contains reading and hand calculations, not an executed
docking, dynamics, free-energy or quantum-chemistry workflow.

## Start with the requested output

A three-dimensional structure does not specify a physical calculation. Before
choosing software, state the molecular identity, protein construct and state,
protonation/tautomer assumptions, solvent/environment and requested quantity.
“Which pose fits this pocket?” differs from “What is the binding-free-energy
difference between these ligands?” A trajectory can inform the latter workflow
without being that endpoint itself.

| Requested object                          | Resource                                             | Inputs and output                                                                                                    | Main boundary                                                                                         |
| ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Docked pose hypotheses                    | [AutoDock Vina](/atlas/tool-autodock-vina/)          | Prepared receptor/ligand files, search region and scoring configuration → poses and docking scores                   | Score identity and preparation belong to the result.                                                  |
| A parameterized molecular system          | [OpenFF Toolkit](/atlas/tool-openff-toolkit/)        | Molecular topology, a SMIRNOFF force-field definition and charge choices → assigned parameters or an OpenMM `System` | Parameter assignment is not simulation or validation of chemical coverage.                            |
| Dynamics of a specified system            | [OpenMM](/atlas/tool-openmm/)                        | System, topology, initial positions, integrator and simulation settings → configured trajectory and state outputs    | A completed trajectory does not establish sufficient sampling.                                        |
| Measurements from an existing trajectory  | [MDAnalysis](/atlas/tool-mdanalysis/) or [MDTraj](/atlas/tool-mdtraj/) | Matching topology and trajectory → atom selections and trajectory-derived measurements                          | Atom order, time, units and selections must match.                                                    |
| An alchemical free-energy endpoint        | [OpenFE](/atlas/tool-openfe/)                        | Chemical systems, transformation/protocol and settings → free-energy estimates and diagnostics                       | The requested endpoint may require more than one simulation leg.                                      |
| A specified electronic-structure quantity | [Psi4](/atlas/tool-psi4/) or [xTB](/atlas/tool-xtb/) | Geometry, electronic state and method settings → energy, geometry or other requested properties                      | Method level and environment define the answer; a geometry optimization is not a binding free energy. |

These are complementary roles, not a ranking of software quality. OpenFF's
[ForceField API](https://docs.openforcefield.org/projects/toolkit/en/stable/api/generated/openff.toolkit.typing.engines.smirnoff.ForceField.html)
documents creating an OpenMM system from supported molecular topology. It does
not imply that one small-molecule force field parameterizes every protein,
metal or cofactor in a complex. Resolve component coverage before simulation.

## Three boundaries to inspect in the official examples

The [Vina basic tutorial](https://autodock-vina.readthedocs.io/en/latest/docking_basic.html)
explicitly warns that AutoDock4 and Vina scores are not comparable. It also
separates ligand preparation, search configuration and output conversion.
A common energy unit does not make two scoring functions the same estimator.

The [OpenMM simulation example](https://docs.openmm.org/latest/userguide/application/02_running_sims.html)
separates `System`, `Integrator`, starting positions and output reporters.
[MDAnalysis Universe](https://userguide.mdanalysis.org/stable/universe.html)
then reads existing topology/trajectory information; trajectory coordinates
must follow the topology's atom order. A plausible contact plot from a
mismatched atom ordering is not rescued by smooth-looking curves.

OpenFE's [relative hybrid-topology protocol](https://docs.openfree.energy/en/stable/guide/protocols/relativehybridtopology.html)
runs one thermodynamic-cycle leg per protocol instance. Binding comparisons
need both complex and solvent legs. Its [absolute binding protocol](https://docs.openfree.energy/en/stable/guide/protocols/absolutebinding.html)
instead includes both legs in one protocol graph, with orientational restraints
and their release treatment. Check the selected protocol's output contract;
do not assume that every file labelled `Delta G` is the final binding quantity.
The relative-protocol documentation also flags possible systematic errors when
dummy-group contributions do not cancel. Read such protocol-specific limitations
before adapting the [OpenFE tutorials](/atlas/tutorial-openfe/).

## Constructed case: what can the current files decide?

A team supplies the following **invented teaching packet**:

- ligand A: Vina score `-8.2 kcal/mol`;
- ligand B: AutoDock4 score `-9.1 kcal/mol`;
- one trajectory with matching topology, run for `50,000` steps at `2 fs`,
  saved every `1,000` steps, with no initial-frame output;
- one completed OpenFE relative-protocol result for the complex leg only.

The request is “rank A and B by binding affinity.” None of the supplied objects
settles that request. The docking scores use different functions. The trajectory
has `50,000 × 2 fs = 100,000 fs = 100 ps = 0.1 ns` of simulated time and
`50,000 / 1,000 = 50` saved frames. Fifty frames are not 50 independent binding
experiments, and duration alone does not establish convergence. The complex-leg
result is missing the solvent leg needed for the relative binding endpoint.

The useful next output is therefore a corrected interpretation, not a ranking.
Keep the two docking scores separate; identify preparation and scoring parity
needed for a pose comparison. Use MDAnalysis only for a named trajectory
observable with units, atom selection and sampling limits. For a binding-free-
energy question, inspect both leg definitions, their direction and diagnostics
before combining results. This packet does not authorize a new simulation and
does not imply that doing every step in sequence is always necessary.

## When the question is electronic rather than configurational

For a selected geometry, [Psi4's input guide](https://psicode.org/psi4manual/master/psithoninput.html)
distinguishes the method, basis and calculation driver; `energy()` returns the
requested total energy in hartree. The [xTB manual](https://github.com/grimme-lab/xtb/blob/main/man/xtb.1.adoc)
documents its approximate methods and inputs such as charge and unpaired
electrons. Neither software name specifies a universal “QM accuracy.”
If the question concerns a reactive site in a protein environment, continue to
[QM/MM boundaries](/learn/physics-based-modeling/qm-mm-and-reactive-events/)
to define the environment and coupling. Do not substitute an isolated-molecule
optimized energy for a sampled protein free-energy barrier.

## Independent exercise

A second **constructed packet** has `200,000` steps at `2 fs`, coordinates
saved every `5,000` steps and no initial-frame output. Its ligand-heavy-atom
RMSD stays near its starting value. The analyst reports “40 independent
replicates prove binding.” Calculate the duration and frame count, correct the
claim, and identify the tool category needed to inspect the stated observable.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

The duration is `200,000 × 2 fs = 400 ps = 0.4 ns`; there are
`200,000 / 5,000 = 40` saved frames, not 40 independent replicates. RMSD is a
trajectory-derived structural observable, so MDAnalysis is an appropriate
analysis resource once topology, atom correspondence, alignment/reference and
units are fixed. Staying near a starting pose under a particular simulation
does not establish binding, an equilibrium affinity, or adequate exploration of
alternative states. The statement should describe the observed RMSD and its
simulation scope, without a binding conclusion.

</details>
