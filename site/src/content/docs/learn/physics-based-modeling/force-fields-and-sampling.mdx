## Learning goals and prerequisites

You will name the terms and scope of a classical force field, distinguish solvent
and long-range-interaction choices from a coordinate file, and estimate why saved
trajectory frames are not automatically independent observations. Read [Physical
model and system state](/learn/physics-based-modeling/physical-model-system-state/)
and [Docking through FEP](/learn/physics-based-modeling/docking-to-fep/) first.
This is a browser and hand-calculation lesson. It does not prescribe a production
simulation, validate a parameter set, or report a molecular-dynamics result.

## A force field is an approximate energy model

For coordinates `x`, a common fixed-charge molecular-mechanics model can be
written schematically as

`U(x) = sum_bonds k_b(r-r0)^2 + sum_angles k_theta(theta-theta0)^2 + sum_torsions V_n[1+cos(n phi-delta)] + sum_i<j [4 epsilon_ij((sigma_ij/r_ij)^12 - (sigma_ij/r_ij)^6) + q_i q_j/(4 pi epsilon_0 r_ij)]`.

The first terms restrain bonded geometry and torsions. The final sum represents
short-range van der Waals and electrostatic interactions in one common form.
The equation is a teaching sketch, not a universal functional form or a list of
parameters for any molecule. Its parameters, atom types, combination rules,
charge model, nonbonded treatment, and constraints define the model together.
Changing one component can change the effective physical assumptions.

Classical force fields are designed to approximate a named class of molecular
interactions. They do not generally describe arbitrary bond breaking, charge
transfer, electronic excitation, or a protonation state that has not been
specified. A low potential energy is therefore not a general certificate of
chemical correctness, and agreement within one force field is not independent
confirmation of a structural hypothesis.

## Solvent and boundary conditions are part of the system

An explicit-solvent model represents water and ions as particles; an implicit
model represents some solvent effects through a reduced description. Neither is
“the same protein in water” unless the full model and target question are
declared. With explicit solvent, water model and ion parameters must be
compatible with the selected force field. Periodic boundary conditions, the box,
a direct-space cutoff, and a long-range method such as PME also alter what is
being evaluated.

The OpenMM guide illustrates a system built from topology, a force field and a
water model, using PME, a `1 nm` direct-space cutoff, hydrogen-bond constraints,
and a Langevin integrator at `300 K`
[OpenMM running simulations](https://docs.openmm.org/latest/userguide/application/02_running_sims.html).
Those values are an implementation example, not defaults that transfer to every
scientific question. The same guide makes visible why a trajectory cannot be
understood from coordinates alone: topology, positions, force-field files,
nonbonded method, constraints, integrator, temperature, and reporters each
contribute to its meaning.

| Item            | A decision-relevant record                                                  | A tempting but invalid shortcut           |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| molecular state | construct, chains, ligands, protonation and ions                            | “the target”                              |
| energy model    | force-field and solvent-model identities, parameter source                  | “physics-based”                           |
| boundary        | box, periodicity, cutoff and long-range method                              | a screenshot of coordinates               |
| dynamics        | ensemble target, integrator, step size, thermostat/barostat and constraints | frame count alone                         |
| analysis        | observable, burn-in rule, correlation diagnostic and independent unit       | treating every saved frame as a replicate |

## Sampling is not merely generating many frames

For a canonical ensemble, the ideal equilibrium coordinate distribution is
`p(x) = exp(-beta U(x))/Z`, where `beta = 1/(k_B T)` and `Z` is the normalizing
constant. An integrator and thermostat are numerical means of drawing an
approximation to a declared ensemble; they do not demonstrate that equilibrium
was reached. Initialization, equilibration, slow transitions, finite box effects,
and model error can all dominate an apparently long trajectory.

Adjacent frames in dynamics are correlated because the system evolves
continuously. If a distance or pocket volume remains high for hundreds of saved
frames, those frames provide repeated measurements of one slowly changing state,
not hundreds of independent molecules. A useful report names the observable,
the time origin or discarded burn-in, the correlation diagnostic, and the unit
over which uncertainty is calculated. Independent starting systems or validated
replicas can add evidence; repeated output from one state initialization cannot
silently become a new biological sample size.

The OpenMM theory guide describes the library as a toolkit for molecular
simulation rather than a scientific validation protocol
[OpenMM theory introduction](https://docs.openmm.org/latest/userguide/theory/01_introduction.html).
That distinction matters: executable choices can be precise while the answer to
the molecular question remains conditional on the model and sampling.

## Worked case: ten thousand frames are not ten thousand replicates

All values in this case are **constructed for teaching**. A trajectory saves
`N = 10,000` frames at `Delta_t_save = 1 ps`, so its saved span is approximately
`T = 10 ns`.
For one declared pocket-distance observable, a diagnostic estimates integrated
autocorrelation time `tau_int = 200 ps`. A rough effective-count approximation
is `N_eff approx T/(2 tau_int) = 10,000 ps/(400 ps) = 25`.

The right inference is not that there are exactly 25 independent molecular
experiments. It is that a statistic treating `N = 10,000` rows as independent
will severely overstate precision under this constructed correlation model.
The team should report the observable used to estimate `tau_int`, the sampling
interval, the discarded portion if any, and the fact that the approximation is
diagnostic rather than a guarantee of equilibrium.

Suppose the same team also compares two stated parameter bundles, `F_A + W_A`
and `F_B + W_B`, where `F` names a biomolecular force field and `W` its intended
water/ion model. It may compare them as two model assumptions if the system,
observable, and sampling rule are held explicit. It should not claim that
`F_A + W_B` is a neutral interpolation simply because coordinates load. Pairing
is part of a parameterization and validation question, not a formatting choice.

## Limits and independent exercise

Force fields can be productive for a declared state and observable while being
wrong for another state or transition. Sampling diagnostics can miss rare events
or shared initialization bias. More frames reduce neither a mismatched endpoint
nor an undeclared protonation assumption. Before comparing trajectories, match
their system definition, energy model, ensemble, analysis window, and independent
unit.

**Handworked exercise (not runnable or empirical):** A constructed trajectory
saves one frame per `2 ps` for `5 ns`, and a named observable has
`tau_int = 100 ps`. Use `N_eff approx T/(2 tau_int)` to obtain the rough count.
Then name one record that is required to interpret that count but is not a
coordinate frame, and one claim the count cannot support by itself.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

`T = 5,000 ps`, so `N_eff approx 5,000/(2 x 100) = 25`. The save interval gives
`2,500` stored frames, but it does not change the rough effective count under
the stated `tau_int`. Required records include the observable definition,
force-field and solvent identities, temperature/ensemble, initialization and
burn-in rule, or the correlation diagnostic. The count alone cannot show that
the trajectory equilibrated, that a pocket is experimentally populated, or that
one parameter bundle is physically correct.

</details>
