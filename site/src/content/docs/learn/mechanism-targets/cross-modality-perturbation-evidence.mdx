## Learning goals and prerequisites

Read [From perturbation to target validation](/learn/mechanism-targets/perturbation-to-target-validation/)
first. That lesson built a mechanism claim out of an object, a contrast and a losing
alternative, and it warned that a score is evidence about that score rather than a
target-validation certificate. This lesson takes one step further and asks what would
have to be true of the measurement itself before a perturbational readout can be read
as mechanism support at all.

After this lesson you should be able to:

1. state what agreement between two readouts of the same perturbation does and does not
   establish about mechanism.
2. explain why a random split over perturbations overstates that agreement when the
   measurement has plate and batch structure.
3. read a design cost and a survival ratio, and say which number to report.
4. name the comparisons a design-blind analysis would not have run.

This is a conceptual lesson with a runnable companion. It provides no gene-editing
conditions, guide design, cell culture steps, or clinical recommendation, and it
nominates no target.

## Two readouts of one perturbation

A perturbation does not produce one observable. It produces a cellular state, and
every instrument that records that state records a different projection of it. In this
lesson the two projections are a transcriptomic profile, 978 landmark probe sets
measured with L1000, and a morphological profile, 291 features measured with Cell
Painting. When the same perturbation is measured by both instruments on the same cells
at the same time, the two profiles can be joined and a question becomes measurable.
How well does the transcriptomic profile of a perturbation predict its imaging
profile?

That question is worth asking because a signature that predicts a phenotype the
signature was never derived from is harder to explain away than a signature that only
reproduces itself. A cross-modal map from transcriptome to imaging is one concrete
way to ask it.

The answer is a correlation, and a correlation is not a mechanism. Two instruments
agreeing about a perturbation says that the perturbation left a coherent state that
both instruments could see. It does not say which molecular event caused that state,
which of the two instruments is closer to the truth, or that the state matters for
anything downstream. The map in this lesson is descriptive. It names no target and
predicts no phenotype.

The subtlety is that agreement has a second possible source. The two readouts were
also produced by the same experiment. They shared plates and plate maps, and every one
of them belongs to a single batch of a single study. A map can learn the shape of the
experiment as easily as the shape of the perturbation, and when it does, the agreement
it reports is real and completely uninformative about biology.

## The design between the readouts

Suppose a random split divides the perturbations into a training side and a test
side, with one perturbation on one side or the other. A perturbation is measured on
8 of the 16 plates in each readout, so the training side usually contains
perturbations that were measured on the same plates, in the same plate map and in the
same run as the held-out perturbation. The map can then lean on the design instead of
on the perturbation, and the held-out score measures how well the design generalises
within itself.

Two controls make that visible, and both are cheap.

The first destroys only the pairing. Each perturbation keeps its plate, its batch and
its position in the experiment, but its imaging profile is replaced by another
perturbation's imaging profile from the same batch. Any agreement that survives this
null is produced by the design alone, and the agreement of a real analysis has to be
read against it rather than against zero.

The second fixes the training-set size. A blocked evaluation holds out a whole plate
or a whole batch, which necessarily leaves fewer training rows than a random fold, so
a drop in performance is ambiguous on its own. The control is a random split with
exactly the same training and test sizes drawn from the same population. The gap
between a blocked fold and its size-matched twin is the design cost of the structural
separation, and it is the part of the apparent agreement that a design-blind analysis
would have reported as biology.

The last quantity is the survival ratio. It is the share of the above-null agreement
that remains once the structure is held out, so it is bounded below by zero and above
by one, and it reads directly as the fraction of the cross-modal signal that is a
property of the perturbation rather than of the experiment.

## Worked case

The data is one batch of one Cell Painting Gallery study, `cpg0003-rosetta`, batch
`LUAD-BBBC041-Caicedo`, in A549 lung adenocarcinoma cells carrying engineered
genetic perturbations, read 96 hours after the perturbation. The transcriptomic object
carries 4,232 wells over 16 assay plates, and the imaging object 6,144 wells over 16
assay plates drawn from two plate maps, which are the two batches of this batch.
Joining the two readouts on the perturbation identifier leaves 528 perturbations that
carry both profiles.

The apparent agreement is strong. With a random five-fold split, the imaging profile
of a held-out perturbation is predicted from its transcriptomic profile at a
correlation of 0.8158 per perturbation and 0.8256 per imaging feature. The first control
says the design is not the explanation. Replacing each perturbation's imaging profile
with another perturbation's imaging profile from the same batch drops the per-feature
agreement to 0.0033 and the per-perturbation agreement to 0.1317. A model-free check
agrees with the fitted one, fitting nothing and drawing no split, with a median
Spearman correlation of 0.7449 between the transcriptomic and the imaging similarity
rankings against 0.0007 under the permuted pairing.

The design is then taken apart. The imaging plate accounts for 2.71% of the imaging
feature variance, the L1000 plate for 1.56% of the transcriptomic feature variance, and
the imaging batch for 0.36%. A small variance share is not a small effect on a
prediction, so the agreement is measured again with the structure held out.

| Evaluation | Perturbation r | Feature r | Perturbations tested |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Apparent (random five-fold) | 0.8158 | 0.8256 | 528 |
| Null, pairing destroyed within batch | 0.1317 | 0.0033 | 528 |
| Batch blocked (one whole batch held out) | 0.6886 | 0.6326 | 509 |
| Size-matched random, same sizes | 0.7923 | 0.7877 | 509 |
| Imaging plate blocked | 0.6921 | 0.6516 | 528 |
| Size-matched random, same sizes | 0.7931 | 0.8014 | 528 |
| Transcriptome plate blocked | 0.7041 | 0.6694 | 528 |
| Size-matched random, same sizes | 0.7972 | 0.8089 | 528 |

Holding out one whole batch drops the per-feature agreement from 0.8256 to 0.6326, while
a random split with exactly the same training and test sizes reaches 0.7877. The batch
separation costs 0.1551 of per-feature agreement that a smaller training set does
not explain. Holding out one imaging assay plate costs 0.1497 and holding out one
transcriptome assay plate 0.1394, by the same comparison. The survival
ratio is the share of the above-null agreement that remains, computed as the blocked
per-feature correlation minus the null, divided by the apparent per-feature correlation
minus the null. It is 0.7653 for a held-out batch, 0.7885 for a held-out imaging plate
and 0.8101 for a held-out transcriptome plate.

Read back into the language of the earlier lesson, this is what a mechanism claim can
rest on and what it cannot. Three quarters of the agreement between two readouts of the
same perturbation survives an evaluation in which the model never sees the batch the
perturbation came from. That is real support for the claim that the perturbation
produced a coherent cellular state visible to two independent modalities. The other
quarter does not survive, and a design-blind analysis would have reported all of it as
though it had. The claim is therefore weaker than the statement that the signature is
the mechanism, and stronger than the statement that the two readouts agree.

The named perturbations at the ends of the apparent agreement distribution show what
the number is about and what it is not. `LAP3_WT.o` sits at 0.9861 and `MDM2_p.E421Q` at
-0.1525, where a negative correlation means the transcriptomic profile of that
perturbation points the wrong way for its imaging profile. These are the ends of a
distribution, so they are where to start reading if you want to know which entity a
number concerns. They are not evidence about LAP3 or about MDM2, and the same
discipline the earlier lesson applied to a rescue and a stress control applies here.
A claim about a gene needs a contrast that separates the gene's mechanism from the
alternatives, and no number of readouts supplies that contrast.

## What this does not show

- The agreement is not a mechanism. It is not evidence about a gene, a target
  hypothesis or a clinical outcome, and this material nominates none.
- The blocked evaluations hold out structure inside one published experiment. A batch
  or plate held out here is still a batch or plate the laboratory has seen before, so
  this is not a deployment setting.
- The design cost does not separate the batch's nuisance effect from the fact that a
  batch-homogeneous training set covers less of the perturbation space. It isolates
  the cost of the structural separation at a fixed training-set size and nothing more.
- The protocol fixes one batch of one study, one cell line, one timepoint and one set
  of engineered edits. The study's other four batches, the other gallery studies and
  the JUMP pilot are outside it, and nothing here transfers to them without being
  re-measured.
- The imaging feature set is the gallery's variable-selected release of 291 features.
  The full-feature release for the same batch was not used, and no claim is made that
  these numbers transfer to it.
- The map is a ridge map and not a state-of-the-art cross-modal predictor. Its penalty
  is selected inside each training fold, but the architecture is not tuned, so the
  numbers bound this simple map's agreement and not the agreement that is achievable.
- No reader outcomes have been collected for this material. The committed summary
  records what the pipeline computes, not whether anyone has learned anything from it.

## Independent exercise

A slide states that the transcriptome predicts the Cell Painting profile of a held-out
perturbation at a feature correlation of 0.8256, and that the perturbation signature is
therefore a mechanism.

1. Name the two controls this lab runs that a design-blind analysis would not have, and
   say what each one would show if the plate and batch structure, rather than the
   perturbation, were producing the agreement.
2. Holding out one whole batch drops the per-feature agreement to 0.6326, while a random
   split with exactly the same training and test sizes reaches 0.7877. Compute the
   difference, name it, and state which of the two numbers you would report as the
   agreement of a cross-modal map.
3. The most agreeable perturbation in the distribution is `LAP3_WT.o` at 0.9861 and the
   least is `MDM2_p.E421Q` at -0.1525. What do those two rows establish about the
   perturbation, and what do they not establish?

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

The two controls are the null that keeps the plate and batch structure and destroys only
the pairing, and the size-matched random split. If the design were producing the
agreement, the permuted null would sit near the apparent 0.8256 rather than at 0.0033, and
a blocked fold would reach the value of its size-matched random twin. The model-free
neighbourhood concordance of 0.7449 is a third, independent agreement, because it fits
nothing and draws no split and so cannot inherit a split-specific shortcut. Its
permuted counterpart falls to 0.0007.

The difference is 0.7877 - 0.6326 = 0.1551, and the protocol calls it the design cost of
the structural separation at a fixed training-set size. The blocked value of 0.6326 is
the number to report, because the size-matched random split at 0.7877 is the value the
design would have produced with the same amount of training data.

The two named rows are the ends of an agreement distribution and nothing more. They say
that under one fitted map the transcriptomic profile and the imaging profile of those
two perturbations pointed in the same direction, or in opposite directions for the
second one. They are not evidence about LAP3 or about MDM2, and the negative value says
the map pointed the wrong way for that perturbation rather than that the perturbation
does nothing.

</details>
