## Learning goals and prerequisites

After this lesson you should be able to write a six-field disease-state record for
a target hypothesis, recompute what share of a reported population a subgroup
result describes, and name the field whose absence lets one hypothesis be both
supported and reversed. Read [From disease mechanism to
target](/learn/mechanism-targets/disease-to-target/) first. [Target product
profiles and chemical-series feedback](/learn/drug-discovery-foundations/product-profile-and-series/)
and [Data contracts and licences](/learn/data-governance/data-contracts/) supply
the decision context and the record discipline this lesson assumes.

This is a reading-and-reasoning lesson. It uses published trial and case results
as evidence about how a disease state is defined. It is not treatment guidance,
adds no patient data, and claims nothing about any therapy beyond what the cited
abstracts report.

## Six fields of a disease state

A target hypothesis has the form "changing target T in disease state D moves
outcome Y in the intended direction". D is usually written as a disease name, and a
disease name is not enough.

| Field            | The question it answers                                                                | A record for the worked case                                                              |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Indication       | Which clinical condition, at what stage or line of treatment?                          | Advanced pulmonary adenocarcinoma with no prior treatment                                 |
| Phenotype        | Which measurable disease outcome is the hypothesis about?                              | Tumour progression or death, read as progression-free survival                            |
| Tissue           | Where must the target act for that outcome to change?                                  | The tumour                                                                                |
| Cell state       | Which molecular state of the relevant cells does the hypothesis require?               | Tumour cells carrying a somatic EGFR kinase-domain mutation                               |
| Population       | Who is the evidence about, as selected, and who will the decision be about?            | The trial's eligibility criteria, and the share of patients whose cell state was measured |
| Temporal context | At what point in the disease course, and does the state change with time or treatment? | Before any treatment, as opposed to after relapse on a first inhibitor                    |

The fields interact. The cell state is often what makes a hypothesis about a
tissue true, and the temporal context is often what changes the cell state.

Reference resources can fill some of these fields for their own samples, never for
your intended patients directly. The GTEx Portal records tissue-specific expression
in tissue from human donors, and DepMap records dependencies in cancer models. Each
describes the tissue or cell state of its own samples. It becomes evidence for a
field of your disease state only after those samples are shown to match it.

## Worked case, step 1: a cell state is found

Lynch and colleagues (2004) sequenced the EGFR gene in tumours from patients with
non-small-cell lung cancer who had been treated with the EGFR inhibitor gefitinib.
Somatic mutations in the tyrosine kinase domain were found in 8 of 9 patients whose
cancer responded, and in none of the 7 patients with no response (P < 0.001).
Before this, "gefitinib-responsive lung cancer" was an outcome with no cell state
attached. Afterwards the cell state could be named and measured before treatment.

## Worked case, step 2: one trial, two answers

Mok and colleagues (2009) reported a phase 3, open-label trial in East Asia. It
randomised previously untreated patients with advanced pulmonary adenocarcinoma
who were non-smokers or former light smokers to gefitinib (609 patients) or to
carboplatin plus paclitaxel (608 patients). The primary endpoint was
progression-free survival, in which either disease progression or death counts as
the event. A hazard ratio compares the instantaneous rate of such events in one arm
with the rate in the other. It has no units, and it is neither a probability nor a
ratio of proportions. A value below 1 means events occurred at a lower rate with
gefitinib than with carboplatin plus paclitaxel.

| Population as defined  | Patients | Hazard ratio for progression or death with gefitinib (95% CI) |
| ---------------------- | -------: | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Intention to treat     |    1,217 | 0.74 (0.65 to 0.85)                                           |
| EGFR mutation positive |      261 | 0.48 (0.36 to 0.64)                                           |
| EGFR mutation negative |      176 | 2.85 (2.05 to 3.98)                                           |

Work the population field through before reading any effect.

1. Randomised: `609 + 608 = 1,217`.
2. Mutation status known: `261 + 176 = 437`, which is `437 / 1,217 = 35.9%` of the
   trial. The other `1,217 − 437 = 780` patients, 64.1%, have no evaluable mutation
   status reported in these subgroups.
3. Among patients with a known status, `261 / 437 = 59.7%` were mutation positive.

Now the effect. The two cell-state subgroups point in opposite directions, and
their confidence intervals do not overlap: 0.36 to 0.64 against 2.05 to 3.98. The
intention-to-treat estimate of 0.74 is not a property of "lung cancer" or of
"EGFR". It describes a mixture fixed by the eligibility criteria, in which most
patients had no evaluable mutation status reported. A hypothesis written without a
cell-state field, such as "gefitinib delays progression or death compared with
carboplatin plus paclitaxel in untreated advanced adenocarcinoma", is supported in
one subgroup and reversed in the other within the same randomised trial.

Note what the trial compares. Both arms are active treatments, so the negative
subgroup shows that chemotherapy did better there. It does not show that gefitinib
had no effect, or did harm, relative to no treatment, and nothing in this trial is a
statement about EGFR inhibition in general.

Two cautions keep this reading honest. These are subgroup estimates, and the lesson
uses them to show a disease-state field, not to teach how subgroup analyses should
be designed. The population field also still binds. The estimates describe
patients chosen by these criteria in East Asia, which is not automatically every
population with the same cell state.

## Worked case, step 3: the disease state moves

Temporal context is not a date stamped on the record. It can change the cell
state, and with it the target state that a new molecule has to act on.

Pao and colleagues (2005) found that in two of five patients with acquired
resistance to gefitinib or erlotinib, the progressing tumours carried, in addition
to the original drug-sensitive EGFR mutation, a second mutation that substitutes
methionine for threonine at position 790 (T790M) in the kinase domain. Tumour
cells from a sixth patient, whose tumour progressed on adjuvant gefitinib after
complete resection, also carried T790M. Kobayashi and colleagues (2005) reported
the same substitution in one patient whose EGFR-mutant, gefitinib-responsive cancer
relapsed after two years of complete remission.

Before treatment and after relapse, the gene, the tissue and the diagnosis are the
same. The line of treatment and the cell state are not. A target hypothesis for the second setting has to name
the resistant state, and evidence gathered in the first does not carry over to it
by default.

| Field            | Before treatment                                        | After acquired resistance                                                      |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Indication       | Advanced pulmonary adenocarcinoma, untreated            | The same diagnosis, relapsed on a first inhibitor                              |
| Phenotype        | Progression or death, read as progression-free survival | Further progression or death, the outcome a next treatment would aim to change |
| Tissue           | The tumour                                              | The progressing tumour                                                         |
| Cell state       | A somatic EGFR kinase-domain mutation                   | The original mutation plus T790M, in the tumours where it was found            |
| Population       | Trial eligibility, with status measured in 437 of 1,217 | One small case series and one case report                                      |
| Temporal context | First treatment                                         | After an initial response, in one report after two years of remission          |

The population cell on the right is deliberately thin. One small case series and
one case report are enough to define a new cell state. They are far too little to
estimate how often that state occurs.

## What the deposited structures are evidence of

The [EGFR structure evidence lab](/labs/egfr-structure-evidence/) reads three
deposited crystal structures of the EGFR kinase domain. Its first command maps
each file onto UniProt P00533 reference numbering and reports, for each
crystallised construct, the residues it adds and any substitution against that
sequence. It reports no substitution
in 1M17 or 1XKK and one engineered substitution, V948R, in 4HJO. Position 790 is
one of the five landmark positions whose residue the lab's check confirms, and it
holds threonine in all three files, the residue that the T790M name says is
replaced. Applying the lab's own 4 Å site definition to the committed files
(computed on 2026-09-11) places position 790 inside all three ligand sites.

So the resistant disease state changes a residue inside the binding site that
these three structures define. They are evidence about the constructs that were
crystallised. Using them for the resistant state is a modelling hypothesis, and it
has to be stated as one. The lab's check says the same of itself: it verifies what
the files say, not that any structure is the state that matters in a cell.

## Independent exercise

A **constructed** target proposal reads: "Inhibiting kinase K will slow colorectal
cancer. In a panel of 30 colorectal cancer cell lines, knocking out K reduced
growth in 12. We propose a trial in patients with metastatic colorectal cancer."
The proposal also notes that all 12 dependent lines carry marker M and that none
of the other 18 does. A separate, also constructed, record estimates that marker M
is present in 10% of patients with metastatic colorectal cancer.

1. Fill in the six fields as the proposal states them. Which are missing?
2. Which field, once written down, could make the evidence support the hypothesis
   in one group and not in another?
3. Roughly what share of the proposed trial population carries the cell state in
   which the panel showed dependency, and what does that share not tell you?

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

**Fields.** The indication is metastatic colorectal cancer for the trial, but no
line of treatment is given. The phenotype is unspecified in the proposal: "will slow
colorectal cancer" names no measurable endpoint, while the evidence measures the
growth of cultured cells. The tissue, colorectal cancer, is stated. The cell state is
missing from the hypothesis, although the data point straight at it. The population
is 30 cell lines for the evidence and unselected patients for the decision. The
temporal context is missing: the cell lines' treatment history is unknown and the
trial line is unstated.

**The field that splits the answer** is the cell state. Growth dependency on K
appeared in `12 / 30 = 40%` of lines, every one of them marker M positive, and in
none of the 18 marker-negative lines. In these cell lines the knockout result
supports a hypothesis about marker-M-positive disease and argues against dependency
in marker-negative lines. It says nothing yet about an inhibitor, because a knockout
is not a drug, or about patients, because cell lines are not patients. Written with
a cell state, the hypothesis to test becomes "inhibiting K slows marker-M-positive
colorectal cancer", with its clinical endpoint still to be named.

**Share of the population.** If about 10% of patients carry marker M, the cell state
in which the panel showed dependency is carried by roughly one patient in ten of an
unselected trial. The other nine in ten fall outside it. That is a statement about
who carries the state, not about who would benefit, which the cell lines cannot
show. As in the worked trial, an estimate over an unselected mixture would average
over subgroups that the evidence treats differently. The proportions are not the
same as in that trial: here the marker is constructed to be rare, so an averaged
result would be dominated by patients outside the only state the evidence concerns.

</details>

## Limits

- The trial and case numbers are taken from the cited abstracts, opened on
  2026-09-11. Full texts, later analyses and overall survival are not used.
- Six fields are necessary for a disease-state record, not sufficient for causal
  target evidence. For that, continue with [genetic
  evidence](/learn/mechanism-targets/genetics-to-target-evidence/) and
  [perturbation across contexts](/learn/mechanism-targets/perturbation-context-generalization/).
- The structural statements are observations about three committed files, not
  about the protein in any patient's tumour.
