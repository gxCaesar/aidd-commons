## Learning goals

After this lesson, you should be able to distinguish a GWAS locus, fine-mapped
variant, and effector-gene hypothesis; state assumptions behind a credible set,
colocalisation result, and single-variant MR ratio; and explain why they inform
but do not determine target direction.

## Prerequisites and boundary

Read [From disease mechanism to target](/learn/mechanism-targets/disease-to-target/)
and [Biological representations](/learn/representations/biological-representations/)
first. This reading-and-reasoning lesson supplies no workflow, real genetic
data, clinical interpretation, or causal proof.

## Three objects that must not be merged

A GWAS association says a tested marker or locus is associated with a phenotype
in a declared population. It does not identify the causal DNA change, gene, or
drug target; a lead marker can merely tag the causal change.

Linkage disequilibrium (LD) means nearby co-inherited variants can correlate.
Fine mapping uses the association pattern and LD to distribute support. Its PIP
is the model-conditional posterior that a candidate variant is causal; in the
one-causal-variant setting used here, a credible set collects candidates to a
declared mass, often 95%. Neither is a gene list or functional certificate.
[Maller et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3791416/) illustrate
credible sets and a single typed causal-variant limitation.

An effector gene is a further hypothesis: a variant changes a gene-related
molecular process that contributes in a relevant context. Proximity is not
enough; a locus can contain several genes, affect a distal gene, or carry signals.

### Worked case: PIP mass is not a gene claim

This **constructed teaching example** assumes one causal variant among five
represented candidates. Its PIPs are:

| Variant |  PIP |
| ------- | ---: |
| `v1`    | 0.44 |
| `v2`    | 0.27 |
| `v3`    | 0.16 |
| `v4`    | 0.08 |
| `v5`    | 0.05 |

The mass is `0.44 + 0.27 + 0.16 + 0.08 + 0.05 = 1.00`. `v1` through `v4` sum to
`0.95`, the smallest 95% credible set. This does **not** make all four causal
or name an effector gene. It is conditional on the candidate set, LD, model,
and prior, and fails or becomes incomplete if the causal variant is absent,
poorly imputed, or multiple when the model allows one.

LD and allele frequencies differ across ancestry groups. A PIP or credible set
is not automatically transportable to another population or molecular-QTL
dataset with different ancestry. This is an inference condition.

## Colocalisation asks a narrower question

Colocalisation compares two association patterns. A disease phenotype and
expression trait may be consistent with distinct causal variants or one shared
variant. The classic summary-statistic formulation separates these hypotheses:
`H3` supports distinct variants and `H4` a shared variant.
[Giambartolomei et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1004383)
make the distinction explicit.

High shared-variant support is model-conditional evidence for a shared
association signal, not named-gene expression mediating disease. One variant
could affect both traits through another route or unmeasured intermediate; it
does not set intervention direction. The original formulation assumes comparable
allele frequencies and LD, a represented causal variant, and at most one signal
per trait. Multiple signals, incomplete coverage, ancestry mismatch, or
inconsistent allele/build definitions need analysis, not visual overlap.

## MR is an instrument argument, not a target verdict

MR uses genetic variation `G` as an instrument for exposure `X` and outcome
`Y`. It needs relevance (`G` is associated with `X`), independence (`G` is not
associated with `X`--`Y` confounders), and exclusion (`G` affects `Y` only
through `X`, conditional on stated confounders). Horizontal pleiotropy can
violate exclusion.
[Burgess, Butterworth, and Thompson](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/gepi.21758)
state these conditions and a ratio construction for linear, continuous traits.

Before a ratio, allele harmonisation is essential: `beta_GX` and `beta_GY` must
describe the same variant and effect allele. Flip a sign only when reported
alleles are known opposites; phenotype definitions and effect scales must match.
An ambiguous or unrecorded effect allele is unresolved, not a reason to guess.

### Worked case: a linear Wald ratio with stated units

This second **constructed teaching example** uses continuous, linear phenotypes
only. For one effect allele, let `beta_GX = +0.20 X units per allele` and
`beta_GY = -0.06 Y units per allele`. The single-variant Wald ratio is
`beta_GY / beta_GX = -0.06 / 0.20 = -0.30 Y units per X unit`.

Under the stated instrumental-variable and linear-model assumptions, the ratio
has a negative direction on these measurement scales. It is not a clinical risk,
diagnosis, therapeutic effect, or dose. There is no confidence interval because
this is arithmetic, not an analysis of observed data.

## From genetic direction to a testable target hypothesis

A target-direction argument preserves its object chain: variant/effect allele;
molecular trait/putative effector gene; phenotype; relevant tissue or cell
context; and perturbation/readout. The worked `-0.30 Y units per X unit` ratio
means, under its assumptions, that higher `X` corresponds to lower `Y`. If lower
`Y` were a stated experimental objective, this could motivate testing higher
`X`—not inhibition—as a hypothesis. `X` is an unspecified molecular trait, not
automatically protein activity; map expression to protein and function before
proposing an intervention. This is not a claim of clinical benefit. It still
needs a perturbation with stated identity, specificity, duration, baseline, and
readout in the relevant disease context. [Perturbation prediction across cellular
contexts](/learn/mechanism-targets/perturbation-context-generalization/) explains
why that context cannot be silently averaged away.

Genetic variation can act across development and life; a perturbation is timed,
finite, and may act on one molecular species. Feedback, compensation, tissue
exposure, and magnitude can differ. Genetic direction is not dose equivalence
or proof that one protein change gives the same phenotype. Convergent evidence
makes a hypothesis more testable, not proven.

## Independent exercise

A different **constructed** locus is analysed under the same one-causal-
variant-in-the-candidate-set assumption. Its PIPs are `u1 = 0.51`, `u2 = 0.31`,
`u3 = 0.12`, and `u4 = 0.06`. Separately, for continuous linear traits and one
already harmonised effect allele, `beta_GX = -0.15 X units per allele` and
`beta_GY = +0.045 Y units per allele`.

1. What is the smallest 90% credible set?
2. What is the Wald-ratio value and unit?
3. Name one inference condition that could fail for the credible set and one
   reason why the ratio does not prove an intervention direction.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

`u1 + u2 = 0.82`; adding `u3` gives `0.94`, so the 90% set is `{u1, u2, u3}`.
The ratio is `0.045 / -0.15 = -0.30 Y units per X unit`. The set can fail if the
causal variant is absent, LD mismatches the population, or several causal
variants exist. The ratio does not prove direction: pleiotropy can break an IV
premise and a life-long genetic shift is not a timed perturbation or dose.

</details>

## Failure boundaries and source path

Do not rename a locus as a gene, a credible set as a mechanism, a shared variant
as mediation, or an MR ratio as a drug effect. Record population, variant/build/
effect allele, coverage, LD/model assumptions, molecular context, and
perturbation contract before making a target hypothesis.
