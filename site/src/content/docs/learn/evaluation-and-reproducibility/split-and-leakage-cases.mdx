## Before you start

Read [Honest evaluation](/learn/evaluation-and-reproducibility/honest-evaluation/)
and the distinction between records and entities in
[Data contracts](/learn/data-governance/data-contracts/). You need only a browser
and somewhere to write your answers. Do not download data, fit models, or
reopen a completed evaluation for this exercise.

The cases reuse the completed T05 ChEMBL comparison, T11 rMD17 energy lab and
T15 retrospective campaign. The quoted proposals below are deliberately flawed
teaching scenarios, not additional actions taken in those experiments.

Your goal is to distinguish three things that a train/test percentage hides:

- **Membership:** which entity or group must stay together?
- **Information use:** which records may influence fitting or selection?
- **Claim:** which new situation does this evaluation actually examine?

A grouping key enforces membership. It does not, by itself, prove that groups
are statistically independent or representative of future deployment.

## Case 1: split rows or molecules?

Open the ChEMBL lab's **Entity and label contract** and **Scaffold split**
sections from the case materials below. Repeated eligible measurements are
aggregated by parent before modelling, and whole scaffold groups are assigned
to partitions.

> “Randomly split the activity rows first. Repeated measurements add test
> samples. Move a few parents between partitions to get exact percentages.”

Write the grouping unit, the unit contributing an error to MAE, and one check
that would reject this proposal. Explain why the realised train, validation
and primary evaluation counts are **634, 136 and 132**, rather than the nominal
targets **631, 135 and 136**. Then write a narrower generalization claim.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Case 1 — worked answer</summary>

The split assigns whole chirality-aware RDKit Bemis-Murcko scaffold groups.
The label and prediction belong to a parent molecule; repeated assay rows are
not extra evaluated parents. Splitting rows can put measurements of the same
parent on both sides. Moving individual parents can also split a scaffold.

- Check that each parent has one partition and that no scaffold key crosses
  partitions. Counts alone cannot establish either property.
- Whole groups can overshoot a nominal boundary. Both count lists sum to 902,
  but matching that total does not justify changing membership. The realised
  allocation preserves groups rather than exact percentages.
- Ring-free molecules use `acyclic:<canonical isomeric SMILES>` keys here.
  This is an explicit grouping convention, not proof that distinct acyclic
  molecules are unrelated.

A defensible statement is “evaluation on held-out scaffold keys within this
primary assay under the declared grouping rule.” It does not imply generalization
to all future EGFR chemistry, targets, or laboratories. Parent-level MAE is a
descriptive aggregate; scaffold disjointness does not make every parent an
independent replication.

</details>

## Case 2: what may influence a model?

Read **Frozen representations** and **Completed evaluation and remaining
boundaries** in the ChEMBL lab.

> “Scaling uses no labels, so fit the imputer and scaler on all molecules.
> Select alpha on validation, refit on train plus validation, and extend the
> grid if the separate-assay score is disappointing.”

For each proposed action, name the boundary it crosses in this lab. Distinguish
computing a fixed fingerprint, learning preprocessing statistics, selecting a
hyperparameter, and changing the study after evaluation.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Case 2 — worked answer</summary>

Descriptor imputation medians, means and scales are fitted on training parents
only. A transform need not use labels to incorporate held-out information.
Validation, primary evaluation and separate-assay molecules receive the same
training-fitted transform; their feature distributions do not fit it.

The fixed Morgan bit vector has no data-fitted preprocessing. Computing that
declared representation is not the same as fitting a scaler on all molecules.
Validation MAE selects each Ridge alpha from the frozen grid. In this specific
protocol, the selected models remain training-only fits: no train-plus-validation
refit is allowed. Such a refit can be specified in another study, but cannot be
silently added to this one.

Extending the grid after seeing an evaluation outcome makes that outcome part
of development. Keep this run unchanged. A future study would need a declared
selection plan and a new evaluation boundary, not another look at these same
scores presented as untouched confirmation.

The source parser reads records to enforce the declared entity and data
contract. That access is distinct from using non-training records to fit a
transform or using evaluation labels to select a model. The separate question
of prior label exposure is addressed in Case 4. The general preprocessing rule
is also explained in the
[scikit-learn leakage guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/common_pitfalls.html#data-leakage).

</details>

## Case 3: ordered frames are not independent systems

Read **Sampling and split**, **Feature and model contract**, and the completed
results in the rMD17 lab.

> “The indices increase across the split, so the evaluation frames are
> independent. The same alpha works for benzene and uracil, proving that one
> trained model transfers between molecular systems.”

Describe what is shared and what is fitted separately. Does a positive index
gap measure decorrelation time? Rewrite the claim without pooling frames into
independent molecular-system replications.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Case 3 — worked answer</summary>

The procedure sorts by `old_indices`, samples declared ranks, then takes
contiguous portions of that selected order: 600 training, 200 validation and
200 evaluation frames per system. These are not simply the first 1,000 raw
NPZ rows. Increasing indices and positive gaps describe ordering and spacing;
they do not measure a decorrelation time or establish independent errors.

The development-selected alpha is shared, but each system has its own
training-fitted scaler, energy centering and Ridge coefficients. Benzene and
uracil each contribute their own training frames. The feature channels can
also differ with elemental composition.

The supported observation is a lower energy MAE than the train-mean control
in each of two within-system evaluations. It is not zero-shot transfer,
force prediction, or hundreds of independent system replications. Correlation
is an inference limitation even when membership is disjoint; it is not itself
proof that labels entered training.

The original run did not retain per-frame Ridge predictions. Its Ridge scores
were not independently recomputed after the run. Keep that record limitation
alongside the within-system claim; this reading exercise does not repair it.

</details>

## Case 4: separate files, shared evidence

Read **Completed evaluation and remaining boundaries** in the ChEMBL lab and
**Reproduction and remaining boundaries** in the pool-budget lab. Use the real
ChEMBL campaign, not the artificial-score simulator.

> “T15 has a different result file and T05 evaluates a separate assay. Both
> therefore provide untouched independent confirmation of the representation.”

Trace the direction of reused information. State what remained unchanged,
what the exposure prevents you from claiming, and whether the two assay
outcomes support a single winning representation.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Case 4 — worked answer</summary>

T15 reused the Morgan Ridge alpha chosen in T05 development. T15 then accessed
labels shared with T05 before the completed T05 evaluation. Separate result
files and different tasks do not erase that shared information history.

T05 retained its split, grid and selected alphas without retuning after the
exposure. That preserves the declared comparison, but does not restore an
entirely untouched independent confirmatory evaluation. T15 is retrospective
instructional evidence, not independent T05 corroboration or prospective
discovery.

The second ChEMBL assay has no parent-molecule overlap with the primary assay,
but measures the same target in a separate experimental source. Parent
disjointness is not a blanket guarantee of independence. Morgan Ridge has the
lowest primary MAE and the highest separate-assay MAE; descriptor Ridge has
the lowest separate-assay MAE. Keep both outcomes and all three baselines,
including the train mean, visible. There is no uniform winner across these
evaluations, and the scores alone do not establish the cause of failed transfer.

</details>

## Submit a short evaluation memo

Choose one case and write four sentences: the membership unit; permitted
information use; the observed result or documented procedure; and the narrowest
supported claim with its main limitation. Add a link to the relevant case
material. For each flawed proposal, distinguish a protocol violation from an
unsupported inference: they are not interchangeable diagnoses.

A complete answer explains why row disjointness is weaker than parent and
scaffold checks, why unsupervised preprocessing can still cross a fitting
boundary, why ordered frames do not establish independence, and why prior
label exposure cannot be undone by renaming an output. If any point is missing,
revisit that case before using the optional lesson-completion button. The
button records reading progress, not assessed competence.
