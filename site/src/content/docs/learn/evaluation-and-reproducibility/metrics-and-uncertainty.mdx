## Four different questions

- **MAE and RMSE:** how far are predicted values from their recorded targets?
  MAE averages absolute errors. RMSE takes the square root of the mean squared
  error, giving larger errors more weight before aggregation. Both retain the
  target's units; lower is better under the same task and evaluated records.
  See the [regression metric definitions](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/model_evaluation.html#regression-metrics).
- **Spearman correlation:** how well does the ordering of predictions agree
  with the ordering of targets? It is a dimensionless rank association, not an
  error in target units or a probability of correctness. A constant prediction
  vector has undefined Spearman correlation, not zero correlation. Higher values
  indicate better agreement with increasing target values here. See the
  [SciPy definition and constant-input warning](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.spearmanr.html).
- **Interval coverage and width:** how often do recorded targets fall inside
  their prediction intervals, and how wide are those intervals? Coverage is a
  fraction of evaluated targets; width is in target units. Coverage alone does
  not tell you whether an interval is useful.
- **Recovery AUC:** how early does a policy acquire a fixed set of high-scoring
  pool entries within the declared budget? Its axes and normalization are
  defined by the campaign, not by the letters “AUC.”

The number of rows in a metric denominator is not automatically the number of
independent units available for statistical inference.

## Case 1: rank agreement is not prediction accuracy

Read the ChEMBL primary and separate-assay tables, keeping all three baselines.

> “Replace the train mean's missing Spearman with zero. Average Spearman with
> MAE to rank models, then report the primary winner as best across assays.”

Identify three errors. State the endpoint, the scoring unit and the direction
of each metric. Would adding the same offset to every nonconstant prediction
preserve Spearman? Must it preserve MAE?

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Case 1 — worked answer</summary>

The target is median-aggregated parent-molecule pChEMBL derived from exact
IC50 measurements. MAE and RMSE are errors on that logarithmic scale, not nM
errors. Spearman uses ranks and is dimensionless. Adding a constant preserves
prediction ranks, but can change absolute errors. Good ordering therefore
does not establish accurate endpoint values.

The primary metrics use 132 parents; the separate-assay metrics use 170.
The train mean has valid error metrics but no rank variation, so its
`null` Spearman and `undefined_constant_prediction` status must remain visible.
Zero would instead be a defined correlation value. Do not discard the baseline
or fabricate a replacement value to make a composite score computable.

Raw MAE and Spearman have different scales and preferred directions. No rule
for combining them was declared. Morgan Ridge has the lowest primary MAE but
the highest separate-assay MAE; descriptor Ridge has the lowest separate-assay
MAE. These are separate descriptive comparisons, not a uniform winner or a
significance test. No confidence interval or p value is reported for them.
T15's prior access to shared labels also prevents an untouched independent
confirmation claim; no retuning followed that exposure.

</details>

## Case 2: what does a 90% interval cover?

Read the FreeSolv scaffold-primary table and its downloaded aggregate summary.
Both learned models target experimental hydration free energy in kcal/mol.
The summary records 64 test molecules, empirical coverage **0.875** for Ridge
and **0.90625** for the random forest.

> “These are 90% confidence intervals for MAE. The random forest is calibrated
> for every new scaffold, so it is the better predictor. Widen the Ridge
> intervals until this test set reaches the target coverage.”

Correct the interval's object, distinguish nominal from empirical coverage,
and compare coverage together with width and MAE. Which records determine
the interval radius? What does the aggregate not tell you about subgroups?

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Case 2 — worked answer</summary>

These are prediction intervals around individual predicted hydration free
energies, not confidence intervals for MAE or a population mean. Each learned
model uses a validation-only split-conformal radius, calibrated at a nominal
90% level. Test labels evaluate coverage; they do not set that radius.

Empirical coverage divides covered test targets by all 64 test molecules.
The reported fractions correspond arithmetically to 56 covered targets for
Ridge and 58 for the random forest. This reads the existing aggregate; it does
not independently recheck individual predictions. Nominal 90% is the procedure's
target, not a promise that every finite test sample has exactly that fraction.

The random forest's observed coverage is higher and its mean interval is wider,
while Ridge has lower MAE on this scaffold-primary split. Neither coverage alone
nor MAE alone resolves every predictive objective. Widening an interval can
increase coverage without improving point predictions; changing radii after
reading this test would make the test part of calibration.

The usual split-conformal marginal guarantee relies on exchangeability of
calibration and test examples. It is not a per-molecule or every-subgroup
guarantee. Scaffold separation alone does not establish that assumption, and
the aggregate does not show coverage by scaffold. See
[Angelopoulos and Bates, sections 1 and 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511).
Do not label these results conditional calibration or guaranteed performance
under a new distribution shift.

</details>

## Case 3: a denominator is not an independence claim

Read the rMD17 completed result and its limitations.

> “Pool the 200 evaluation frames from each system. There are 400 independent
> repeats, so the lower MAE proves a precise, general improvement. FreeSolv
> also uses kcal/mol, so compare the two labs' MAEs directly.”

Distinguish a frame-level error average from replication over systems. Which
uncertainty estimate was actually reported? Does a shared physical unit make
the endpoints or prediction tasks interchangeable?

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Case 3 — worked answer</summary>

Each rMD17 MAE averages errors over 200 evaluation frames within one system.
Results are reported separately for benzene and uracil. Each system's model is
fitted only on its own training frames. Frames are correlated configurations, not 400
independent molecular-system replications. Even the two system results do not
establish representative sampling of all molecular systems.

Ridge has lower energy MAE than the train-mean control in each completed
within-system comparison. The delivered summary reports no confidence interval
or p value, and no frame-pooled result. A range or median of two system scores
is a descriptive summary, not a confidence interval. Missing uncertainty is
not evidence of no uncertainty. No new interval is computed here.

FreeSolv predicts experimental hydration free energy across molecules; this
rMD17 exercise predicts configuration energy within a named system. Both use
kcal/mol, but their endpoints, target variation, splits and baselines differ.
The common unit does not create a matched comparison. The rMD17 run also lacks
saved per-frame Ridge predictions, so its Ridge scores were not independently
recomputed after the run. Retain that limitation rather than imply that this
lesson supplies the missing record.

</details>

## Case 4: recovery AUC is not ROC AUC

Read the pool-budget lab's completed real campaign and
[the recovery definition](/learn/active-learning/experiment-pools-and-budgets/#4-define-the-recovery-denominator-and-integrate-it).
Keep the artificial-score simulator separate from the real ChEMBL replay.

> “AUC below 0.5 means below chance. Five random seeds are five independent
> assays, and final recovery is enough to reconstruct the AUC.”

Name the numerator, denominator, horizontal axis and integration rule. What
varies across random seeds, and what stays shared? Explain why the order and
checkpoints matter even when final recovery is identical.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Case 4 — worked answer</summary>

At a checkpoint, recovery counts acquired members of the fixed top-decile
target set and divides by that target-set size. In the real replay, the target
set contains exactly `ceil(P/10)` parents ranked by decreasing pChEMBL from the
original acquisition pool of `P` parents, with the declared tie rule. Initial
training molecules and the separate predictive evaluation set are outside
this denominator; it is not the number selected so far.

The horizontal axis is cumulative acquisitions. The lab integrates recovery
by trapezoids at the declared round boundaries, including the initial zero,
then divides by the final acquisition count. Final recovery alone does not
determine this area: earlier recovery contributes for more of the budget.
Changing checkpoint placement can also change the result. This is not an ROC
curve, and it has no universal 0.5 chance reference.

For the real primary assay, MaxMin's recovery AUC is 0.05078125; for the separate
assay it is 0. In both assays it is below every one of the five fixed random
policy instances. Retain all instances, including zeros, rather than select
the most favorable seed. The seed changes a policy's selections within the
same pool; it does not generate another independent assay. These descriptive
outcomes do not establish random selection as universally better, and no
confidence interval or significance test is supplied.

The real replay's separate evaluation-set MAE on pChEMBL values answers a different question
from recovery: prediction of unacquired evaluation labels versus acquisition
of designated pool targets. T15 remains retrospective teaching evidence, not
prospective discovery or independent T05 corroboration.

</details>

## Write a metric card

Choose one reported quantity and record its endpoint, units, evaluated set,
numerator and denominator where applicable, preferred direction, and the
uncertainty actually supplied. Finish with one supported sentence and one
claim the result cannot establish. Link the lab or downloaded summary you used.

A complete answer keeps undefined correlation distinct from zero, prediction
intervals distinct from confidence intervals, marginal coverage distinct from
subgroup coverage, and repeated seeds or frames distinct from new independent
experimental units. Do not fill an unavailable uncertainty field with zero.

This exercise does not change the frozen metrics, splits, comparisons or
results. It supplies no new confidence interval, p value or learner-effectiveness
measurement. Original lesson prose is CC BY 4.0; linked data and software
