## Aim

This reading-existing-results lesson uses the completed ChEMBL 37 EGFR
comparison to diagnose a cross-assay reversal. Do not fit a model, access
row-level labels, reopen the completed evaluation, or choose from its outcomes.

After a recipe works on a scaffold-disjoint primary assay but fails on a
separate assay, what is evidence, what is hypothesis, and what should precede
a new evaluation?

## Learning objectives and prerequisites

After this lesson, you should be able to:

- separate the molecule scoring unit from the assay transfer-inference unit;
- preserve recipe parity while reading the reversal; and
- distinguish observed failure from an unestablished cause in a decision memo.

Read [Metrics and uncertainty across four labs](/learn/evaluation-and-reproducibility/metrics-and-uncertainty/)
and [Assay controls and dose-response](/learn/drug-discovery-foundations/assay-controls-dose-response/)
first. You need only a browser and notes. The ChEMBL lab and its aggregate
summaries, rather than this page, remain the numerical authorities.

## 1. Name the comparison and its units

The endpoint is median-aggregated parent-molecule pChEMBL from exact IC50
records under the frozen contract. One parent molecule contributes one target
and one prediction to each MAE. The primary scaffold test has 132 parents; the
separate-assay evaluation has 170 parents.

They are metric denominators, not 302 independent assay replicates. A molecule
is the scoring unit; an assay is the transfer context, including source,
procedure, compound set, and recorded labels. One completed primary context
and one separate context cannot establish a reliable estimate of variation
across a broader assay population.

Fair recipe parity is more specific than a shared model name. Source release,
endpoint filters, parent aggregation, scaffold membership, Ridge family, alpha
grid, and validation selection rule were fixed. The recipes differ in declared
input representation; their development-selected alphas are 1000 and 1. The
documented paired evaluation applied the selected train-only recipes to both
surfaces, without outcome-selected alpha, representation, or refit.

Transport assumes more than no parent overlap: comparable endpoint construction,
relevant structural signal for the new parents, and a relationship that remains
useful in the new measurement context. The transfer test examines those
assumptions; target identity, a matched solver, and scaffold separation do not
certify them.

T15 accessed labels shared with T05 before T05 evaluation. T05 retained its
split, grid, and alphas without retuning, but is therefore not an untouched
independent confirmation. This neither erases the scores nor makes the assays
independent confirmations.

## 3. Keep hypotheses separate from evidence

The aggregate-only summary has no identities, individual labels, predictions,
or residual diagnostics. The MAEs cannot distinguish label-range difference,
prediction offset, a few large errors, structure-distribution change, or their
combination. None has been measured here.

Three hypotheses may be carried forward, not promoted to causes: a different
label range or chemical regime; different extrapolation of local fragments and
descriptors; and different measurement context. The official primary
[assay record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/api/data/assay/CHEMBL5736995.json)
describes a TR-FRET biochemical assay with 6 or 12 µM ATP; the
[separate assay record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/api/data/assay/CHEMBL3705421.json)
describes a different kinase-assay kit and 100 µM ATP. ChEMBL classifies both
as human single-protein EGFR assay type `B` (`Binding`) records, while their
descriptions are biochemical kinase-activity readouts rather than direct-binding
Kd measurements. Target identity does not make their measurement contexts
interchangeable. The records make context a hypothesis, not a causal result.

## 4. Diagnose in an order that does not retune on the test

Preserve failed transfer as an outcome. Do not expand the alpha grid, alter
features, remove difficult parents, or select a replacement because the score
is poor. Those actions turn evaluation into development information.

The cheapest next check is documentary: compare frozen contracts and source
metadata for target assignment, endpoint and aggregation, assay format, ATP,
substrate, detection, and parent overlap. It can reveal a mismatch or sharpen
a context hypothesis without manufacturing a score; it cannot decide cause.

Then design a fresh evaluation. Before its labels are used, specify assay
source, grouping, endpoint transformation, recipes, selection rule, and
descriptive diagnostics. A label-range or residual check must not select the
model reported on that same fresh evaluation.

## Transfer exercise: write the decision memo

An independent group wants to use the current separate-assay result to choose
a larger Morgan fingerprint and new alpha, then call a third EGFR assay an
independent confirmation. Before third-assay labels, write a four-paragraph
memo from the aggregate only; do not calculate a new statistic or inspect rows.

Your memo must state the molecule and assay units, the supported conclusion
from the reversal, one plausible cause that is not established, the cheapest
next check, and why a fresh evaluation is still needed.

<summary>Worked decision memo</summary>

Morgan Ridge has lower MAE on 132 primary parents, while Descriptor Ridge has
lower MAE on 170 separate-assay parents. These are parent-level denominators
within two contexts, not 302 assay-independent replications. There is no
uniform winner.

A plausible explanation is changed measurement context or chemistry and label
range. The aggregates cannot distinguish it from prediction offset, a few large
errors, or representation-specific extrapolation. T15's earlier shared-label
access prevents calling T05 untouched independent confirmation.

The cheap next check is a read-only comparison of contracts and official
metadata: format, ATP, substrate, detection, endpoint rules, and parent
overlap. It may sharpen a hypothesis, but not revise completed recipes or
claim a metadata-only cause.

A third assay could provide a further bounded transfer check, not broad
generalization. Before its labels, predeclare its source and context plus the
entity/group separation appropriate to the transfer claim, freeze recipes and
selection rule, and ensure no prior label exposure for this evaluation. Choosing
fingerprint size or alpha from the current separate-assay result makes it
development evidence; the third assay remains fresh only if its labels drive no
further choice.

## State what was reproduced

Reading the supplied aggregates reproduces neither model fitting nor the underlying
assay. Recomputing MAE from saved predictions checks a metric calculation for
those rows. Reproducing fitting additionally requires the source/version, eligible
population, entity and split rules, representations, preprocessing, candidate
grid and checkpoint selection, software environment, and the actually executed
commands. A different assay tests transfer, not an identical rerun.

For this lesson, a precise scope statement is: “We interpreted the completed
ChEMBL 37 paired evaluation and its recorded limitations. No new fit or assay
was run.” To practise this distinction, suppose another reader obtains the same
MAE from the saved rows but has not executed training. Their supported claim is
metric recomputation, not end-to-end reproduction or independent biological
confirmation. Continue to [capstone case 2](/learn/candidate-nomination/capstone-workbook/)
for a constructed prediction-table exercise with a worked answer.
