## Learning goals and prerequisites

You will distinguish route feasibility from a retrosynthetic score, make material
and safety constraints visible without proposing laboratory operations, and keep
forward-prediction output separate from observed feedback. Read [Reaction mapping
and route search](/learn/synthesis-and-automation/reaction-mapping-and-route-search/)
first. This is a browser and handworked planning lesson. It does not authorize
procurement, laboratory work, safety assessment, or a synthesis procedure.

## Feasibility is a contract with several gates

A route graph becomes decision-relevant only after each edge and terminal node
has a feasibility record. Material availability is one field: it includes the
declared identity and form, source or inventory reference, date checked, amount
or scale requirement if relevant, and status such as confirmed, unavailable, or
not checked. “Purchasable” in a search index is not the same as a material being
available for the stated project at the required time.

Safety and operational review are separate authority fields. A route may have a
plausible graph and confirmed materials yet remain `approval_required` because
a competent institutional safety review has not occurred. The absence of a
warning in a dataset does not mark a transformation safe. This lesson deliberately
does not list operational conditions, hazards, or mitigation steps; it teaches
only the decision boundary and record structure.

One compact status function is
`route_status(r) = material_gate(r) AND safety_authority(r) AND evidence_gate(r)`.
Here each term is a named status, not a probability or a promise of success.
For example, an unavailable terminal can make a route ineligible for the current
campaign, while an unreviewed safety field makes it pending rather than false.
Keeping the reasons distinct prevents a search score from silently overriding a
real constraint.

| Gate              | Minimum record                                                             | Honest status examples                           | What it is not       |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------- |
| materials         | identity/form, source, date, availability and quantity boundary            | confirmed, unavailable, not checked              | a purchase order     |
| safety/operations | declared review owner, authority state and restriction note                | approval_required, cleared under named authority | a safety procedure   |
| route logic       | graph version, node states, edge hypotheses and unresolved fields          | consistent, mismatch, unresolved                 | a proof of execution |
| forward model     | input representation, model/version, output set and uncertainty/abstention | predicted, unavailable, out of scope             | an observed product  |
| laboratory record | actual inputs, outcome, measurement/provenance and failed/unknown fields   | observed, failed, inconclusive                   | a model score        |

## Forward prediction and a result are different data objects

A forward model maps a declared representation of inputs to an output proposal,
schematically `X -> P_hat`. Its input contract includes molecular identities,
roles, standardization, and whatever context the model requires. Its output may
be a ranked set, a probability-like score, or an abstention. It does not state
that `P_hat` was observed, isolated, characterized, or obtained at any scale.
The next valid action is a scoped feasibility or human review, not an inflated
claim about a route.

An observed record has a different form: `X, protocol_context, measurement ->
outcome`. It needs provenance and must preserve negative and inconclusive cases.
The Open Reaction Database schema explicitly separates reaction inputs, setup,
conditions, observations, workups, outcomes, and provenance
[ORD schema documentation](https://docs.open-reaction-database.org/en/latest/schema.html).
The `ord-schema` repository likewise describes schema and validation tools rather
than a database of guaranteed executable routes
[ord-schema repository](https://github.com/open-reaction-database/ord-schema).

This distinction is the foundation of honest feedback. A model's prediction may
be useful for prioritization only when its version and input contract are saved.
An observed failure may be useful for updating a future policy only when the
input identity, context, and outcome are retained. Do not delete failures to
make a model look calibrated, and do not train or select on a held-out outcome
without declaring that protocol change.

## A feedback loop needs fixed definitions

Before feedback changes a route policy, define what counts as the unit of
feedback. It could be a reaction record, a route edge, or a target campaign;
these are not interchangeable. Freeze the outcome label, reason for exclusion,
and decision rule for the current batch. Then record whether a later update is
exploratory, retrospective, or prospectively tested. A change from “route score”
to “observed laboratory success” is a change in evidence type, not a stronger
number on the same scale.

The smallest useful feedback table retains both successes and failures, links
each to a route-graph version and input state, and says whether it may be used to
update the next model. If a record lacks critical fields, label it incomplete
rather than filling gaps from a similarity search. This protects both laboratory
learning and the interpretation of a forward model.

## Worked case: two routes with different blockers

All values and statuses in this case are **constructed for teaching**. Route
`R_A` has `2` terminal materials, both marked `confirmed` on `2026-09-09`. Its
route graph is internally consistent, but its safety authority is
`approval_required`; its forward model returns proposed outcome `P_hat_A` with
uncertainty field `not reported`. Route `R_B` also has `2` terminal materials,
but one is marked `unavailable` on `2026-09-09`; its safety authority is
`not checked`.

Neither route is ready for execution. `R_A` is pending the named safety authority
and requires a review of the forward model's missing uncertainty before any
decision that depends on it. `R_B` is currently ineligible because of material
status and independently lacks a safety review. It would be misleading to call
`R_A` “validated” from `P_hat_A`, or to call `R_B` chemically impossible from an
availability field. The correct output is a bounded status record with separate
blockers, not a laboratory recommendation.

Suppose a later constructed feedback record for `R_A` contains an input identity
but an outcome marked `inconclusive`. It must remain in the table. It cannot be
relabelled as a success or used to claim model accuracy; it may motivate a
separate data-quality review of the missing outcome fields.

## Limits and independent exercise

Availability changes, models have coverage limits, and laboratory records can be
incomplete or non-comparable. A forward model can be internally consistent while
wrong for an unrepresented transformation. A safety field can be cleared under
one authority and still not transfer to another setting. These are reasons to
maintain a route contract, not to convert unknowns into favourable scores.

**Handworked exercise (not runnable or empirical):** A constructed route `R_C`
has `3` terminal fields: two `confirmed` and one `not checked`. Its graph is
`consistent`; safety authority is `approval_required`; a model returns `P_hat_C`
with score `0.93`. Which fields prevent a readiness claim? State whether
`P_hat_C` is an observed outcome, and name one field needed before an observed
result could be used in a later feedback update.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

The `not checked` material field and `approval_required` safety authority prevent
a readiness claim, regardless of `0.93`. `P_hat_C` is a model proposal, not an
observed outcome. A later feedback record needs at least the exact input identity,
route-graph version, outcome/measurement field, provenance, and an explicit
label for failed or inconclusive cases. The score does not replace any of them.

</details>
