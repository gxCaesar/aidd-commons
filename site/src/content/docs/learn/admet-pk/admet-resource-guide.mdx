## Learning goal and boundary

Use this guide after [ADMET evaluation](/learn/admet-pk/admet-evaluation/) and
[clearance, exposure, and PK/PD](/learn/admet-pk/exposure-clearance-pkpd/).
Its purpose is resource selection and inspection, not model execution, dosing,
or toxicology advice. The useful first question is not “which ADMET tool is
best?” It is: **what object, endpoint, and next decision do I have?** A molecule
identifier, a concentration-response record, and an individual’s concentration
time course are different entities. A resource that connects only one of them
cannot establish the others.

Keep a compact request card: chemical identity and form; endpoint and assay
system; species, route, matrix, and time; intended output; and the alternative
explanation that would change the decision. “Predict safety from a SMILES” lacks
an endpoint, exposure context, and decision rule. “Find reported human
midazolam half-lives after an oral intervention in healthy subjects” is a
retrieval question with named entities and a usable inspection target.

The first row is a **software and tutorial** choice. OpenADMET Demos shows a
CYP3A4 case through curation, model training, comparison, and application to an
unseen set; it is not a released universal CYP3A4 truth set. OpenADMET Models
contains model implementations and training routines. Inspect the endpoint
definition, source records, split, and selection rule before adapting either.
The Atlas records source code under its stated licences, but do not assert
released weights or complete data provenance.

The middle rows are **evidence retrieval**, not prediction. CompTox is a useful
identity-centered entry point. Its pages may bring together experimental and
predicted fields, so retain field provenance and do not turn a displayed
prediction into an observed label. ToxCast is an EPA resource of heterogeneous
in-vitro assays. Its relevant input is a chemical plus assay or biological
endpoint; its output is assay-level evidence with concentration-response
processing. It does not answer whether a candidate is safe at a human exposure.

PK-DB keeps the study context that flat ADMET tables often lose. A concentration
or half-life is interpretable only with its study, subject or group,
intervention, matrix or output, and time context. Its web and REST interfaces
support filtering and downloading. Its current repository
[LICENSE](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/pkdb/blob/develop/LICENSE) is MIT;
the API's LGPL label conflicts with that file. The catalog follows the source
licence, which does not automatically cover imported records. Similarly,
[OpenADMET Models' LICENSE](https://github.com/OpenADMET/openadmet-models/blob/main/LICENSE)
is Apache-2.0 despite a conflicting MIT sentence in its README. PK-Sim's
[GPL-2.0 licence includes a clarifying addendum](https://github.com/Open-Systems-Pharmacology/PK-Sim/blob/develop/LICENSE).
Finally, PK-Sim is not a data repository: it combines individuals
or populations, compounds, formulations, administration protocols, and other
building blocks into a PBPK simulation. A curve is therefore an implication of
the entered model, not newly measured PK evidence.

## Constructed case: choose before you calculate

This case is **invented for teaching**. A team has a neutral small-molecule
structure and asks whether it should be prioritized because “CYP3A4 risk is
low.” No assay, species, dose, concentration, or comparator is supplied. The
correct first output is not a rank.

First, use [CompTox](/atlas/db-comptox-chemicals-dashboard/) to resolve and
inspect the chemical record, retaining the submitted structure form and every
matched identifier. Next, use [ToxCast](/atlas/dataset-toxcast/) only to inspect
whether a relevant assay and its annotations exist; a missing or positive record
does not settle CYP3A4 inhibition. If the project needs a modelling template,
inspect the [OpenADMET Demos](/atlas/tutorial-openadmet-demos/) CYP3A4 example
and the [OpenADMET Models](/atlas/tool-openadmet-models/) implementation, then
freeze a source and split before fitting. If the decision is instead about
reported exposure, query [PK-DB](/atlas/db-pk-db/) for a matched intervention,
population, matrix, and time-course or PK output. Only after explicit compound,
physiology, and administration assumptions are assembled is
[PK-Sim](/atlas/tool-pk-sim/) a suitable simulation resource.

Every sentence above is a selection plan, not a claim that this molecule has a
particular liability, exposure, or simulation result. The missing assay and
exposure information are the result of the case, not fields to fill with a
model score.

## Independent resource-selection question

A report asks: “Compare oral exposure of compound X in healthy adults with
compound Y in rats, and assess a mitochondrial-toxicity concern.” Which two
resources should be inspected first, what entity filters must remain attached,
and why should PK-Sim not be the first source of evidence?

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

Start with [PK-DB](/atlas/db-pk-db/) for published PK records and
[ToxCast](/atlas/dataset-toxcast/) for an in-vitro mitochondrial assay search.
For PK-DB, retain compound identity, study, species, health state, route, dose,
matrix, sampling time or derived output, and whether the record represents an
individual or group. Do not compare an oral human AUC with a rat parameter
without these qualifiers. For ToxCast, retain the chemical identity, assay and
endpoint annotation, concentration-response processing, and the fact that it is
in vitro. PK-Sim is subsequent: it can test declared translation assumptions,
but its simulated curve cannot replace either the observed PK record or the
assay evidence.

</details>
