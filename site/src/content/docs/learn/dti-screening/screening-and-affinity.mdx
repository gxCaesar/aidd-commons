## Foundations

Virtual screening, binding-affinity prediction, pose prediction, and
repurposing answer different questions. The decision unit must include the
target state, chemical input, assay condition, and intended action. Benchmarks
such as [DUD-E](https://dude.docking.org/) and
[LIT-PCBA](https://drugdesign.unistra.fr/LIT-PCBA/) make specific benchmark
design choices; they are not natural distributions.

## Practical workflow

Start with an explicit target and assay plan, then use a cheap docking or
similarity baseline before a learned pose or affinity system. Record decoy
construction, protein preparation, protonation, and the candidate budget.
[PoseBusters](https://github.com/maabuu/posebusters) can test pose plausibility,
not biological activity.

## Evaluation

Separate binder detection from ranking affinity among binders. Check target and
scaffold overlap, retrospective versus prospective evaluation, and whether a
pose is chemically reasonable. A generated pose or score must be followed by
experimental binding and functional feedback.

## Frontier and open questions

Systems such as [DiffDock](https://github.com/gcorso/DiffDock),
[Uni-Dock](https://github.com/dptech-corp/Uni-Dock), and
[NeuralPLexer](https://github.com/zrqiao/NeuralPLexer) expose distinct assets
and protocols; do not merge their reported numbers into one leaderboard.

## What v0.1 does not yet teach

v0.1 does not provide a prospective screening campaign or a claim of clinical
or biochemical efficacy.

for each asset-level evidence gap.
