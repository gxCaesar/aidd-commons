## Learning goals and prerequisites

You will distinguish a pose hypothesis from a binding claim, calculate simple
ranking quantities at declared prevalence, and assess whether a virtual-screening
or repurposing result transfers to a new target–assay decision. Read [Screening
and affinity](/learn/dti-screening/screening-and-affinity/) and [Regression and
classification errors](/learn/qsar-properties/regression-classification-errors/)
first. This is a browser/handworked reasoning lesson, not a runnable screening,
docking procedure, activity claim, or experimental protocol.

## Four outputs, four different objects

A **pose** is a hypothesis for a ligand's geometry and contacts in a declared
target state. A **binder classification** labels or ranks a compound relative to
an assay-defined binding outcome. **Affinity regression** targets a numerical
endpoint such as `pK = −log10(K in mol/L)` or a stated `pIC50`; these are not
interchangeable. **Virtual screening** prioritizes a finite candidate library for
a next action. Repurposing adds a source indication, target hypothesis,
formulation and exposure constraints, so it is not simply screening a familiar
compound catalogue.

Every output needs its decision unit. At minimum record target identity and
state, ligand representation and protonation assumptions when relevant, assay
endpoint and units, selection population, and follow-up action. A chemically
plausible pose does not prove binding. A binary label derived from a threshold
does not provide reliable ordering among binders. A strong rank correlation
within one assay may fail at the top of a different library. Confusing these
objects is a direct route to favourable but unusable comparisons.

## Binding modes are hypotheses, not structures in evidence clothing

A docking score or generative pose can be useful when it proposes contacts that
would distinguish a binding mode from alternatives. It is weak evidence when
target state, protonation, water, flexibility, or assay correspondence is
unresolved. A pose may be physically impossible even if a model score is high;
pose plausibility checks ask a different question from affinity or activity.
Conversely, a measured binder can have a wrong predicted pose. The right follow-up
is dictated by the decision: a pose-sensitive structure experiment, a defined
analogue contrast, or a binding assay may each test different links.

## Prevalence makes classification precision conditional

Let sensitivity `Se = P(positive call | active)`, specificity
`Sp = P(negative call | inactive)`, and prevalence `π = P(active)` in the
selection population. Then positive predictive value is

`PPV = Se·π / (Se·π + (1 − Sp)·(1 − π))`.

`PPV` is the expected fraction of true actives among positive calls under those
assumptions, not a portable model property. Enrichment at a fixed top fraction
also needs the library composition. If `N` compounds contain `A` actives and the
top `k` contains `a_k` actives, then `EF_k = (a_k/k)/(A/N)`. It is undefined
when `A = 0`; it is sensitive to the active/decoy construction and to `k`.

This is why decoys are not benign negatives. DUD-E-derived comparisons can carry
analogue and decoy bias, including results in which ligand-only information
explains apparently structural performance [Chen et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31430292/).
LIT-PCBA was built from processed dose-response assay data with confirmed active
and inactive compounds and explicit design choices; it is still a benchmark,
not a natural deployment population [Tran-Nguyen et
al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32282202/).

## Worked case: a constructed shortlist

All values below are **constructed for teaching**. A library has `N = 1,000`
compounds and `A = 20` assay-defined actives, hence `π = 0.020`. A model's top
`k = 50` contains `a_k = 8` actives. Its enrichment is
`EF_50 = (8/50)/(20/1000) = 0.16/0.02 = 8`. This says the top 50 have eightfold
the constructed library prevalence; it does not tell us a hit rate in a new
library, a binding mechanism, or an in-vivo effect.

Suppose a separate classifier is described by constructed `Se = 0.80`,
`Sp = 0.90`, and the same `π = 0.020`. Then
`PPV = (0.80×0.020)/((0.80×0.020)+(0.10×0.980)) = 0.016/0.114 = 0.140`, or
14.0%. Even apparently good sensitivity and specificity leave most positive
calls false under low prevalence. If the deployed library had `π = 0.10` with
the same artificial `Se` and `Sp`, `PPV = 0.08/(0.08+0.09) = 47.1%`. The change
comes from prevalence, not from model improvement.

For transfer, imagine the score was trained on inhibitor-labelled assay records
for target state T1 and is used to nominate agonist-like molecules for T2. The
output has crossed target, state, phenotype, assay, and prevalence boundaries.
The honest claim is a hypothesis for a new shortlist, with a documented library
and predeclared follow-up—not “repurposing validation.” A known drug's prior
human use does not establish target engagement or adequate exposure for a new
mechanism.

## Alternatives, limits, and independent exercise

PR curves retain precision dependence on prevalence, whereas ROC curves summarize
ranking across thresholds and can look stable while practical precision changes.
Report both only when the label definition and population are shared. Scaffold or
target-family splits test a more difficult representation shift, but cannot by
themselves make historical assay labels causal or make a retrospective benchmark
prospective. For pose work, report the target state and a physical plausibility
check separately from any enrichment number.

**Handworked exercise (not runnable or empirical):** A constructed library has
`N = 500`, `A = 10`; its top `k = 25` contains `a_k = 3`. Calculate `EF_25`.
For a classifier with `Se = 0.75`, `Sp = 0.95`, and `π = 0.020`, calculate PPV.
Name one missing field that prevents a pose score from becoming a binding claim,
and one requirement before calling a repurposing shortlist transferable.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

`EF_25 = (3/25)/(10/500) = 0.12/0.02 = 6`. The classifier has
`PPV = (0.75×0.020)/((0.75×0.020)+(0.05×0.980)) = 0.015/0.064 = 23.4%`.
A declared target state, ligand protonation assumption, assay endpoint, or
independent binding evidence is needed before a pose score becomes a binding
claim. Transfer requires at least a matched target–state–assay decision contract
and a predeclared follow-up in the new selection population; historical use of a
compound is not enough.

</details>

## Move the definitions and watch the benchmark change

Prevalence is one of several choices that decide what a binder dataset is. The
[binder definition lab](/labs/binder-definition/) holds twelve molecules fixed and
moves the rest: the positive count more than doubles across a grid of cutoffs,
four compounds are filed by an assay uncertainty they sit inside, two thirds of
the enlarged negative class was never measured, and six positives turn out to
occupy three scaffolds.

## Check a real transport claim

The [screening transport lab](/labs/screening-transport/) reads this repository's
own completed two-assay replay and expresses both contexts in recovered targets,
the only unit that makes them comparable. It shows that the two assays measure in
steps 4.92 times apart, that the whole five-instance baseline spread is under two
targets in both, and that the result which sounds most decisive is supported by
an eighth of a single target.

## Judge one pose three ways

The [EGFR structure evidence lab](/labs/egfr-structure-evidence/) places each of three
experimental ligands into each receptor state and then moves one crystal pose in two
declared ways. Both constructed poses pass a 2 A RMSD convention, one having lost the
hinge contact and the other clashing with 4 atoms, which is why a binding-mode
hypothesis needs more than one criterion.
