## Before you start

Read the [nomination introduction](/learn/candidate-nomination/capstone/) and
[assay controls lesson](/learn/drug-discovery-foundations/assay-controls-dose-response/).
This research-planning exercise teaches how to choose an observation that can
change a claim. It is not an executable lab, wet-lab procedure, candidate
nomination, or treatment recommendation. It needs no installation, data download,
or GPU. All compounds, measurements and decisions in the worked case are invented.

By the end, distinguish prediction, measurement, target engagement and mechanism;
write competing explanations for a result; and specify how positive, negative
and uninterpretable follow-up outcomes change the next decision.

## Start with the decision, not the model score

Suppose a model ranks compound B above A for a target-associated cellular endpoint.
The immediate question is whether either compound merits mechanistic follow-up
in a named cell context. It is not whether B is a drug candidate. A model score
can prioritize a measurement only within its endpoint and applicability limits;
it does not fill missing selectivity, exposure or safety fields.

For an empirical example of that limitation, read the existing
[cross-assay prediction failure case](/learn/evaluation-and-reproducibility/assay-shift-diagnosis/).
Its failed transfer does not identify a biological cause, but prevents treating
the primary-assay ranking as a generally reliable activity model. No new fit or
selection using those exposed evaluation labels is part of this lesson.

Write the proposed claim as a causal sentence: “B changes endpoint Y through
target T in context C.” This makes three links visible: B reaches and engages T;
changing T alters Y; and the measured signal represents Y in C. An attractive
structure prediction or an endpoint correlation does not establish all three.

## Give different observations different jobs

The [SGC chemical-tools guidance](https://www.thesgc.org/chemical-tools) distinguishes
target-directed chemical probes from broader chemical tool collections and
emphasizes selectivity and suitable controls. A named probe is therefore a
reagent with a defined evidence scope, not an automatic validation of every
phenotype observed after its use.

For our invented reporter-based case, consider three explanations that can coexist:

| Explanation                                                  | Observation that would help distinguish it                                                                  | What that observation still cannot establish                               |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Target-related biology changes Y                             | A suitably validated target-engagement readout plus a biologically relevant, detection-independent endpoint | Engagement alone does not show that T mediates the phenotype               |
| B changes the reporter or detection signal                   | A matched detection-interference control                                                                    | Interference does not rule out simultaneous biological activity            |
| The total signal falls because fewer viable cells contribute | An independently validated viable-cell measurement aligned to the endpoint's timing                         | Cell loss does not identify its cause or establish therapeutic selectivity |

An orthogonal assay asks about the intended biology through a different
measurement method. A counterscreen asks about a competing source of signal.
They are not interchangeable, and merely repeating the original assay is
neither. Even orthogonal agreement does not by itself establish the intended
mechanism. These distinctions follow the
[Assay Guidance Manual discussion of reporter interference](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK374281/).

Choose an orthogonal measurement whose own sensitivity, dynamic range and failure
modes have been characterized for the question. An insensitive follow-up cannot
turn a missing signal into evidence that the biology is absent. Likewise, a
control compound must be informative in the tested context, not just carry a
“negative control” label in a catalog.

## Worked case: is the larger signal decrease more informative?

Assume a teaching model in which total reporter signal `S = N × r`: `N` is the
number of viable contributing cells and `r` is mean reporter signal per such
cell. Assume linear detection, comparable background correction and matched
sampling times. Normalize both columns independently to their vehicle values.
These assumptions enable the arithmetic; they are not guaranteed in a real assay.

| Invented condition | Relative total reporter signal | Relative viable-cell count |
| ------------------ | -----------------------------: | -------------------------: |
| Vehicle            |                            100 |                        100 |
| A                  |                             72 |                         90 |
| B                  |                             44 |                         55 |

The raw signal decreases are 28% for A and 56% for B. Under the stated model,
the relative per-cell signal is `(S / 100) / (N / 100)`. It is `0.72 / 0.90 = 0.80`
for A and `0.44 / 0.55 = 0.80` for B. Thus the larger total decrease for B does
not show a larger per-cell effect. The same calculation would hold without any
target-specific mechanism at all.

This is a counterexample to an inference, not a recommended normalization
pipeline. Dividing by a noisy or treatment-dependent cell count can change the
estimand and introduce its own problems. Do not post-hoc repair a failed endpoint
with this ratio and report it as a prespecified result.

### A bounded next decision

The observed teaching pattern makes both a cell-number contribution and a
per-cell signal change relevant. A useful proposed follow-up would pair a
validated measurement of the intended endpoint using different detection with
a context-matched detection-interference control, while retaining the cell-count
information. Repeating only the model ranking would not distinguish the three
explanations. If the original measurement system has not been validated, establish
that first; this is not a universal order for every project.

Before obtaining new outcomes, agree what each branch means:

- If the independent endpoint is unchanged and the interference control reproduces
  the signal change, do not use the original reporter as evidence of target-mediated
  biology. Check that the independent measurement could have detected the proposed effect.
- If the endpoint changes without the tested interference and without a corresponding
  loss of viable cells, that supports a biological response. Target engagement and
  a separate intervention that tests target dependence are still needed for the causal claim.
- If controls fail or measurements disagree outside their characterized uncertainty,
  retain the outcome as unresolved. It is neither a confirmed hit nor proof of inactivity.

These are proposed interpretations, not observations from an experiment. Effect
thresholds, precision, sample size, allocation and exclusion rules require the
actual assay's validation data and a project-specific protocol. Do not manufacture
them from the teaching table. Technical wells can characterize measurement
variation, but cannot substitute for independent biological preparations.

## Independent exercise

In a second invented table, A has reporter signal 60 and viable-cell count 75;
B has 72 and 90; C has 72 and 100. Vehicle remains 100 and 100. Under the same
teaching assumptions, calculate all three relative per-cell signals. Can you
nominate C? Write one next observation that could weaken its target-mediated
interpretation, and distinguish “stop this interpretation” from “discard the compound.”

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer and decision limits</summary>

A and B both give 0.80; C gives 0.72. C has the larger per-cell decrease in
this constructed example, but there is still no evidence that the decrease
represents the intended biology, involves T, is selective, or has useful exposure.
The arithmetic therefore does not nominate C.

For example, a valid interference control that reproduces C's signal change,
together with an adequately sensitive orthogonal endpoint that does not change,
would weaken the reporter-based target claim. It would not prove that C has no
biological activity. Stop using that signal as mechanistic evidence; whether
to characterize C further is a separate decision about the project's question
and resources. Conversely, an independent endpoint response is not enough to
declare T causal. State what engagement and target-dependence evidence is still missing.

</details>

## Inspect a real dossier's counterevidence

The argument above is easier to accept than to practise, because the pressure is
always to report the comparison that worked. This repository's own completed labs
kept both. The
[counterevidence dossier lab](/labs/counterevidence-dossier/) inventories six
kinds of preserved counterevidence across those artifacts with one offline
command, and recomputes the case where the model with the lowest error on one
evaluation is several times the baseline error on the other.

## A research meeting deliverable

Prepare a one-page decision memo, not a composite “candidate quality” score.
State the candidate, endpoint and context; separate measured quantities from
predictions; name the leading alternative explanation; describe the discriminating
measurement and its control; and predeclare the outcome that would change course.
Keep unknown selectivity, exposure and safety visible rather than assigning zeros
or favorable guesses. See the [PK/PD lesson](/learn/admet-pk/exposure-clearance-pkpd/)
for why a concentration or potency number cannot replace an exposure argument.

For a reader exercise, a complete answer connects each proposed observation to a
decision and explains one remaining ambiguity. A list of assay names without
those links is incomplete. This evaluates the written reasoning only; no learner
outcomes or experimental validation have been collected here.
