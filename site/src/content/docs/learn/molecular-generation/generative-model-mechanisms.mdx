## Learning goals

After this lesson, you should be able to distinguish autoregressive,
denoising-diffusion, latent-VAE, and flow-matching mechanisms at the level of
their target and inference procedure. You should also be able to keep candidate
validity, novelty, synthesizability, and a scored objective separate.

## Prerequisites and task boundary

Read [Generation and search](/learn/molecular-generation/generation-and-search/)
first. Let `x` be a molecular object represented as tokens, a graph, or atomic
coordinates, and let `c` be a declared condition such as a target-site
representation or a property constraint. A generator samples a candidate from
`p(x | c)`; it does not by itself establish activity, synthesis, or a useful
experimental outcome.

## Four mechanisms, four inference paths

An autoregressive model factorizes a sequence into conditional next-token
decisions:
`p(x | c) = product_i p(x_i | x_<i, c)`. Inference chooses or samples one token
after another. During training, a standard objective is the negative
log-likelihood `L_AR = -sum_i log p_theta(x_i | x_<i, c)`. The factorization,
tokenization, and conditioning all belong to the model contract.

A denoising diffusion model defines noisy versions of a clean object and learns
to remove noise conditional on time and, when used, `c`. One common teaching
form is `x_t = sqrt(alpha_bar_t) x_0 + sqrt(1-alpha_bar_t) epsilon`, with a
noise-prediction loss `E ||epsilon - epsilon_theta(x_t, t, c)||^2`. Inference
begins from noise and applies a reverse denoising procedure. The original
[DDPM paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239) describes the connection between
diffusion probabilistic models and denoising score matching; it is a mechanism
source, not evidence that every molecular diffusion implementation uses the
same representation or sampler.

A latent variational autoencoder (VAE) introduces a latent variable `z`. For a
fully conditional VAE, the encoder can approximate `q_phi(z | x, c)`, the prior
can be `p(z | c)`, and the decoder models `p_theta(x | z, c)`. Its conditional
evidence lower bound balances reconstruction with
`KL(q_phi(z | x, c) || p(z | c))`. An unconditional encoder `q_phi(z | x)` is
also possible, but the chosen encoder, prior, and decoder conditions must be
declared rather than mixed implicitly. [Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6114)
is the original source for the reparameterized variational-learning mechanism.
At generation time, sampling `z` and decoding it is not the same operation as
iterative denoising in object space.

Flow matching trains a continuous vector field. In the narrow sense used here,
it learns a velocity `v_theta(x_t, t, c)` along a chosen probability path, then
an ODE solver integrates `dx/dt = v_theta(x_t, t, c)` from a base distribution
to data. The original [Flow Matching paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02747)
frames this as regressing vector fields of fixed conditional probability paths.
Velocity learning alone says neither that a generated molecule is valid nor that
the path is a physical reaction trajectory.

## Worked likelihood: a candidate is more than its token probability

For a **constructed conditional-token example**, suppose a model assigns
`p(A | c) = 0.80`, `p(B | A, c) = 0.25`, and
`p(stop | AB, c) = 0.50`. Its probability for the token sequence `AB` followed
by stop is `0.80 x 0.25 x 0.50 = 0.10`. This arithmetic says only how likely
that sequence is under this factorization and condition.

It does not answer four separate downstream questions:

| Question | Example evidence needed |
| --- | --- |
| Validity | A parser, valence check, or valid coordinate/graph construction |
| Novelty | A declared reference set and similarity or identity rule |
| Synthesizability | A route, reagents, conditions, and an explicit feasibility criterion |
| Objective proxy | A named model score with its target, unit, and calibration limits |

A high token likelihood does not imply any one of these. Conversely, a novel
or synthetically plausible candidate need not optimize the proxy score.

## Conditional generation and examples at the right scope

The condition `c` must be visible in both training and inference claims. For
example, a pocket, residue constraint, desired property, or atomic motif can be
provided as a condition, but no universal pretrained capability to use every
such `c` follows. The resulting sample remains conditional on how that item was
encoded, which conditions appeared in training, and the model's declared
inference interface.

[RFdiffusion2](https://github.com/RosettaCommons/RFdiffusion2) is a useful
official example of protein atomic active-site scaffolding. Its repository
states that the current release supports inference only and describes protein
design demonstrations. It is not evidence of small-molecule discovery, a
general molecular-generation benchmark result, or complete training
availability.
Its 41 M-CSA enzyme benchmark does not identify the training corpus, and the [project documentation](https://rosettacommons.github.io/RFdiffusion2/) recommends the original RFdiffusion for binder design.

## Independent exercise

Choose a condition `c` and a molecule representation `x`. In two sentences,
state how an autoregressive sampler and a diffusion sampler would begin
inference. Then list one test for each of validity and objective-proxy quality.
Do not use “the model generated it” as either test.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

For example, let `c` be a target-site representation and `x` a molecular graph.
An autoregressive sampler begins with a start token or partial graph and samples
the next component conditioned on the prefix and `c`. A diffusion sampler begins
with noisy graph or coordinate variables and repeatedly denoises them using
time and `c`. Validity might be assessed by a graph/valence rule. Objective-proxy
quality requires a separately named scorer and a check that its output has the
claimed unit, target, and calibration scope. Neither test establishes synthesis
or biological activity.

</details>

## Failure boundaries and source path

Generation changes a proposal distribution; it does not remove uncertainty in
the target state, the condition, a scoring proxy, or a later experiment. Record
the representation, conditioning information, sampling rule, validity filter,
reference set for novelty, and decision endpoint before comparing candidate
lists.
