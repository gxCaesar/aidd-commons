## Learning goals

After this lesson, you should be able to:

1. name the fields an archived assay record carries that a single potency number hides;
2. distinguish an equality from a bound, and state what each of them licenses;
3. read a control regime and a normalisation equation as the two things that fix a scale;
4. read a recorded replicate count together with the archived declaration that gives the count its meaning; and
5. decide which conclusions an assay's own control record supports and which it does not.

## Prerequisites and scope

Review [Assay controls and dose response](/learn/drug-discovery-foundations/assay-controls-dose-response/)
first. That lesson explains the mechanisms this one applies. It names what a
blank, a vehicle, a negative control and a positive control each do, it writes
down how percent inhibition and percent stimulation are computed from them, and
it shows how to read a concentration-response curve without mistaking it for
mechanism or efficacy.

This lesson does not repeat that. It opens six real, archived PubChem BioAssay
dose-response assays and reads their own records. The readout mix over endpoint
labels, the relation mix over the archived qualifiers, the confidence-score
distribution, the replicate structure each archived protocol declares, the
control regime and reference wells each protocol names, and the normalisation
equation each protocol writes down. The teaching point is that an assay
licenses a conclusion only to the extent its own control record supports it.

The companion route is the [archived assay lab](/labs/assay-endpoint-controls/),
which reads the same six assays out of a byte-checked cache and prints the same
numbers. Nothing here is fitted, predicted or scored, and no reader outcome has
been measured for this lesson.

## The cheap baseline

The cheap baseline comes before any comparison, and here it is the whole first
half of the reading. Nothing is fitted and no curve is looked at yet.

The readout mix counts every admitted endpoint value.

| Endpoint label | Admitted endpoint values | Fraction of 1529 |
| -------------- | -----------------------: | ---------------: |
| AC50           |                      706 |        0.46174   |
| IC50           |                      429 |        0.280576  |
| Ki             |                      272 |        0.177894  |
| EC50           |                       69 |        0.045128  |
| MIC            |                       53 |        0.034663  |
| Total          |                    1529 |                  |

The relation mix counts one qualifier per admitted row.

| Relation qualifier | Admitted rows | Fraction of 1257 |
| ------------------ | -----------: | ---------------: |
| An equality        |          832 |        0.661893  |
| A greater-than bound |        406 |        0.322991  |
| A less-than bound  |            4 |        0.003182  |
| No qualifier stated |          15 |        0.011933  |
| Total              |         1257 |                  |

The two mixes do not share a denominator, and the difference is instructive.
The readout mix reaches 1529 because assay 519 records two endpoint columns,
IC50 and Ki, on each of its 272 admitted rows. The relation mix reaches 1257
because one qualifier attaches to a row rather than to an endpoint value. The
two totals therefore differ by exactly the admitted rows of assay 519.

The confidence score reaches its maximum of 100 for 0.002386634844868735 of the
admitted rows and sits at zero for 0.3206046141607001 of them, with a median of
23 and a minimum of 0. The score is a field of the archived record, so it
is read here as a property of that record rather than a property of the
underlying experiment.

None of these numbers is a potency. They describe the shape of the archive,
and they are the cheapest evidence in the reading because they need no
assumption about any compound.

## Equality and bound

An equality is a measurement. A bound is a censored value that states a side of
the number rather than the number. Among the 1257 admitted rows, 832 carry an
equality, 406 a greater-than bound, 4 a less-than bound and 15 carry no
qualifier at all. Almost a third of the recorded relations are therefore
bounds, and a bound states a side and not a magnitude.

Two compounds from the same assay show what the qualifier changes.

Compound 1044226 was tested over concentrations from 0.0023 to 45.0
micromolar. Its lowest archived response is -1.82 percent and its highest is
-0.07 percent, so no tested concentration produced a positive archived
response. The archived endpoint is EC50 45.0 micromolar with a greater-than
qualifier, which states that the half-maximal concentration is above 45.0
micromolar and not that it is 45.0 micromolar.

Compound 1160933 was tested over the same concentration series. Its lowest
archived response is -0.67 percent and its highest is 60.18 percent. The
archived endpoint is EC50 30.8 micromolar with an equality qualifier, which is
a measurement.

Both rows come from the same assay, on the same scale, under the same control
record. The only difference is the qualifier, and the qualifier is the
difference between a number and a side. Writing "EC50 45.0 micromolar" for the
first compound converts a censored value into a measurement that the archive
never made.

## The replicate structure

The archive records a per-row count, and the protocol says what the count
means. Reading the count without that declaration is reading an integer
without a unit.

| Assay | Recorded column | What the count is | Archived declaration |
| ----- | --------------- | ----------------- | -------------------- |
| 439 | Number of Points | Data points per curve | Each compound dilution series was assayed in triplicate |
| 464 | N DataPoints | Data points per curve | 750 of 765 compounds were serially diluted ten times at three fold-dilutions |
| 519 | N_POINTS | Data points per curve | Each compound was tested in duplicate to result in 18 data points |
| 548 | Number of Datapoints | Data points per curve | Each compound dilution was assayed in triplicate |
| 691 | Number of MIC determinations | Independent determinations | Compounds that gave a measurable MIC were retested a second time |
| 1277 | Number of DataPoints | Data points per curve | Test compounds were assayed in triplicate in a 10-point, 1:3 dilution series |

Five of the six assays record the number of points on a curve, and the sixth
records the number of independent determinations, which is a different kind of
replicate. Assay 691 records 1 determination for 41 rows and 2 determinations
for 12 rows, matching the declaration that a compound with a measurable MIC was
retested a second time.

The declaration and the recorded count are two separate fields, and they do not
always agree on their face. Assay 464's protocol declares ten serial dilutions
at three-fold steps, while the archive records 30 data points for 679 of its
706 admitted rows and between 26 and 29 for the rest. Assay 519's protocol
declares 18 data points per compound, while the archive records 18 for 121 of
its 272 rows, 36 for 84 and 90 for 17. A reader who reports the declaration and
a reader who reports the count have reported two different facts, and both
have to be read.

## The control record

The control regime is the part of the record that decides what the numbers
mean, because it is the part that sets the scale.

| Assay | Control regime | Reference wells named | Keywords found in the archived protocol |
| ----- | -------------- | --------------------: | -------------------------------------- |
| 439 | Single positive reference |            1 | control, positive control |
| 464 | Single positive reference |            1 | control, positive control |
| 519 | Positive and negative reference |            2 | control, positive control, negative control |
| 548 | Positive and negative reference |            2 | control, positive control, negative control |
| 691 | None declared |            0 | none |
| 1277 | Positive and negative reference |            2 | control, high_control, low_control |

Assay 691 deserves a note. The word "blank" does occur in its archived
protocol, and a naive keyword search would read that as a declared control. The
archived protocol uses the word for an empty table cell rather than for a
control well, so the lab reports it as ambiguous and does not use it to decide
whether a control is declared. The audit finds no control keyword and no
reference keyword for that assay.

Three regimes appear across the six assays. Two assays name one reference and
no baseline, so the absolute percent has no stated origin and the top of the
scale is the only end that is anchored. Three name both a positive and a
negative reference, so both ends of the scale are named. One names nothing, so
no end of its scale is anchored at all, and no ranking by potency follows from
it.

## The normalisation equation

Where a protocol writes the equation down, the equation can be run at its own
reference wells and checked against the archived values. The lab requires the
two values it produces to be 100 units apart and the lower one to sit at zero
before the equation counts as a zero-to-hundred response scale. The archived
per-concentration values must then fall inside the range the equation spans,
widened by a band of 25.0 points, for at least a declared fraction of 0.95.

The archive's own words are these.

- Assay 439. "All data reported was normalized on a per-plate basis to wells that contained cells in the presence of 1 micromolar S1P (i.e. 100% activation)."
- Assay 464. "Percent inhibition was calculated from the median of the positive control doxorubicin at 4 micromolar final screening concentration."
- Assay 519. "%Inhibition = 100 x [1 - (MFI_test - MFI_PC)/(MFI_NC - MFI_PC)], where MFI_PC is the average median fluorescence intensity of positive control wells (unlabeled receptor-blocking peptide) and MFI_NC is the average median fluorescence intensity of negative control wells (buffer)."
- Assay 548. "%inhibition = (test_compound - median_positive_control)/(median_positive_control - median_negative_control)*100, where positive control is Staurosporine and negative control is DMSO only."
- Assay 1277. "Ratio = (T45 / T0); %Inhibition = [1-((Ratio Test_Compound - Median_Ratio_High_Control)/(Median_Ratio_Low_Control - Median_Ratio_High_Control))]*100, where Low_Control is wells containing DMSO, NPY and isoproterenol and High_Control is wells containing DMSO and isoproterenol."
- Assay 691. The archived protocol names no control and writes no equation.

Four of these five equations reproduce the scale their archived values are on.
The equation for assay 519 puts the positive reference at 100 and the negative
reference at 0, and every one of its archived values falls inside the range it
describes. The equation for assay 1277 does the same, with the fraction inside
the range at 0.9841269841269841.

The equation for assay 548 does not. It puts the positive reference at 0 and
the negative reference at -100, and only 0.7620064034151548 of its archived
values fall inside the range it describes, against 1.0 for assay 519. Its
archived values run from -5.5 to 111.3 percent, with 5 above 100 and 33 below 0.
The protocol names both references and writes an equation, and the equation
still does not describe the scale the archive's own values are on. The
endpoint and the equation then have different standing, and the lab reports
them separately.

One more property of the curve check is worth reading. It skips exactly the
rows whose relation is a bound. In assay 1277 it refits 28 curves from the
archived points, which is the number of equality rows, and skips 35 rows, which
is the 4 less-than bounds plus the 31 greater-than bounds. A censored value has
no curve to recompute, because the archive does not say where the half-maximal
point sits.

## Worked case

A model table is being assembled from the six declared assays. Three rows are
candidates.

| Row | Assay | Compound | Archived endpoint | Qualifier |
| --- | ----- | -------- | ---------------- | --------- |
| 1 | 548 | 1234059 | IC50 11.1 micromolar | An equality |
| 2 | 1277 | 1099239 | IC50 35.0 micromolar | A greater-than bound |
| 3 | 691 | 135415497 | MIC 100.0 micromolar | A greater-than bound |

Row 1 may be written as IC50 11.1 micromolar. The number is a measurement, and
it may be compared with another compound measured in assay 548, because that
protocol names both a positive control and a negative control and writes the
normalisation down. What may not be done is to describe the scale with the
archived equation, because that equation does not reproduce the scale the
archived values are on. The endpoint is licensed and the equation is not.

Row 2 may be written as IC50 greater than 35.0 micromolar. The archive states
that the half-maximal concentration is above 35.0 micromolar and does not state
how far above. Writing IC50 35.0 micromolar turns a censored value into a
measurement. The row may be compared with another compound measured in assay
1277, because that protocol names its two reference wells, high_control and
low_control, and writes the equation down.

Row 3 may be written as MIC greater than 100.0 micromolar, and nothing more.
The ends of that assay's response scale are set by controls that the archived
protocol never names, so no statement about the response being 0 percent or 100
percent follows, and no ranking of compounds by potency follows. Its readout is
categorical, so it has no concentration-response curve at all.

The three rows cannot share one potency column. Two assays with two different
control records do not share a scale, and an assay with no control record has no
scale to share. Row 1 may be compared with another row from assay 548, row 2
with another row from assay 1277, and row 3 with nothing outside itself. The
qualifier travels with the number in every case, because the number means
different things without it.

## What this does not show

Six assays are not a sample of the archive, so nothing here estimates how
relation qualifiers, confidence scores or control regimes are distributed across
PubChem BioAssay as a whole. The admitted values are what the declared
admission rule kept, and a reader who changes the rule gets a different cohort.
A missing control record is reported as a fact about the archive and not as
evidence that the experiment was performed without controls, because the archive
records what it records.

No potency comparison is made between two different assays, and no statement is
made about any in vivo, clinical or pharmacokinetic consequence. Every declared
assay here is an in vitro or cell-based measurement, and every one of them is
an archived record rather than a measurement this repository performed. The raw
instrument signal is not in the archive either, because the archived CSV carries
the response after the depositor's own normalisation, so the normalisation
equations are exercised on the reference wells rather than on a real plate.

The curve check compares a recomputed curve against the endpoint values the
archive itself recorded, under a fixed tolerance of 5.0 points. It is a check
on the archive's internal consistency and on the archive's own normalisation
equation. It is not an assessment of the underlying experiment, and it cannot
detect an error that the archive made consistently in both the readings and the
recorded endpoints.

## Independent exercise

Answer these three questions from the numbers the reading produces.

1. How many of the admitted rows carry an equality and how many carry a bound, and what does dropping the qualifier do to a greater-than bound and to a less-than bound?
2. Three of the six assays declare both a positive and a negative reference. Name them, name the one whose archived equation does not reproduce the scale its archived values are on, and give the number that shows it.
3. For the assay whose archived protocol names no control, blank or vehicle, write one sentence that its own record licenses and one sentence that it does not.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

<p>The 1257 admitted rows each carry one relation qualifier. 832 of them are
equalities, 406 are greater-than bounds, 4 are less-than bounds and 15 carry no
qualifier at all, so the bounds are almost a third of the admitted rows. A
greater-than bound says the true value is larger than the number written, so
dropping the qualifier reports the boundary
as if it were the measurement and makes the compound look more potent than the
archive states. A less-than bound says the true value is smaller, so dropping
the qualifier makes the compound look less potent than the archive states. A
ranking built on those numbers inherits both errors.</p>

<p>Assays 519, 548 and 1277 declare a positive and a negative reference. Assays
439 and 464 declare a single positive reference and assay 691 declares none.
Assay 548 is the one whose archived equation does not reproduce the archived
scale. Its equation puts the positive reference at 0 and the negative reference
at -100, and only 0.7620064034151548 of its archived values fall inside the
range that equation describes, against 1.0 for assay 519 and 0.9841269841269841
for assay 1277.</p>

<p>Assay 691 licenses a statement about one compound at the stated
concentrations, on the terms the archive itself uses, including the boundary
of its reported threshold. The archive records compound 135415497 as INACTIVE
and reports its MIC as greater than 100.0 micromolar. It does not license a
ranking of compounds by potency, a statement that the response was 0 percent
or 100 percent at any concentration, or a comparison of its thresholds with an
assay whose scale is defined by named controls.</p>
