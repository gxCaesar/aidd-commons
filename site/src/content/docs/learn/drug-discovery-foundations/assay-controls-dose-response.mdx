## Learning goals

Distinguish biochemical or target-binding, cellular-phenotype, and reporter
readouts; name each control in a normalization; and read a concentration-response
curve. Explain why a tidy curve alone is not evidence of target engagement,
mechanism, organismal efficacy, or clinical benefit.

## Prerequisite and decision boundary

Read [Drug discovery context](/learn/drug-discovery-foundations/discovery-context/)
first. An assay maps a named biological object to a signal and a decision. Before
comparison, declare the system, readout, controls, normalization direction,
endpoint, and an alternative explanation not excluded. This is a reading and
hand-calculation lesson, not an operating protocol.

## Which question reaches the readout?

| Assay kind                    | Object and readout                                         | A conclusion it can support                     | What it does not establish alone                                            |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Biochemical or target-binding | Defined molecular component; binding or enzymatic product  | Target-proximal response under named conditions | Cell entry, metabolism, phenotype, selectivity, or efficacy                 |
| Cellular phenotype            | Intact cells and a named marker, morphology, or cell count | Response in that cell context                   | Causal molecular target or transfer to another context                      |
| Reporter                      | Reporter enzyme, fluorescent protein, or similar construct | Change in reporter signal under stated logic    | That pathway, target, or phenotype changed rather than reporter or detector |

A reporter can be cell-based but remains a measurement layer: a compound can
affect biology, reporter, or optics. Do not upgrade a target-binding measurement
to a cellular claim, or a reporter change to target mechanism, merely because
all quantities are called “activity.”

## Controls set both the baseline and the sign

Controls label expected assay states. A **blank** `B` estimates instrument or
reagent background without the relevant biological signal. A **vehicle** `V`
contains carrier without test compound and sets the baseline. A **negative
control** `N` is expected not to produce the effect; it coincides with `V` only
when declared. A **positive control** `P` is a reference that produces the
defined effect. In inhibition, `P` can have lower raw signal than `V`: “positive”
means the intended effect, not a high number.

When high raw signal means residual activity, background-correct as `R_b = R - B`,
`V_b = V - B`, and `P_b = P - B`. With `V` at 0% inhibition and `P` at 100%,
use `% inhibition = 100 * (V_b - R_b) / (V_b - P_b)`: it gives 0% when `R = V`
and 100% when `R = P`. For stimulation, use ` % stimulation = 100 *
(R_b - V_b) / (P_b - V_b)`. Declare direction and controls before fitting.
The 100% label is an assay scaling reference, not proof of complete biological
inhibition. If `V_b = P_b`, the denominator is zero and normalization is undefined; a small control window
amplifies noise.
[Data Standardization for Results Management](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK91993/)
discusses directional normalized endpoints and three- or four-parameter fits.

### Technical wells and independent repeats answer different questions

Technical wells repeat one sample or preparation within a run; they quantify
within-run variation but are dependent observations. Independent repeats use
distinct preparations at the declared source of variation, such as cell
populations, target lots, or sample batches. A same-source run on another day
may be inter-run instead. Report source, plate, run, and aggregation rule, not
only “n = 6”; six technical wells are not six independent preparations.
[Basic Guidelines for Reporting Non-Clinical Data](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK550206/)
distinguishes technical, independent, and inter-run repeat labels.

## Concentration-response curves locate a response within an assay

For a rising response, use horizontal `log10(x in mol/L)`, where `x` is
concentration, and vertical normalized response `y`, for example % stimulation:

`y(x) = Bottom + (Top - Bottom) / (1 + (EC50 / x)^Hill)`.

For these stated rising and falling signs, take `x > 0`, `Hill > 0`, and `Top > Bottom`.

`Bottom` and `Top` are the fitted lower and upper response asymptotes. `Hill` is
dimensionless steepness; `EC50` is `x` at `(Top + Bottom) / 2`. To plot an inhibition
experiment as a falling curve, define `y = % residual activity = 100 - % inhibition`,
then use `y(x) = Bottom + (Top - Bottom) / (1 + (x / IC50)^Hill)`. As `x`
increases, this `y` falls while `% inhibition` rises. At `IC50`, `y` is the
fitted midpoint, and `% inhibition` is `100 - y`; it is 50% only when that
midpoint is 50%. This is a relative `IC50` (half the fitted range), not
necessarily an absolute 50%-of-control inhibition when fitted asymptotes differ
from the control-defined 0% and 100% endpoints. The constructed examples below
use symmetric endpoints and therefore hide that distinction. Do not silently fix
fitted asymptotes or `Hill`.

`EC50` and `IC50` are curve locations under these conditions, not `Kd`, `Ki`,
or efficacy. `Top - Bottom` is the fitted assay range, not organismal or
clinical efficacy. `Hill` describes shape, not binding stoichiometry or a single
mechanism.

## Worked case: normalize before interpreting

This entire case is **constructed for teaching**; it reports no assay, compound,
target, or dose. Invented raw signals `B = 10`, `V = 110`, `P = 30`, and
`R = 70` give `R_b = 60`, `V_b = 100`, `P_b = 20`, and
`% inhibition = 100 * (100 - 60) / (100 - 20) = 50%`: the test well is midway
between these declared controls.

An invented falling fit uses `y` as residual activity with `Top = 98%`,
`Bottom = 2%`, `IC50 = 0.40 µM`, and `Hill = 1.2`. At `0.40 µM`,
`y = (98 + 2) / 2 = 50%` residual activity and inhibition is 50%. At the
invented nonmidpoint `x = 0.80 µM`,
`y = 2 + (98 - 2) / (1 + (0.80 / 0.40)^1.2) = 31.1%` residual activity, so
`% inhibition = 68.9%`. Increasing `x` lowered residual activity and raised
inhibition. The range is `98 - 2 = 96` percentage points. These invented values
are neither `Ki` nor `Kd`, and do not demonstrate efficacy.

## Interference, counterscreens, and orthogonal evidence

An apparent signal can reflect biology, reporter-enzyme inhibition, optical
effects, aggregation, or another measurement interaction. A counterscreen tests
a named alternative such as detector or reporter interference; a negative result
does not support that alternative, but does not prove biology. An orthogonal
assay retains relevant biology while changing detection. Concordance supports
detection independence, not a molecular-mechanism claim by itself.
[Interferences with Luciferase Reporter Enzymes](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK374281/)
explains why counterscreens deselect interference while orthogonal results do
not alone prove mechanism.

## Independent exercise

On an unseen **constructed** inhibition plate, `B = 8`, `V = 108`, `P = 28`,
and `R = 68`; a falling curve has `Top = 96%`, `Bottom = 4%`, `IC50 = 0.80 µM`,
and `Hill = 0.9`, with `y` defined as residual activity. Calculate normalized
inhibition, then `y` and `% inhibition` at `IC50` and at `x = 1.60 µM`. State
which quantity rises as `x` rises, name one thing `IC50` cannot establish, then
interpret a hit active in a direct reporter counterscreen. What would an
orthogonal assay add?

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

`R_b = 60`, `V_b = 100`, and `P_b = 20`, so `% inhibition = 100 *
(100 - 60) / (100 - 20) = 50%`. At `IC50`, `y = (96 + 4) / 2 = 50%` residual
activity and inhibition is 50%. At `1.60 µM`,
`y = 4 + (96 - 4) / (1 + (1.60 / 0.80)^0.9) = 36.1%` residual activity, so
inhibition is 63.9%. Thus increasing `x` lowers `y` and raises inhibition.
`IC50` does not establish `Kd`, `Ki`, target engagement, or efficacy. Activity
in the direct reporter counterscreen supports reporter/detection interference,
not a target-mechanism claim, but does not prove absent biology. An orthogonal
assay tests whether the signal persists when detection changes.

</details>
